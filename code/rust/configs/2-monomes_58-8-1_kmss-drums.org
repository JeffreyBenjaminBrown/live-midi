#+title: Two monomes (58-8-1) + KMSS drums -- reference
#+date: 2026-07-01

The =2-monomes_58-8-1_kmss-drums= config drives *three surfaces at once*: two
16x16 monome grids as independent 58-EDO play surfaces, and the Keith McMillen
SoftStep (KMSS) pedalboard as a velocity-sensitive drumkit. It is the first config
that *composes* what used to be three mutually-exclusive runtimes (the sawwave EDO
grid, the looper's scroll pad, the drumkit) into one -- see the "surfaces runtime"
in =code/rust/midi_pulse/surfaces_runtime/=.

Background + the runtime-unification rationale: =TODO/2-monomes_58-8-1_kmss-drums.org=
(the plan) and its =progress.org=.

* glossary

- *surface* -- one of the three playable devices this config brings up at once: grid
  A, grid B, or the KMSS.
- *play grid* -- a 16x16 monome, the whole grid a 58-EDO note surface (=x_step 8=,
  =y_step 1=, =fundamental_hz 80=). Each grid has its *own* voices and its *own*
  scroll register.
- *scroll pad* -- a 3x2 pad in a grid's bottom-right (=[13,14,15,15]=) that moves
  *that grid's* pitch window: the four arrows shift by one cell, the two top corners
  by an octave. Reuses the looper's =edo_shift_pad=.
- *waveform strip / selector* -- a 4-cell radio on a grid's top row
  (=[0,0]..[3,0]=): sine / triangle / square / saw, left to right. Each grid's strip
  sets its *own* timbre (self-control, since 2026-07; the =controls= field can still
  point strips across grids, but this rig no longer does). No AM/FM.
- *volume strip / fader* -- the 12 cells right of the waveform strip (=[4,0]..[15,0]=): a
  log fader over 30 dB, top cell = unity, starting at column 10. Like the waveform strip it
  sets its *own* grid's loudness, and it is *live* -- moving it rescales
  that grid's already-sounding voices, not just future notes.
- *trail* -- up to ~trails_max~ recently played *pitch classes* (octave-independent; 9
  here), shared across both grids, shown dim on both as a backdrop; a currently-sounding
  class shows bright instead. Repeating a note refreshes its single entry (so hammering one
  note never floods or erases the trail), and playing a note clears any trailed neighbour
  within ~1/trail_clobber_radius~ of an octave (1/27 here, ~44 cents), so close pitches
  never crowd the backdrop. Both knobs live in the ~[surfaces]~ table. Lets you see where
  to finger on one grid to harmonize with the other.
- *independent voices* -- the same pitch fingered on both grids is *two* separate
  voices; releasing one never cuts the other. Even two cells on one grid that collide
  to the same pitch are independent (one voice per finger). Voices are keyed by
  =(grid, cell)=, not by pitch (contrast the looper, which ref-counts by pitch).
- *distortion toggle* -- a single cell at =(0,1)= (second row, far left) on each
  grid: key-down flips a *global* soft-clip distortion on the summed synth mix (both
  grids; drums are untouched). One shared switch -- either grid's button, one state.
  The curve's =distortion_scale= / =distortion_shape= live on the synth sink; see
  [[../../../learnings/distortion.org]].
- *accrete buttons / sustained set* -- three single-cell buttons at the bottom-left
  of each grid (=(0,15)= clear, =(1,15)= needs-holding, =(2,15)= accrete). Notes added
  to the *sustained set* keep ringing after their fingers lift, until *clear*. Both
  grids share ONE accrete state, so either grid's buttons act globally. See *sustain
  (accrete)* below.
- *drumkit* -- the KMSS as nine TR-808 one-shots, velocity-sensitive (tether mode).
  Reused verbatim from =kmss-drumkit= (see [[./kmss-drumkit.org]]).

* usage

Launch under =pw-jack= so the synth *and* the drum sampler share the sound card via
PipeWire (two JACK nodes in one process):

#+begin_src sh
pw-jack cargo run --bin midi_pulse -- 2-monomes_58-8-1_kmss-drums
#+end_src

Needs *both* 16x16 grids connected (the runtime hands each config monome a distinct
live grid in config order; it does not matter which physical grid is which) and the
KMSS. On the host, connect audio with =./code/rust/connect-midi.sh= if needed. The
drum WAVs resolve under the top-level =drum-samples/= folder (same as
=kmss-drumkit=); that folder must be present in this worktree.

=Ctrl-C= blanks *both* grids, stops the synth, and restores the KMSS to standalone
mode.

A headless device-layer smoke (two virtual grids, no sound, no KMSS) runs as a unit
test (=surfaces_runtime::tests::two_grids_run_against_mock_grids=) against the
=2-monomes_58-8-1_kmss-drums-mock= rig config; see [[../../../MOCK-MONOME.org]].

* per-grid layout

The two grids are identical. The whole 16x16 is the play surface, with two
overlays drawn on top:

#+begin_example
  col: 0   1   2   3   4   5  ...        13  14  15
  row 0  [SIN TRI SQR SAW][ volume fader: columns 4..15 ]   <- top row controls THIS
                                                               grid: waveform (0..3) + volume
  row 1  DIST .   .   .   .                    .   .   .   <- global distortion on/off
   ...                     (58-EDO play cells)
  row 13  .   .   .   .   .                    .   .   .
  row 14  .   .   .   .   .  . . . . . . .  8vaDN UP  8vaUP  <- scroll pad
  row 15 CLR HOLD ACC  .  .  . . . . . . .  LEFT DOWN RIGHT  <- accrete + scroll pad
#+end_example

- The whole top row (0,0)..(15,0), the pad cells (13..15, 14..15), the accrete
  buttons (0..2, 15), and the distortion toggle (0,1) *occlude* the play grid:
  pressing them re-timbres / sets volume / scrolls / sustains / distorts rather than
  sounding a note.
- The scroll-pad corners follow the looper's convention: top-left = down an octave
  (notes sound lower), top-right = up an octave; arrows raise pitch down/right,
  lower it up/left. See =midi_pulse::edo_play=.

* LEDs

The play-grid LEDs are *register-aware* and *shared across both grids* (the visuals pass
-- see =TODO/3-surfaces-visuals/=):

- *Held notes (bright).* A held note lights all its octave-equivalents solid, computed
  through the *current* scroll register -- so shifting the window *moves* the lit cells to
  where that pitch now lives (the sounding pitch itself stays put; only the lights move).
- *Cross-grid reflection.* A note held on *either* grid lights on *both* grids; each grid
  places it under its own register, so the same note can appear at different cells on the
  two grids -- it shows where to finger to match the other surface.
- *Trails (dim).* Up to ~trails_max~ recent *pitch classes* (shared across both grids,
  octave-independent; 9 here) stay lit *dim* after release, until newer presses push them
  out. A trailed class that is also currently sounding shows *bright* (bright clobbers dim).
  Repeating a note keeps a single entry, and playing a note clears any trailed neighbour
  within ~1/trail_clobber_radius~ of an octave (1/27 here) -- so the dim backdrop stays
  sparse. Both are set in the ~[surfaces]~ table.
- *Waveform strip*: the selected waveform is bright, the other three dim; a grid's strip
  shows the waveform *it controls* (its own timbre, in this rig).
- *Volume strip*: the active cell is lit, the rest dark; it shows the grid's own volume.
- *Scroll pad*: the four arrows are *dim*, the two octave corners dark. The pad fully
  occludes the edo grid beneath it -- no note or trail ever shows on a pad cell.
- *Distortion toggle*: *dim* at rest, *bright* while the global distortion is on;
  mirrored on both grids (one switch).
- *Accrete buttons*: *dim* at rest (findable, like the arrows), *bright* when active --
  clear while pressed, needs-holding while on, accrete while the accreting condition is
  live. Mirrored on both grids (one shared state). A *sustained* note paints exactly
  like a fingered one: bright octave-equivalents on both grids, until cleared.

*Monobright vs varibright.* One of the two 16x16 grids (an old Series 256, id =m256-282=)
cannot dim a single LED -- it thresholds any level <= 7 to off. So its "dim" (unselected
waveform cells, trails, arrows) is *faked by flashing* the cell on/off fast (~1/32 duty,
~67 Hz) via whole-quad writes; the varibright grid (id =m0000102=) uses a native dim
level. The runtime decides per grid from the *serial id* (both report type "monome 256").
With many dim cells lit at once the flash slows into mild flicker on the monobright grid
-- an accepted trade.

* the waveform control (how to read it)

Each grid's strip re-timbres its *own* grid. In the config this is the =controls=
field of the =waveform_selector= window:

#+begin_src toml
[[monome_windows]]        # on grid A
id = "wave-a"
monome = "a"
kind = "waveform_selector"
rect = [0, 0, 3, 0]
controls = "a"            # -> sets grid A's own timbre
#+end_src

So: press a cell on *grid A's* strip -> *grid A's* future notes use that waveform,
and the strip lights the chosen cell (a strip always *displays what it controls*).
The change is *radio* semantics: it affects future notes on the target grid; notes
already held keep their waveform.

Historical note: this rig originally *cross-controlled* (each strip set the OTHER
grid, 6_plan-style "edit X on the not-X surface"); per =TODO/misc.org= (2026-07)
the two-edo-grid rigs now self-control. The =controls= field still accepts any
play grid, so cross-wiring remains a config choice, not a code change.

* sustain ("accrete")

Three buttons at each grid's bottom-left; both grids drive *one* shared state
(pressing clear on grid B kills a drone started from grid A). Requested in
=TODO/misc.org= "sustain (accrete)".

| cell   | button        | on key-down                            | LED                     |
|--------+---------------+----------------------------------------+-------------------------|
| (0,15) | clear         | silence + flush the sustained set      | bright while pressed    |
| (1,15) | needs-holding | toggle how *accrete* behaves           | bright while on         |
| (2,15) | accrete       | hold / toggle accretion (see below)    | bright while accreting  |

- *needs-holding OFF* (the default): tapping *accrete* toggles "accrete mode". While
  it is on, every note played -- plus any that were held when it turned on -- joins
  the *sustained set*. Un-toggling stops additions; nothing leaves the set.
- *needs-holding ON*: notes fingered while *accrete* is physically held join the set
  (including notes pressed before the hold began -- they are captured at the moment
  the hold starts, or when they are released under a live hold).
- A note in the set keeps ringing after its finger lifts -- the very same voice, so
  the transition is seamless -- until *clear* flushes everything. There is no
  per-note removal.
- Toggling *needs-holding* cancels an active accrete mode (the set is untouched).
- Sustained voices follow the *volume fader* of the grid they came from, and paint
  bright on both grids like any sounding note.

* distortion

The =(0,1)= cell on either grid toggles a *global* distortion (key-down; key-up does
nothing) applied to the *sum* of all synth voices -- fingered and sustained, both
grids -- before the output clamp (and inside the oversampling loop, so its harmonics
don't alias). The drums bypass it. The curve is the algebraic soft-clipper
=f(y) = y / (1 + |y/s|^k)^(1/k)= ([[../../../learnings/distortion.org]]); =s= and =k=
are the sink's =distortion_scale= / =distortion_shape= (defaults 1.0 / 2.0 -- a
clearly-audible, smooth saturation).

* the KMSS drumkit

Identical to [[./kmss-drumkit.org]] -- the same pedal->sample map, the same tether-
mode velocity path, the same =cpal_sampler= sink. The surfaces runtime *consumes*
the drumkit's bring-up (=drumkit_runtime::start=) rather than forking it, so any
future drum change (kit, decode, velocity feel) is inherited for free. Pedal map:

| foot  | pedal | voice        |
|-------+-------+--------------|
| left  |     1 | kick         |
| left  |     2 | snare        |
| left  |     6 | low tom      |
| left  |     7 | high tom     |
| left  |     8 | crash        |
| right |     4 | hat          |
| right |     5 | ride         |
| right |     9 | cowbell      |
| right |     0 | wood block   |

* what is deliberately NOT here

- No looper (no slots / transport / loop display), no remap, no piano.
- No AM/FM on the play grids -- the strips control waveform + volume only. (The
  waveform-capable synth *can* do AM/FM; surfaces leaves those at their inert defaults.)
- No on-grid metronome. (The only flashing is the monobright grid faking its dim LEDs --
  see *LEDs* above -- not a clock.)
