*** the range blows up if the row is the full grid width
This is the load-bearing consequence of (1a) and it's quietly broken as written.
The vision's numbers all assume *8 buttons*: "if 8 buttons, the highest value is
32 Hz" (0.25 x 2^7). But the timbre window spans the grid, so a row is plausibly

  separate field -- so the .toml carries =[low, high]= per row and the per-cell

- *Or fix the factor and the floor, and make the rows 8 wide* (half the grid),

** 3c. Slow AM -- and the answer to "is there something like that?"
Yes. You asked for a one-parameter family from sine to square; the standard cheap
answer is a *tanh-shaped sine*:

#+begin_example
y(p) = tanh(k * sin(2*pi*p)) / tanh(k)
#+end_example

As k -> 0 this is a pure sine; as k -> inf it approaches sign(sin) = square; it's
monotonic in k and smooth in between. Map the shape cell index to k (e.g.
k = t/(1-t) for t in [0,1)). For the *unipolar* AM multiplier, scale the result
into =[floor, 1]=.

Two corrections to the vision's own asides:
- *Iterating sine does NOT approach a square.* sin(sin(sin(...))) *flattens and
  shrinks* toward zero -- it gets rounder and quieter, the opposite of square. So
  that fallback is unsound; drop it.
- *Cost is a non-issue here regardless.* The modulator is a 0.25-32 Hz LFO, not an
  audio-rate oscillator, so even an expensive shape is negligible -- and you can
  precompute each shape as a small wavetable. So choose the shape for *sound*, not
  cost.

If you'd rather keep the cheaper *triangle<->square* fallback you describe (widen
the triangle's points into flats until the sides go vertical), that's a clean
trapezoid wave -- exact, piecewise-linear, monotonic in one parameter. It's a fine
choice; just note its endpoints are triangle and square, *not* sine and square, so

* 4. Reuse and implementation notes
"These should build on the code in =configs/= and =monome-looper=" -- the
build/borrow split as the code stands:

** Reusable:
*** *Slot store/recall* is the =scale_slots= / =scale_control= idiom from
=..._save-scales.toml= (arm, then press a slot to capture; press to recall),
the same shape as the looper's copy gesture. The arm-timbre button and timbre
slots should lift it directly.
*** *Single-lit-cell rows*
resemble the offset board's row selection -- a known pattern.
*** *Loop-region selection*
reuses the looper's rows/columns/cells pickers and their
mode-exclusivity wholesale; only the *application* step is new (1c).
*** *Window Z-order / overlay*
uses the existing back-to-front compositor (README),
so "activate button drawn on top" needs no new mechanism -- just a toggleable
window whose visibility is mutually exclusive across the two monomes.
** Genuinely new (nothing today does these):
*** *Polytimbral voices* -- waveform/gain/AM/FM per =VoiceState=, and a
  =render_block= that branches and modulates per voice (1b). The biggest piece.
*** *AM and FM DSP* -- per-voice (or shared) LFOs, the sine<->square shape morph
(3c), cents-based FM depth.
*** *Real sine/square/saw oscillators*, band-limited, replacing the lone triangle
(and reconciling the "saw" sink name).
*** *Per-note timbre in the loop* -- a timbre field on stored loop notes plus the
routing so loop playback voices carry it (1b/1c).
*** *A timbre overlay that can move between the two monomes* and suspends/occludes
what's under it (1d).
*** *Loop-mode timbre performance* (1c) -- a playhead-following read head that
drives the six rows from the lowest still-sounding note's stored timbre, plus
three controls: a per-row live override (toggle; value persists on key-up), a
"recall whole timbre," and a momentary "forget" that drops the override. Whether
it also *writes* the loop is the open question at 1c.
*** *Record-time timbre capture* (likely; pending 1c) -- each recorded loop note
remembering the live timbre it was played with is probably the only thing that
ever writes per-note storage.
