#+title: Unitary implementation plan: sustain, chords, and compact loops

* Authority and scope

This plan is the single declarative specification for the next revision of
=monomes_two-timbres_sustain_chords=.  It incorporates the settled decisions in
=5_layout-and-loops.org= and supersedes the layout and behavior portions of
=3_plan.org= where they differ.  The implementation remains in the surfaces
runtime; it does not select or embed the older metronomic looper runtime.

The two physical monomes remain identical, independent instruments.  Each owns
its own sustain state, chord bank, compact-loop bank, loop clocks, phase mode,
record target, recording state, layer selections, and LEDs.  Chord and loop
storage is keyed by physical serial id.  Audio synthesis and crowded-pitch
allocation remain shared so collisions between otherwise independent sources
are still handled globally.

* Final grid layout

The top two rows contain the timbre, target, volume, and register controls:

#+begin_example
y=0  W W W W TARGET . . . . . . . .  ^  ^  ^
y=1  V V V V      . . . . . . . .  ^  ^  ^
#+end_example

The waveform selector remains =(0..3,0)=, the volume selector remains
=(0..3,1)=, the single three-state TARGET control is =(4,0)=, and the six
register-navigation cells move to =(13..15,0..1)=.  The four corner arrows in
that six-cell block always perform register navigation.  Their delta is the
rig's configured =edo=; no octave size is hard-coded.

The bottom two rows are exactly:

#+begin_example
       chord half, x=0..7                  loop half, x=8..15
y=14   C C C C C C C ACCRETE              CLEAR-SUSTAIN SELECT-RECORD L L L L L L
y=15   C C C C C C CHORD-MODE ARM-CHORD   STOP START-RECORD L L L L L PHASE-MODE
#+end_example

The thirteen chord slots are row-major =(0..6,14)= followed by =(0..5,15)=.
The eleven loop slots are row-major =(10..15,14)= followed by =(10..14,15)=.
The fixed controls are:

- ACCRETE =(7,14)=;
- CLEAR SUSTAIN =(8,14)=;
- SELECT RECORD TARGET =(9,14)=;
- MOMENTARY/TOGGLE CHORD MODE =(6,15)=;
- ARM CHORD STORAGE =(7,15)=;
- STOP LOOP RECORDING =(8,15)=;
- START LOOP RECORDING =(9,15)=; and
- LOOP PHASE MODE =(15,15)=.

The rig schema exposes these regions to the surfaces runtime without invoking
legacy loop dispatch.  Geometry validation checks their exact dimensions,
non-overlap, grid bounds, and required pairing with the EDO note grid and
self-controlling timbre and volume selectors.

* Three layer targets

TARGET cycles in this order:

1. fingered/sustained, shown black;
2. recalled chord, shown dim; and
3. compact loop, shown solid.

Each target remembers its own selected timbre.  The waveform selector reads and
changes the selected target only.  Each timbre has one fingered/sustained base
gain, one chord dB delta from that base, and one loop dB delta from that base.
Changing the base moves all three effective gains for that timbre; changing a
chord or loop delta moves only that layer.  The volume selector displays and
edits the effective gain of the selected target.

Layer changes update live voices immediately.  Chord target edits affect every
currently recalled chord voice on that monome.  Loop target edits affect every
currently sounding compact-loop voice on that monome and become the timbre and
gain used for future loop attacks.  Stored chords and loops contain pitch and
timing information only, never timbre, gain, envelope, or crowded-pitch offset.

With fingered/sustained selected, the register corners move the playing
register.  With chord selected, they glide currently recalled chord voices but
do not rewrite stored pitches.  With loop selected, they permanently transpose
the current record target's stored nominal pitches by one configured EDO step,
persist the change atomically, and glide that slot's live voices if it is
sounding.  Loop-target register edits are ignored while a recording is active.
While record-target selection is merely awaiting a destination, they still
address the existing record target.

* Sustain controls

ACCRETE keeps its existing musical semantics.  CLEAR SUSTAIN removes only
accreted sustain membership and releases only fingerless sustain voices.  A
finger still physically holding the originating note continues to sound, but
that voice loses its right to survive its later key-up.  The decision follows
voice ownership rather than pitch, so a separate sustained voice at the same
nominal pitch may end without ending the held finger voice.

CLEAR SUSTAIN never releases or unlatches recalled chords, stops loop playback,
clears either persistent bank, or ends a physically held finger voice.  The
existing broader clear behavior remains available to other rigs and pedal
paths; this rig's cell invokes the source-specific operation.

At rest ACCRETE and CLEAR SUSTAIN display opposite halves of one one-second,
50/50-duty cycle.  A physically held control is solid and overrides the idle
animation.

* Chord bank

The chord bank contains thirteen pitch-only slots.  Existing version-one
persistence retains the former first eight slots in the same row-major order
and pads slots nine through thirteen as empty.  Loading, normalization, and
atomic saving continue to be keyed by physical monome serial id; mock/headless
runs do not write hardware persistence.

CHORD MODE begins solid in momentary mode.  In momentary mode a slot key-down
recalls an occupied chord and its key-up releases exactly that recall.  In
toggle mode CHORD MODE is black and an occupied slot key-down independently
toggles that slot's recall; key-up has no musical effect and any number of
slots may be latched simultaneously.  Empty slot presses do nothing.  Changing
from toggle mode to momentary mode immediately releases every latched recall
through the normal release ramp and leaves no hidden latch state.

ARM CHORD STORAGE flashes at 200 ms on / 200 ms off while armed.  In momentary
mode it overwrites all physically held source chord slots; in toggle mode it
overwrites all latched-on source chord slots.  If no source chord slot is in
use, the arm state remains available for the next destination press according
to the existing storage gesture.  Capture takes the normalized nominal pitches
currently emitted by local fingered, sustained, and eligible recalled-chord
sources.  It never captures compact-loop playback or crowded-pitch deviations.

A chord slot is black while empty, dim while populated and silent, and solid
while any recall owned by that slot is sounding.  A source release, mode
transition, clear operation, or another slot's action releases only voices it
owns; equal-pitch recalls remain independent.

* Compact-loop data model

Each monome owns eleven compact-loop slots.  A populated slot contains an
immutable positive cycle duration and a multiset of identity-bearing nominal
pitch intervals.  An interval is either finite, with a normalized start phase
and duration less than the cycle, or a full-cycle drone.  Equal pitches and
repeated performances remain separate occurrences.  A loop voice is owned by
its monome, slot, and occurrence, so gating or clearing one slot cannot release
another source at the same pitch.

The runtime maintains a per-monome registry of every live, recordable logical
source voice: fingered, accreted sustain, and recalled chord.  Each registry
entry has a stable identity, exact nominal pitch, and actual monotonic onset
time, and survives the ownership transitions needed by the sustain model.
Compact-loop playback voices are never entered in this registry.  Recording
therefore observes logical source lifetimes rather than audio-envelope tails
and never feeds playback from any loop on either monome back into either
recorder.

For cycle duration =D= and a source lifetime =[a,b)=, actual onset may precede
START LOOP RECORDING.  If =b-a < D=, recording stores a finite interval starting
at =a mod D= with its true duration, including wraparound.  If =b-a >= D=, it
stores a full-cycle drone.  Equality is a drone.  A source still live at STOP is
cut at STOP unless its actual lifetime has already reached =D=, in which case it
is a drone.  An overdub lasting several cycles folds every performed occurrence
modulo the fixed cycle and preserves successive-lap layering without
deduplication.

All input and playback boundaries use monotonic timestamps plus event order.
There is no quantization, metronome, grace window, or wall-clock phase.  Events
accepted before START remain eligible through their remembered actual onset;
events accepted at or after START are recorded; events processed through STOP
are included; later events are not.  At an exact playback phase boundary an
onset is included once and is not also delivered by the scheduler as a second
attack.

* Record target and capture transaction

Loop slot 1, =(10,14)=, is the record target at every program start whether it
is empty or populated.  The target remains selected after recording, clearing,
or playback changes.

SELECT RECORD TARGET enters a waiting state and flashes at 200 ms on / 200 ms
off.  The existing target continues flashing too.  The next loop-slot key-down
is consumed solely as the new record-target choice, even when it chooses the
same slot, and never toggles playback.  Pressing SELECT RECORD TARGET again
cancels only the waiting state and retains the old target.  During recording,
SELECT RECORD TARGET and all retarget attempts are ignored; ordinary slot
presses remain playback controls.

START LOOP RECORDING begins one capture transaction.  A second START during the
transaction is ignored and cannot restart timing or move a boundary.  STOP with
no transaction is a no-op and never stops playback.

For an empty target, accepted START establishes phase zero and accepted STOP
establishes duration exactly as =STOP - START=.  A normal first take that
contains no resulting voice intervals is discarded: the slot remains empty,
dormant, and targeted, and any pending playback request is cancelled.  For a
populated target, recording is an overdub against its immutable existing
duration and current recording phase; the committed interval multiset is
extended rather than replaced.  An eventless overdub is a no-op.

The transaction records local fingered, sustained, and recalled-chord logical
sources whether the target is audible or muted.  Their already audible live
voices are not doubled for monitoring.  Playback gates may change during an
overdub, but playback is excluded from capture.

If elapsed time from accepted START to accepted STOP is strictly less than
=loop_clear_tap_ms=, STOP performs a clear instead of a commit.  The parameter
defaults to 300 ms.  Clear releases the target slot's playback voices, turns
off its gate, cancels pending playback, discards its content and phase, removes
its persisted entry atomically, and leaves the same empty target flashing.
Other slots are untouched.  Equality with the threshold is a normal recording,
so the threshold is also the minimum duration for a new populated loop.

First takes and overdubs are accumulated in temporary state and are published
to memory and persistence only by a normal STOP.  A process interruption before
STOP leaves an old populated target unchanged or an empty target empty.
Playback reads only committed contents and can never see a half-built overdub.

* Playback gates and phase modes

A populated loop-slot key-down toggles that slot's playback gate; key-up has no
musical effect.  Any number of slots may sound simultaneously.  An empty slot
press is normally a no-op.  During that same empty target's first recording,
each press toggles a pending play-on-completion bit.  A normal populated commit
with the bit on begins playback at the newly closed boundary and phase zero;
an empty result or clear discards the bit.

Playback-on reconstructs every interval containing the selected current phase,
including wrapping intervals and full-cycle drones.  An onset exactly at that
phase is on.  All reconstructed and scheduled voices use the rig's ordinary
attack ramp.  Playback-off releases all voices owned by that slot through the
ordinary release ramp.  Rapid off/on therefore produces a new ramped attack,
not an instantaneous amplitude step.

LOOP PHASE MODE is per monome, is not persisted, begins in KEEP-RUNNING mode,
and applies to all eleven slots.  It flashes at 200 ms on / 200 ms off in
KEEP-RUNNING and is black in RESTART.

Every populated slot starts silent and dormant at phase zero on program launch.
In KEEP-RUNNING, its first playback trigger begins the clock and playback at
phase zero.  Thereafter the phase advances while its playback gate is either on
or off, and later triggers reconstruct at the advancing phase.  In RESTART, a
muted slot has no advancing phase; playback-on establishes phase zero and
playback-off releases its voices and returns it to dormant phase zero.

Changing to RESTART discards every slot's old phase.  Sounding slots release and
reconstruct at phase zero through the normal ramps and continue; muted slots
become dormant at zero.  Changing to KEEP-RUNNING leaves muted slots dormant at
zero until triggered and lets already sounding slots continue from their
current phase without a jump.

Recording alone never counts as the first KEEP-RUNNING playback trigger.  An
overdub of a dormant populated target uses a temporary phase beginning at zero
and returns to dormant zero on STOP unless playback was triggered.  Once
triggered, the persistent KEEP-RUNNING clock advances normally.  In RESTART, a
muted target records from phase zero, an already sounding target records at its
audible phase, and playback-on during capture deliberately re-establishes phase
zero.  Exact command order may therefore change overdub phase placement in
RESTART but never changes stored duration.

The per-grid event loop advances clocks and schedules interval boundaries from
absolute monotonic time.  It handles elapsed spans crossing multiple cycles
without dropping or duplicating occurrences.  Gate reconstruction and the
scheduler share one ordered boundary rule so a gate-on at an onset cannot
double-trigger it.

* Loop LEDs

Loop slot LEDs have this strict priority:

1. being recorded to and playing back: alternating 80 ms bright / 80 ms dim,
   never black;
2. record target: flashing at 200 ms on / 200 ms off;
3. sounding: solid;
4. populated and silent: dim; and
5. empty and untargeted: black.

Except for the faster record-plus-play indication, the target flashes even when
it is sounding or being overdubbed.  Multiple non-target sounding slots are all
solid.  START LOOP RECORDING flashes at the same rate while a transaction is
active and is otherwise black.  STOP LOOP RECORDING is solid only while
physically held.  SELECT RECORD TARGET flashes only while awaiting a slot.  LOOP
PHASE MODE uses its mode indication described above.

* Loop persistence

Compact-loop persistence is separate from legacy looper and chord files.  Its
versioned format stores, per physical serial id, eleven slots containing only
duration and nominal-pitch interval occurrences.  It does not store playback
gate, record target, phase epoch, phase mode, layer timbre/gain, envelope, or
crowded-pitch allocation.

Writes use the repository's existing temp-file, flush, and atomic-rename
pattern.  A successful normal STOP, clear, or loop-target octave edit writes a
complete new per-rig image while retaining other connected grids' entries.
Malformed entries are rejected or normalized without panicking, and a missing
or older file yields empty compact-loop slots.  Persistence is disabled when
no physical serial identity/audio runtime is present, matching chord storage.

* Global crowded-pitch allocation

The CPAL synth exposes =crowded_pitch_planck_deviation=, documented in the rig
as cents and initially set to =5=.  Zero disables separation.  This rule
supersedes this rig's special =retrigger_tail_detune_cents = 6= behavior; the
older parameter remains backward-compatible for rigs that still use it.

All currently live surface voices are grouped by exact nominal pitch before
detuning, across both monomes and across fingered, sustained, recalled-chord,
and compact-loop sources.  With configured step =p=, each new voice receives
the first unoccupied lane in:

#+begin_example
0, +p, -p, +2p, -2p, +3p, -3p, ...
#+end_example

The lane is converted to a frequency ratio only at synthesis.  It remains
attached to that voice for its whole audible lifetime, including its release
tail, and becomes available when the audio voice is actually reaped.  Existing
voices are never moved to fill a newly vacant lane.  Voices sharing one exact
input timestamp are randomly ordered before lane allocation.  Allocation is
performance state: it is absent from chord and loop persistence, and every new
recall or playback attack is allocated afresh.

Distinct play-grid cells that are enharmonic under the configured =x_step= and
=y_step= remain distinct finger voices.  If, for example, =(x,y)= and
=(x+1,y-k)= resolve to the same absolute EDO step, holding both invokes the same
crowded-pitch lane allocation as any other exact-nominal-pitch collision;
releasing either cell leaves the other voice untouched.

Every voice source has an independent synthesis key.  Sustain ownership
transfers and nominal-pitch glides preserve that voice's lifetime allocation;
they never allow one source's release to remove another source.  The allocator
and voice map use one documented lock order, and stale lane records are pruned
against the voice map so a panic, release, or reload cannot permanently occupy
a lane.

* Runtime integration and reload

The surfaces runtime gains explicit compact-loop state, persistence, input
dispatch, scheduler ticks, paint state, and synth voice operations.  Loop
playback receives a distinct =VoiceSource= identity.  Source lifecycle helpers
centralize logical onset/release tracking so fingers, sustain transitions, chord
recalls, clears, register shifts, disconnect cleanup, and reload cleanup cannot
silently bypass recording.

Reload preserves audio safety: all voices and transient record/playback state
belonging to a removed or changed grid are released through normal ramps.  New
layout and numeric parameters participate in reload comparison and validation.
Persistent chord and loop contents survive process reload through their files,
while transient chord mode, latches, loop target, gates, clocks, selection wait,
recording transaction, and phase mode return to their startup states.

The rig and operator documentation state the final cell map, three-target
controls, source-specific clear, chord modes, direct loop playback, recording
and overdub gestures, sub-threshold clear, phase modes, persistence scope,
octave behavior, and crowded-pitch parameter.  Obsolete descriptions of the
5x2 chord block, eight slots, top-right chord placement, six-cent tail special
case, and legacy PLAY/STOP transport are removed for this rig.

* Verification

Unit tests cover:

- exact layout geometry, coordinates, and three TARGET states;
- independent per-target timbre and base/chord/loop gain arithmetic;
- source-specific sustain clear and its finger-held edge case;
- thirteen-slot momentary/toggle chord behavior, mode transition, arming,
  equal-pitch ownership, and eight-to-thirteen persistence migration;
- record-target selection/cancellation, first take, silent first take, overdub,
  multi-cycle folding, pre-existing voices, wrap intervals, drones, exact
  duration boundary, and sub-threshold clear;
- direct multi-slot playback, pending play-on-completion, phase
  reconstruction, exact-boundary single attacks, attack/release gating, and
  independent equal-pitch occurrences;
- KEEP-RUNNING and RESTART clocks, mode transitions, dormant overdub behavior,
  startup reset, and multi-cycle scheduler catch-up;
- eleven-slot persistence, atomic transaction publication, clear, octave edit,
  malformed input, per-serial isolation, and headless suppression; and
- crowded-pitch lane order, lifetime stability, gap reuse without reshuffling,
  global cross-source/cross-grid collisions, simultaneous tie randomization,
  tuning-derived enharmonic-cell collisions, release-tail occupancy, zero-step
  disablement, and legacy tail-detune compatibility.

The implementation is complete when =cargo fmt --all -- --check=,
=cargo test -p edo_surface=, and =cargo test --workspace= pass, the current rig
loads in mock mode, and the documented LED/control state can be exercised
without persistence writes or audio hardware.
