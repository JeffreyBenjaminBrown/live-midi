#+title: one monome, two timbre states, sustain/accrete -- discussion

This responds to =1_vision.org=.  I read =ORIENTATION.org=, =rigs/README.org=,
the current two-edogrid rig and its reference, the chord-storage-v2 discussion
and plan, and the live surfaces runtime (especially =keys.rs=, =grid.rs=,
=settings.rs=, =synth.rs=, =chords.rs=, and =chords_persist.rs=).

Verdict up front: nearly all of the machinery exists, but this is not just a
one-monome rearrangement of the current rig.  The vision changes the chord
model in musically important ways: chord voices stop retaining their captured
timbre and loudness, the chord controls apparently shrink from nine slots to
eight, and octave shift becomes a temporary per-slot chord transformation.
Those choices are coherent, but a plan would have to guess at the layout and
at several interaction rules.  The TODO headings below are the decisions I
still need from Jeff before writing that plan.

* What already exists and can be reused

- A whole-monome EDO play surface, a four-cell timbre selector, the shift pad,
  momentary accretion, sustain clear, and chord storage are all live rig window
  kinds.  This work should compose those pieces rather than revive the older
  =sawwave/state.rs= chord implementation.
- The selector already has the useful split behavior that changing timbre
  applies live to an edit selection when there is one, with a short crossfade;
  otherwise it changes future notes.  The new two-target switch is a different
  split (piano layer versus chord layer), but the live retimbre path exists.
- The synth already separates a timbre slot's =gain=, a per-grid volume-fader
  gain, and an expression-pedal gain.  It can therefore change this new control
  without baking the EX-P or master gain into it.
- A horizontal =volume_fader= window exists, but it maps absolute positions
  over a fixed 30 dB range.  The proposed four buttons are relative dB steps,
  so they need a small new window/control kind rather than a differently sized
  existing fader.
- Chord slots already persist atomically under =state/chord-slots/=.  Today a
  stored voice snapshot includes pitch, timbre, loudness, pulse, and phase, and
  different slots can sound at once.  The new shared chord timbre/volume rule
  deliberately supersedes part of that format/recall behavior.
- Current shift-pad octave corners retune the entire edit selection whenever
  one exists; otherwise they scroll the visible register.  Routing them to a
  chosen chord slot is new, but the in-place octave glide exists.
- Square and diamond dances are optional presentation behavior, not required
  for voices to sound or be edited; omitting them is straightforward.

* TODO 2. The physical layout names two different things in the same corners

The vision puts the timbre buttons and four volume deltas at the top-right, and
also puts the 2x5 chord block at the top-right.  At bottom-left it puts clear +
momentary accrete, and also “the usual shift buttons.”  These cannot literally
occupy the same cells unless one set is an overlay selected by a mode, which is
not otherwise described.

Please give rectangles or a little 16x16 sketch.  At minimum I need to know:

- where the four timbre cells and four volume-delta cells go, and whether they
  are two horizontal rows in the order described;
- where the 2x5 chord block goes;
- where clear, momentary accrete, and the shift pad go.

* TODO 3. Confirm that this is eight chord slots, and define their order

In a 2x5 block, reserving the top-left cell for the target switch and the
top-right cell for ARM leaves eight slots.  The existing block instead has ARM
at bottom-left and nine slots, numbered top row 1..5 then bottom row 6..9.

Is eight the intended count?  If so, how are they numbered across the remaining
cells, and should this rig use a new eight-slot persistence file rather than try
to read an existing nine-slot file?

* TODO 4. Precisely what the two timbre/volume states own

I read the intended targets as:

1. the piano layer: fingered notes plus ordinary sustained/accreted notes;
2. the chord layer: every voice recalled from any chord slot.

I also read “there are just two states” as each target having one selected
timbre and one loudness value, with the four delta buttons changing that
target's loudness immediately and cumulatively.  Two details remain unclear:

- Does selecting a timbre or changing volume immediately retimbre/rescale all
  voices currently sounding in the chosen layer, as well as future voices?
- Are the loudness values one per layer, or one per timbre slot within each
  layer?  “Changes the volume only of timbre currently in use” could mean
  either “the selected layer's current sound” or four remembered gains per
  layer.  What are the initial gains and the permitted min/max?

The proposed =volume_coarse = 12= and =volume_fine = 2= are naturally dB
amounts, producing =[-coarse, -fine, +fine, +coarse]=.  Please confirm they are
dB, not linear gain units.

* TODO 5. What a saved chord retains under the new global chord state

Current chord snapshots retain timbre and settled loudness per voice.  The
vision says they should not: recalled chords instead use the one global chord
timbre and volume.  I assume slots should still retain pitch, phase, and
factored-pulse data, and that changing the chord target's controls transforms
all currently ringing chord voices without modifying slot contents.

Please confirm that assumption.  In particular, should saving a currently
ringing chord into another slot discard its present chord-layer timbre and
loudness, while preserving its pitches/pulse/phase?

* TODO 6. Which chord receives an octave shift when slots can layer?

The current chord slots are toggles and several can ring simultaneously; there
is no navigated “current slot.”  The vision refers to “the active stored chord”
and to navigating away from it, which suggests a new selected-slot concept.

What selects the octave-shift target?

- the most recently pressed sounding slot;
- exactly one slot may sound at a time;
- a slot remains selected even while silent;
- or some other rule?

Also, when the shifted chord is saved “the usual way,” is the intended gesture
ARM then the same slot (overwrite it with the shifted pitches), or ARM then any
destination slot?  Until saved, should toggling the slot off and on restore its
stored octave, or keep its temporary shift until another slot is selected?

* TODO 7. Interaction with edit mode

The vision removes the dances but does not say whether it removes per-voice edit
mode itself.  This matters because the current timbre selector edits selected
voices instead of changing the future-note timbre, and the shift-pad corners
retune the edit selection instead of scrolling.

Should this rig have per-voice edit mode at all?  If yes, which precedence wins
when an edit selection exists: the new layer target for timbre/volume and chord
octave shift, or the existing edit-selection behavior?

* Settled unless Jeff says otherwise

- The target switch begins off, so the piano layer is selected at startup.
- Clear ends ordinary sustain and all live chord voices, matching the current
  widened sustain-clear behavior.
- Accrete is momentary only: key-down captures currently fingered piano-layer
  notes into sustain; key-up merely ends capture mode.
- Timbre changes should crossfade and volume changes should use the existing
  gain slew so neither operation clicks.
- “No square dance, no diamond dance” affects only LED animation; sounding
  notes and overlay controls still paint normally.

Once the seven TODO decisions are answered, the plan can be concrete rather
than encoding accidental assumptions into the rig, persistence format, and
interaction tests.
