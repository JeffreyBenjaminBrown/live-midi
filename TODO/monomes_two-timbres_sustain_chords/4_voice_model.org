#+title: monomes, two timbre layers, sustain, and momentary chords -- note/voice model

* Scope and the central distinction

This describes the note model actually implemented for
=monomes_two-timbres_sustain_chords=.  Timbre and volume routing are deliberately
out of scope.  The report follows note identity, ownership, lifetime, accretion,
storage, recall, shifting, collisions, and release.

The player's useful three-way vocabulary is:

1. a *fingered note*: a play-surface cell is physically held;
2. a *sustained note*: an absolute pitch belongs to this grid's accrete set, so
   it may continue without a finger;
3. a *stored chord note*: an absolute pitch is present in one of this grid's
   eight chord slots.

Those are not three parallel kinds of live audio voice.  In particular, a
stored note is dormant data and makes no sound.  Holding its slot creates a
fourth useful concept, a *recalled chord voice*.  A recalled voice is live and
independently releasable; the slot entry from which it came remains unchanged.

There is also a hidden implementation category, the *release tail*.  A tail is
still temporarily audible, but it has already ceased to be fingered, sustained,
or live-recalled for the purposes of buttons, LEDs, capture, and later musical
operations.

#+begin_example
physical cell down        accrete pitch membership       chord-slot data
       |                           |                           |
       v                           v                           | slot down
  FINGER VOICE  -- key up -->  DRONE VOICE                    v
       |             if sustained       |              RECALLED VOICE
       |                                |                     |
       +------ ordinary key up ---------+------ clear --------+
                         |                                    |
                         v                                    v
                    RELEASE TAIL <------- slot up / clear -----+
                         |
                         v
                       reaped
#+end_example

The arrow from a finger to a drone moves the same audio voice; it does not
restrike it.  Every other ending uses a short release before the audio object is
reaped.

* Four separate records of reality

The apparent complexity mostly comes from keeping four different questions in
four different data structures.  No one structure is the complete truth.

| Question | Per-grid record | Identity |
|----------+-----------------+----------|
| Which play cells are physically down, and what did each strike? | =held= map | cell -> absolute pitch |
| Which pitches are entitled to ring after all fingers lift? | =RingStore::Sustain= | absolute pitch set |
| What does each chord slot contain? | =MomentaryChordLayer::slots= | eight optional sorted pitch sets |
| Which recalled voices are live right now? | =MomentaryChordLayer::live= | unique sequence -> source slot + current pitch |

The shared audio map is a fifth, lower-level record.  Its key says who can
address a voice:

- =SurfaceFinger { grid, cell }= is controlled by one physical play cell;
- =SurfaceDrone { grid, pitch }= is the one addressable sustain voice at that
  pitch on that grid;
- =SurfaceChord { grid, seq }= is one recalled voice, independently of its
  pitch and independently of every other recall;
- =SurfaceRetired { grid, seq }= is an unaddressed tail left by cutting a
  drone for a retrigger.

The grid number is part of all four live identities.  Two grids may therefore
have completely independent voices at the same absolute pitch.  Their controls,
held maps, sustain sets, live recalls, and slot data are independent.  Only the
visual pitch-class reflection is unioned across grids.

* Fingered notes

** Identity

A finger is keyed by its cell, not by its pitch.  The pitch is calculated at
key-down and recorded in the held map.  Later scrolling does not alter that
struck pitch or orphan the release.

Cell identity also permits tuning collisions.  In this 41-EDO layout, several
different cells can denote the same absolute pitch.  Holding two such cells
creates two independent finger voices.  Consequently “is pitch P fingered?” is
really a count, not a boolean.

** Ordinary lifecycle

On an ordinary key-down, a fresh finger voice attacks and the held map records
=cell -> pitch=.  On key-up:

- if the pitch is not sustained, the finger voice begins its release and the
  held entry disappears;
- if the pitch is sustained, the same audio state is moved from its cell key to
  the pitch-keyed drone key, without a new attack;
- if a drone already occupies that pitch, the extra finger voice releases
  instead of replacing it.  One drone is sufficient for a pitch-keyed sustain
  set.

Thus an audible release tail is no longer “fingered”: it has no held-map entry
and is excluded from a later chord capture.

* Sustained notes and ACCRETE

** Membership and voice are different things

The sustain bank is a set of absolute pitches.  Membership is a reason for a
note to survive finger release; it is not itself an audio voice.

While a sustained pitch is still under a finger, the live voice normally
remains a =SurfaceFinger=.  It becomes a =SurfaceDrone= only when a finger
lifts.  This distinction explains why the same pitch may be in the sustain set
without there yet being a drone key, and why CLEAR can remove sustain from a
held note without ending the finger's voice.

There can be only one addressable drone at a given =(grid, pitch)=.  The pitch
set is likewise deduplicated.  Multiple fingers at that pitch remain distinct
until release, but they do not create multiple sustained copies.

** ACCRETE is permanently momentary in this rig

The ACCRETE condition is true only while this grid's ACCRETE button is down.
Its edges and note edges behave as follows:

- ACCRETE down immediately adds every currently fingered pitch on this grid to
  the sustain set.
- A play note pressed while ACCRETE is down joins immediately.
- A play note released while ACCRETE is down also joins before the release
  decision.  This covers a note whose relevant down edge predates the current
  accrete hold.
- ACCRETE up merely stops further capture.  It removes no pitch already in the
  sustain set and ends no voice.

ACCRETE only captures play-surface fingers in this rig.  It does not convert
held recalled chord voices into drones, and it does not copy dormant slot data.
A recalled chord still ends when its slot is released even if ACCRETE is held.

** CLEAR

CLEAR down performs two independent live-state operations on this grid:

1. remove every pitch from the sustain set and release the corresponding
   fingerless drones;
2. unregister and release every currently recalled momentary-chord voice.

CLEAR does not:

- end a currently held finger voice;
- erase or edit any of the eight stored pitch sets;
- release the physical-held bookkeeping for a chord slot;
- cancel an armed-next-save state;
- alter the other grid.

The held-button details have useful consequences:

- If CLEAR ends a recall while its slot key is still down, that slot stays
  marked physically held but has no live voices.  Its eventual key-up is
  harmless.  It must be released and pressed again before it can recall again.
- If CLEAR removes sustain from a fingered pitch, that finger continues.  Its
  later key-up releases normally, unless ACCRETE is still down at that moment;
  in that case the release adds the pitch back and makes it a drone again.
- If ARM was flashing, CLEAR leaves it flashing.  The next destination press
  is still a save.

* Stored chord notes and recalled chord voices

** Dormant slot data

Each physical monome owns eight optional chord slots.  A nonempty slot is a
sorted, deduplicated vector of absolute 41-EDO pitches.  It stores neither the
cells which produced them nor a register-relative shape.  Duplicate instances
of a pitch are deliberately collapsed during saving.

Slots persist under
=state/chord-slots/monomes_two-timbres_sustain_chords.toml=, keyed by physical
monome serial id.  Reordering or connecting only one of the two devices does
not swap their chord spaces.

** Momentary recall

With ARM off, slot down does the following once:

1. mark the source slot physically held;
2. allocate a fresh sequence identity for each stored pitch;
3. record =(sequence, source slot, current pitch)= in the live-recall registry;
4. spawn one recalled voice for every sequence.

Slot up removes and releases only the live sequences whose source is that
slot.  Several slots may be held at once.  Their voices are independent even
when the slots contain the same pitch.  One slot cannot contain a duplicate
pitch internally, but two slots can each contribute a voice at that pitch.

An empty slot still becomes physically held, but spawns nothing.  It remains
visually dim.  Repeated down edges while already held do not duplicate a
recall.

Releasing or clearing recalled voices does not change their stored source.
The release tails remain briefly audible but leave the live registry
immediately, so they are neither shown as a sounding slot nor included in a
save made during their fade.

** What “currently sounding” means during save

The pitch set captured for a destination is the normalized union, on this grid,
of:

- every pitch value in the physical held map;
- every pitch in the sustain set;
- every pitch in the live momentary-recall registry.

This is logical sounding state, not a measurement of nonzero audio.  It
includes a sustained pitch even while its voice is still finger-keyed.  It
excludes dormant slots, ordinary release tails, retired drone tails, and chord
tails already unregistered by slot-up or CLEAR.  Multiple fingers, sustain
membership, and several recalled copies of the same pitch all collapse to one
stored pitch.

** ARM with no slot held: save the next destination

ARM down toggles the armed state when no slot key is physically held.  While
armed, a destination slot down captures the union above, writes it to that
slot, and disarms without recalling the destination.

If the captured union is empty, ARM still disarms but the destination is left
unchanged.  Pressing ARM again before choosing a destination simply unarms.

** ARM with one or more slots held: overwrite the sources now

If any slot key is physically held, ARM does not toggle next-destination mode.
It immediately captures the same union and writes that complete set to *every*
held slot, then disarms.  This is the in-place save gesture for shifted
momentary chords.

“Held” here is physical, not audible.  It includes an empty held slot and a
slot whose voices were just ended by CLEAR.  If the union is empty, no held
slot changes.  If several source slots are held, they all receive the same
combined union, including all their live pitches and any simultaneous fingers
or sustained pitches.

Overwriting storage does not alter the live voices created by the current
hold.  It changes what the next recall will instantiate.

** Octave shift

When the chord target is selected, either octave corner changes every live
momentary-recall pitch on this grid by one EDO octave.  Both the live registry
and the corresponding voice frequencies move.  The stored slots do not.

Consequently an unsaved shifted chord returns to its stored octave after
slot-up and the next slot-down.  Pressing ARM while its source slot remains held
captures the shifted live pitch and makes that octave persistent.  If several
slots are held, the shift and subsequent overwrite act on their combined live
voices.

The non-octave arrows merely move the visible play register.  Register motion
never rewrites held, sustained, stored, or recalled absolute pitches.

* Same-pitch interactions

The following table is the compact collision rule for the current reduced
momentary-chord rig.

| New event at pitch P | Existing finger at P | Existing drone at P | Existing recalled voice at P |
|----------------------+----------------------+---------------------+--------------------------------|
| another play-cell down | a second independent finger voice | cut drone to a tail; create a replacing finger voice | create an independent finger voice; recalled voice continues |
| slot recall | create an independent recalled voice | create an independent recalled voice | create another independent recalled voice |
| finger key-up, P not sustained | release only that finger | not normally this combination | recalled voice is untouched |
| finger key-up, P sustained | move finger to drone, or release it if a drone key is already occupied | keep the existing drone and release the extra finger | recalled voice is untouched |
| slot key-up | finger is untouched | drone is untouched | release only recalled sequences born from that slot |
| CLEAR | held finger survives | sustain membership is removed and the fingerless drone releases | all live recalled voices release; slot data survives |

Two direction-dependent facts are worth making explicit:

- Recall onto an already fingered or sustained pitch always layers another
  voice.
- In this new rig, fingering a pitch already sounding from a *momentary* slot
  also layers another voice.  The nearby key-handler code has an older special
  case in which fingering a legacy =chord_block= voice restrikes that chord
  voice and creates no finger voice, but that lookup consults only the legacy
  chord registry.  The new =momentary_chord_block= deliberately has a separate
  registry, so it does not take that path.

ACCRETE and storage remain orthogonal across all of these collisions.  ACCRETE
controls pitch membership for the fingered/sustained side only.  A save may
copy the pitch of either side into dormant slot data, but doing so does not
consume, merge, sustain, or otherwise alter the source voices.

* Re-fingering a sustained pitch

This is the only transition that deliberately replaces an addressable live
voice rather than simply layering:

1. the pitch remains present in the sustain set;
2. the old =SurfaceDrone { pitch }= is moved to a unique =SurfaceRetired=
   identity, shifted by the rig's configured retrigger-tail detune (initially
   six cents sharp), and begins its release;
3. its oscillator position is handed to a new =SurfaceFinger { cell }=;
4. the new finger voice performs a fresh envelope attack while continuing that
   oscillator cycle;
5. key-up later turns the replacement back into the pitch-keyed drone, because
   sustain membership was never removed.

Moving the old voice aside is necessary: it lets the replacement own the
unique drone key after release while the old tail finishes, and avoids an
instant waveform discontinuity from deleting the old object.  Only this 50 ms
tail is detuned.  The fingered voice, the sustain-set pitch, stored slot data,
and the eventual replacement drone remain on the exact EDO pitch.  The
=cpal_synth= parameter =retrigger_tail_detune_cents= is signed, accepts
-1200..1200, and is applied to future retriggers on =r= reload; zero disables
the offset.

** Why the present implementation clicks here

The outgoing tail starts offset by six cents as a deliberately small
de-correlation experiment.  Six cents accumulates limited relative phase during
a 50 ms release, however, and the new rig still enters the replacement through
the older phase-continuing note-on helper.  That helper initializes
the replacement at unity layer gain.  The handler then changes only its target
to this rig's normal fingered/sustained gain, which is initially about 0.251
(-12 dB).  The correction uses a 20 ms slew, while the attack reaches its peak
in 3 ms.  The replacement is therefore much too loud during the attack, on top
of the old drone's 50 ms retired tail.  The resulting narrow excess transient
is the reported click.

A fresh, nonsustained note does not have this mismatch: its layered constructor
starts both current and target gain at the correct value.  The clean repair is
a layer-aware phase-continuing constructor which also initializes both gain
fields to the current fingered/sustained layer value.  That preserves the
existing oscillator-continuation and release-tail model.

* Invariants, consequences, and one collision hazard

The intended invariants are:

- fingers are cell-keyed and may have pitch multiplicity;
- sustain is pitch-keyed and has at most one addressable drone per grid/pitch;
- stored slots are normalized pitch sets and are never live state;
- recalled voices are sequence-keyed and may have arbitrary pitch
  multiplicity;
- a source ending enters release without becoming capturable live state;
- all ownership and destructive controls are per-grid.

There is one current edge case where these rules do not compose cleanly.  It
requires colliding cells at one pitch:

1. hold two cells for P while P is sustained;
2. release one, producing a P drone while the other P finger remains;
3. press CLEAR.

The clear code asks whether P has any remaining finger and, because it does,
does not release the drone.  It assumes the finger is the voice to spare, but
at this point a separate drone also exists.  Sustain membership is nevertheless
removed.  When the last finger later lifts with ACCRETE off, its own voice
releases while the now-reasonless drone can continue indefinitely.

So the state model correctly records a finger *count*, but the clear-to-audio
handoff currently treats “some finger exists” as proof that no drone needs
ending.  A robust clear should independently end any actual drone at every
cleared pitch while leaving all cell-keyed fingers alone.  This is separate
from the ordinary sustained-retrigger click above.

* Button/LED summary for this state model

- ACCRETE is bright exactly while its momentary capture condition is live.
- CLEAR is bright while physically held; its destructive work occurs on
  key-down.
- ARM is black when idle and flashes when next-destination save is armed.
- A slot is dim at rest whether empty or occupied, and bright only while that
  slot owns at least one registered live recalled voice.
- The top-left TARGET cell is black in the default fingered/sustained state and
  solid bright in the chord state.  Apart from choosing the octave-corner
  behavior described above, its timbre/volume role is outside this report.
- Fingered, sustained, and live-recalled pitch classes reflect brightly across
  both grids.  That visual union does not merge their audio ownership or make
  one grid's buttons act on the other.
