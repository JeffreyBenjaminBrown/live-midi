#+title: Problems found in passing
Problems noticed while doing something else: duplication,
inelegance, deviations from a specification, bugs. One headline
each; a plan that will fix one prefixes it 'FIX PLANNED: '.

* Quasi-duplication left in the strings engine
** The two voice kinds repeat their shared state
'StringVoice' ('strings/voice.rs') and 'ModalVoice'
('strings/modal.rs') each hold the same seven fields (birth, the
excitation stream, the gain, the energy parameters, the drive
state, the energy factor, the pedal's power) with the same seven
accessors, and 'VoiceKind' forwards fourteen methods to one or
the other by hand. Each also calls 'energy::drive' the same way.
One struct holding the shared part and an enum of the members
(loops, or modes) would remove about a hundred lines and leave
'drive' one caller. Not done in plan 02: it would coin a type
name and change every test that builds a voice.
** Three rings index by hand
'Loop' (the delay line), 'HowlPath' (the howl's delay) and
'PedalHistory' each keep a 'Vec' and a write index and compute
'(index + length - delay) % length'. 'Loop' and 'HowlPath' both
write one sample and read one at a delay; one small ring type
could serve both ('Loop' also grows its ring and sums the wave
in flight). 'PedalHistory' is indexed by absolute grid point and
fits less well.
** The body's resonator and a modal mode share only their poles
'body::Resonator' is 'b0 (1 - z^-2)' over the pole pair, run in
direct form I, peak gain near 1; the wind's bandpass already
reuses it. A modal mode is 'beta (r cos(theta) z^-1 - r^2 z^-2)'
over the same pole pair, run from its all-pole state so that its
output depends only on earlier inputs (the junction needs the
arriving wave before it runs) and so that its stored energy can
be read from that state. The forms disagree, so one second-order
type would need a mode switch; only the pole pair's design
('2 r cos(theta)', 'r^2') is common, two lines.
** Two milliseconds-to-samples helpers
'howl::delay_samples' and 'exciter::knock_length_samples' are
both 'round(ms / 1000 * sample_rate)', at least 1.
** Three ceilings below Nyquist
The exciter's cutoff clamps at
'HIGHEST_EXCITATION_CUTOFF_FRACTION_OF_SAMPLE_RATE' (0.45), body
modes, modal modes and the wind's bandpass at
'body::HIGHEST_MODE_FRACTION_OF_SAMPLE_RATE' (0.45), and the
junction's damping lowpass at a literal 0.49. They mean different
things (a lowpass cutoff, a resonance), but nothing says why the
junction's differs.
** The pedal's power is computed twice per voice per sample
The renderer calls 'energy::ex_post_energy_factor' and
'energy::pedal_power' for every voice every sample; both raise
the pedal to 'pedal_energy_exponent' with 'powf', and the pedal
moves only once per block. Once per block per distinct exponent
would do.
** The tests' modal render repeats the plain loop render
The crossover test runs a lone 'ModalVoice' by the same
read-then-write loop as 'render_plain_karplus_strong' does a
'Loop'. A trait for "a junction member" would let one helper do
both; the two types share no trait today.
** The harness and the tests each build their own render loop
'tools/strings/strings_harness.rs' and 'strings/tests.rs' both
render a 'VoiceMap' in 128-frame blocks and both define a
'finger_key'. The harness is a binary and the tests are
'#[cfg(test)]', so sharing needs a public helper in
'strings::scripted_voices'. Their RMS helpers are two as well
('rms' in the harness's tests, 'rms_between' in the strings
tests).

* VoiceState construction and release
** Every 'VoiceState' constructor spells out all 27 fields
'SurfaceSink::note_on_with_phase_and_gain' and four more sites in
'surfaces_runtime/synth.rs', 'sawwave::voices::
spawn_accretion_voice', the fingered voice in 'sawwave/state.rs',
the looper's sink ('buried/midi_pulse/looper_runtime/sink.rs'),
'strings::scripted_voices::struck_voice_state' and about
twenty-five tests each list every field. A constructor for a
struck voice at rest (every gain 1, no glide, slide or pulse, the
default timbre) plus struct-update syntax would serve all of
them; 'struck_voice_state' is already that constructor for the
strings sink's attack and could honestly become the runtime's,
with the attack ramp and the pluck envelope as arguments.
** The release ramp is written out eight times
'target_env = 0; ramp_per_sample = env / (release_secs *
sample_rate)' appears in 'SurfaceSink::note_off',
'sustain_note', 'cut_sustained', 'end_chord_voices',
'end_loop_voices', 'end_drones_at', 'sawwave::voices::
ramp_chord_accretion_to_zero' and
'strings::scripted_voices::release_voice_state'. Only some also
clear 'pending_attack'; see the next headline.
'release_voice_state' could be the one helper.
** Possible bug: a release that keeps a pending attack re-strikes
'note_off' clears 'pending_attack' ("a finger lifting
mid-drag-dip must NOT re-strike"), but 'sustain_note' (its branch
that finds the drone key taken and releases the finger's voice in
place), 'cut_sustained' and 'end_drones_at' do not. A drag sets
a pending attack on the dragged voice, a drone included. A voice
still inside a drag's 2 ms dip when one of those ends it would
reach silence, take its pending attack, and sound again at full
level with nothing left to end it. Unverified; the window is 2
ms.

* Structural problems in the old code
** About twenty free functions in 'synth.rs' walk the voice map
'surfaces_runtime/synth.rs' (2949 lines) has eighteen free
functions taking '&Arc<Mutex<VoiceMap>>', each locking the map
and filtering it by 'VoiceSource' ('set_grid_fader_gain',
'end_drones_at', 'retimbre_voices', ...), beside 'SurfaceSink'
methods that do the same through 'self.voices'. A voice-map type
with methods for "the voices of this grid", "the drones at these
pitches" and the release would replace the repeated lock, match
and loop.
** The cpal glue is copied four times
'surfaces_runtime/audio.rs', 'drumkit_runtime/audio.rs',
'buried/midi_pulse/looper_runtime/audio.rs' and
'buried/midi_pulse/sawwave_runtime.rs' each choose the JACK host,
pick a device and format, and build the stream. 'audio.rs' says
the copies are on purpose (no test reaches cpal), but every fix
to device selection must be made four times.
** 'VoiceState' copies everything for every engine
'VoiceState' is 'Copy' and carries the sawwave engine's
oscillator phase, AM, FM, timbre crossfade and factored pulse
beside the control state both engines read. The strings engine
reads only the envelope, the pitch and the gain factors, and the
grid threads snapshot all of it under the map lock. Splitting the
control state from the oscillator state would let each engine
own its own.
** One engine had to be threaded through five files
Adding the strings engine touched 'audio.rs' (two new
arguments to 'start', a branch in the callback), 'settings.rs',
'reload.rs', 'pedal_volume.rs' (a new argument to
'expression_pedal_loop') and 'mod.rs' (the new atomic, passed to
both). The sub-problems:
*** 'Settings' is flat
The play sink decides fifteen fields of 'Settings', so
'resolve_sink_settings' builds a 'SinkSettings' and
'resolve_settings' copies it back field by field. A nested
'Settings.sink' would be one field.
*** 'LiveParams' mirrors 'Settings' by hand
'reload::live_params' copies twenty-one fields one by one; every
hot-reloadable setting is added in three places ('Settings',
'LiveParams', 'live_params').
*** Loose arguments instead of a shared-state struct
'audio::start' takes nine arguments and 'expression_pedal_loop'
nine, both under 'allow(clippy::too_many_arguments)'; each new
cross-thread word ('pedal_slide_frac', 'string_energy_position')
is one more 'Arc' passed by hand from 'run'.
The pedal positions are now one array, 'pedal_positions', a word
per pedal, where 'string_energy_position' was one word more.
** 'validate_rig' checks a cpal_synth's stream by itself
'rig::validate_sink_stream' now serves the 'cpal_sampler' and
'cpal_strings' sinks; the 'cpal_synth' arm still checks its
sample rate, buffer and amplitude inline, interleaved with its
own keys.
** A pedal role is spelled twice
'PedalRole' takes its rig spelling from
'serde(rename_all = "snake_case")', and 'PedalRole::rig_spelling'
(plan 04) spells every role again by hand for messages. A new
role must be added to both, and nothing checks that they agree;
a test deserializing each 'rig_spelling' back to its role would.

* Names in the strings engine that deserve a decision
** "String voice" means two things
The plan's vocabulary defines a string voice as the engine's
per-voice state; in the code 'StringVoice' is the loops kind
only, while the renderer's map 'string_voices' (and its local
variables 'string_voice') holds 'VoiceKind's, modal ones too.
'StringsRenderer::string_voice' returns only a loops voice,
'voice_kind' either.
** 'StringsParams' and 'StringParams' differ by one letter
The first is every strings parameter, the second its 'string'
table.
** "Loop" already names a recorded loop
'strings::loop_dsp::Loop' is one delay-line loop, but the
surfaces runtime's compact loops, 'VoiceSource::SurfaceLoop',
'end_loop_voices', the looper and 'TimbreTarget::Loop' use
"loop" for a recorded musical loop. The strings tests say "a loop
voice" for a voice of loops, which in 'synth.rs' is a voice that
a compact loop plays.
** "Energy" has three meanings
The energy pedal's energy ('EnergyParams', 'energy_factor', the
'energy' table), the stored energy of a loop (sum of squares,
'Loop::energy', 'VoiceKind::energy'), and 'pick_energy', which is
a peak amplitude.
** "Power" is the pedal's exponent, not a signal's power
'energy::pedal_power' is 'pedal ^ pedal_energy_exponent'; the
tests also say "heard power" for a mean square.
** "Dry" names two things
'exciter::dry_table' is a note's own table as against the knock;
the body's 'mix' blends the "dry" sum with the bank's "wet" one.
** 'wind_speed_hz_max' is a frequency
It is the Strouhal frequency at full pedal, in Hz; "speed" names
the wind it stands for.
** 'struck_voice_state' is any exciter kind's
"Struck" also names 'ExciterKind::Strike'; the helper builds the
voice for a pluck too.
** "Junction" and "bridge" are one thing
The type is 'Junction' and the rig table 'bridge'
('bridge.heaviness', 'bridge_velocity'); the plan fixed both
words.
** "Member" is not in the plan's vocabulary
"Junction member" (a loop, or a whole modal voice) entered with
the modal voice; it has one meaning throughout the code.
** "Pedal target" and 'PedalTarget' are two things
Plan 04's pedal target is a (parameter, min, max) triple
('PedalTargetRig', 'StringsPedalTarget', 'pedal_targets'); the
older 'settings::PedalTarget' is what a pedal's role drives (a
grid, every grid, the energy). 'LiveParams' holds both, as
'expression_pedals' and 'pedal_targets', and
'PedalTarget::StringParameters' binds the pedal whose only job
is its pedal targets. The plan kept the old name; a rename of
'PedalTarget' (to 'PedalBinding', say) would end the clash.

* Open listening questions
** Sympathy is gentle at the default heaviness 3000
A lighter bridge is stronger sympathy and shorter notes
together; at 3000 the bridge alone gives a 110 Hz note a T60 of
about 42 s and a 1760 Hz note 2.6 s. Whether 3000 is the right
default is for Jeff's ears.
** The pre-charge sits about 18 dB under the strike
At 'precharge_gain' 0.5 a full pedal's pre-charge is about 18 dB
below the default strike; about 4 would match it.
** A modal note drains half as fast through the bridge
A modal note is one junction member where a two-unison loop note
is two, so near the crossover it rings about 20 percent longer
(a T60 of about 1.1 s against 0.9 s at 2000 Hz and heaviness
3000). Counting a modal voice as 'unison_count' members would
match them; the plan says one member.
** A modal note is brighter than a loop note
Over its first 0.2 s a modal note's RMS is 1.4 to 1.7 times a
loop note's at the same pitch, because the upper modes' decay
stops falling at 'high_frequency_hz' while a loop's one-pole loss
keeps darkening. Extending the line past the reference measured
too dark.
** The wind's pre-charge sits 26 cents under its partial
The plan puts the pre-charge sine at 'k f0'; the stiff string's
partial sits higher (2010 against 1980 Hz for the 9th of 220 Hz).
** A bowed chord runs near the clamp
Five pre-charged notes under a full pedal peak near 0.85; a
bigger chord can reach 0.95.
** The bow's squeaks are measured only as the strongest partial
From a 'bow_velocity * bow_slipperiness' of about 1.5 the 55 and
110 Hz notes have their 4th partial strongest; whether that
sounds like a squeak has not been heard.
** The howl's level and its dead spot
'howl.level' defaults to 0.003 (0.3 pinned every register at the
clamp). At the default 5 ms delay an 880 Hz note's fundamental is
out of phase with the howl and sustains only on another partial.
** The body boosts the partials on its modes
The bank is normalized to keep white noise's RMS, so its wet
output is fourteen times the raw bank (norm 0.0696), and a voice
at 'mix' 0.5 peaks about 2.4 times higher than at 'mix' 0.
** The DC blocker leaves the upper partials 1.4 cents flat
At 'f0 / 200'; 'f0 / 500' would give 0.55 cents but hold an
in-loop DC offset longer. A rig knob is cheap.
** Nothing has been listened to
Every scene and test of plan 02 was checked numerically only.
