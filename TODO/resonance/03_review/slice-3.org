** Suspicious: the default bridge drains notes ten times too fast
With 'bridge.heaviness' 50, each note dies far faster than the
T60 curve says. Single voices, body off, -40 dB reached at:
| f0 Hz | T60 curve s | r = 50 | r = 500 | r = 5000 | r = 50000 |
|   110 |        6.84 |   0.43 |    3.05 |     3.92 |      4.09 |
|   220 |        4.68 |   0.23 |    1.79 |     2.37 |      2.48 |
|   440 |        3.20 |   0.12 |    0.98 |     1.32 |      1.39 |
|   880 |        2.19 |   0.07 |    0.45 |     0.63 |      0.68 |
|  1760 |        1.50 |   0.04 |    0.16 |     0.32 |      0.36 |
(-40 dB is two thirds of a T60; at r = 50000 the times match the
curve's two thirds within the strike's faster-dying highs.) This
is the junction as specified, not a bug: the in-phase mode of a
voice's loops loses the factor '(r - N) / (r + N)' per round trip
(the discussion's eigenvalue), 0.923 for two unisons at r = 50,
which is 38 dB per second at 55 Hz and grows with f0. The loss
filter alone meets the curve (slice 2's test). The harness's
envelopes show it: the 110 Hz note of 'single_notes' falls about
40 dB in its first half second. A choice for the orchestrator or
Jeff: a default heaviness in the thousands, or a loss filter that
compensates a lone voice's bridge loss. I changed neither.

** Junction stability, with dispersion: the plan's measure fails
With the default 4 dispersion sections the block-to-block rise is
up to +11.8 percent (r = 1), +9.1 (r = 50), +11.1 (r = 500). A
lone loop with no junction shows it worse: +43 to +111 percent
between 55 and 1320 Hz. The allpass chain holds about 15 samples
of the wave per section in its states at a low note, which the
delay lines' sum of squares does not count; a strike's pulses dip
out of the measure and back. So the test asserts block-to-block
non-increase with dispersion off, and with dispersion on that the
mean over successive quarter seconds never rises (measured: it
never does).
** Sympathy: the 1e-6 bound at r = 5000 is unreachable
Struck 220 Hz voice and a silent 220 Hz voice (empty tables),
0.5 s. At r = 20: struck 0.258, silent 0.240. At r = 5000:
struck 0.877, silent 4.58e-3. Identical pitches resonate exactly,
so a heavy bridge still hands the silent string about '2 / (N +
r)' of the struck amplitude each round trip, coherently; below
1e-6 would need r near 3.4e5. The test asserts: at r = 20 the
silent energy is above 1e-6 and above a tenth of the struck
one's; at r = 5000 it is below a fiftieth of the struck one's
(measured 0.0052) and below a tenth of the r = 20 silent
energy (measured 0.019).

* Deviations from the plan's "how", with reasons
** A reaped id is not respawned while it stays in the map
The brief said to respawn a reaped id, silent, the next block.
That brings its delay lines back a block later and reaps them a
second after: a hundred silent drones would again cost a hundred
delay lines, which reaping exists to prevent. The renderer keeps
the reaped ids (pruned when they leave the map) and does not
respawn them. A re-strike makes a new id, which spawns struck. If
the intent was to keep silent drones as sympathetic junction
members, reaping and that intent conflict; say which wins.
** The stability and sympathy tests, as measured above
** The re-strike scene cuts the drone as the runtime does
The brief said to remove the finger key and insert a new voice
at 2 s. The scene instead sustains the chord at 0.5 s (finger key
to drone key, same id), and at 2 s moves the fifth's drone to a
retired key releasing (as 'SurfaceSink::cut_sustained') and
strikes a new voice, sustained again at 2.5 s. Removing the old
voice outright would cut its string mid-ring, a click the runtime
never makes.
** The sympathy scene's loud note is a fresh strike
There is no per-note excitation level (velocity is out of scope),
so 'quiet' means struck at 0 s and decayed by 3 s, when the 110
Hz note is struck.
** A changed unison count applies to notes born afterward
A sounding voice keeps its loop count; its detunes and
coefficients are retuned on a parameter change.
** A changed sample rate restarts the engine
'render_block' given another sample rate rebuilds the renderer;
every voice in the map is then struck again. The runtime never
changes it.
** The body bank's resonators restart from rest on a body change
** 'StringsRenderer.slide_slew_secs' mirrors 'BlockRenderer'
A public field, default 'SLIDE_SLEW_SECS', for slice 4 to set
from the rig.
** The scripted-voice helper is 'strings::scripted_voices'
'struck_voice_state(id, freq, sample_rate)' and
'release_voice_state(voice, sample_rate)', shared by the tests
and the harness.
** The per-sample junction pass is one public function
'renderer::couple_through_junction' runs the read, junction and
write-back over a 'BTreeMap' of string voices; the tests' direct
loop-and-junction harness calls the same function. String voices
live in a 'BTreeMap' by id so the junction sums in the same order
on every run.
