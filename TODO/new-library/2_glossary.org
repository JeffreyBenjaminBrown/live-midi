#+title: Glossary for the new library
#+date: 2026-07-17

Every term the new library is allowed to use, with the one meaning it is allowed to
have. A word that is not here, or that is used here for two things, is a bug waiting to
be filed.

The rule this came from: a name that needs a paragraph of explanation is a defect, and
the fix is the name rather than the paragraph.

* the words that were doing too much

** monomekey
One button on a monome grid, addressed as =(x, y)=. This is the only sense of "key"
the library uses. A hash map does not have "keys" in this codebase's prose; it has
monomekeys nowhere near it, and its lookups are described by the type they take.

The old code called a button a =MonomeKey= and also called =voice_key()= a key and also
spoke of the =pitch= "key" of a drone. Three meanings, one word.

** chord
A stored set of pitches that a chord button emits. It is a real feature, and it lives
in the sawwave runtime (=AppState.chords=, =chord_slot_buttons=, =chord_wipe_button=).

This rig has no chords. The word must not appear (yet, at least) in the new library at all. Where the
old code wrote =chord=, it meant "which grid, and which kind of voice", and the new
=Voice= enum says that in its variants instead.

** accrete
To add a played voice to the sustained set while a pedal is held. The word is
accurate: the set grows by accumulation, one voice at a time, as you play. It stays.

Accrete is the verb; sustain is the state it produces.

* the instrument

** grid
One 16x16 monome, the whole of it a 46-EDO play surface. This rig has two, and each
carries its own voices, its own scroll register, and its own edit state.

** LOM, RNM
The left/older monome and the right/newer monome. LOM is the monobright Series 256
(serial =m256-282=), and it cannot dim a single LED, so it fakes dim by flashing fast.
RNM is varibright (serial =m0000102=). The rig pins each by serial, so which physical
grid is which never depends on plug order.

** OSS, NSS
The old SoftStep (ALSA client =SSCOM=) and the new one (=SoftStep=). OSS carries
sustain and the tap tempo. NSS carries the factored pulse, and it sits rotated 180
degrees, which the code never learns: it binds printed pedal labels, and the rotation
only explains why the map reads mirrored.

** register
A per-grid offset added to every monomekey's step, so the same monomekey sounds a
different pitch after a scroll. The shift pad moves it.

** pitch
An absolute step in the tuning, octave included. Distinct from a pitch class.

** pitch class
A pitch modulo the EDO. The LEDs mostly work in pitch classes, because a sounding
voice lights all its octave-equivalents. The diamond dance is the one exception.

* sounding

** voice
One thing the synth is sounding. It owns its own frequency, envelope, timbre, gain,
glide and factored pulse, so moving or retuning one never touches another.

Jeff: "I'll try to always say voice rather than note, because note is confusible for
pitch. The code should do likewise." So "note" is not a word this library uses. It
survives in one honest place, which is a MIDI note-on or note-off message.

** VoiceId
A voice's identity, for its whole life. It is opaque, it is never reused, and it
carries no information.

That last part is the point. A value that means something is a value that can stop
being true. A voice's pitch changes when an edit drags it, and two voices are allowed
to sound one pitch, so pitch can neither identify a voice nor distinguish two. A
monomekey cannot either, because the register scrolls and a drag re-homes a voice.

** home
The monomekey a voice lives at: where its finger is, or where the finger that last held
it was. Pressing there retriggers that voice rather than starting a second one, and a
drag moves it.

A home is an address rather than an identity. Two voices never share one, but a voice
changes home when it is dragged.

** fingered voice
A voice with a finger on its home.

** sustained voice
A voice in the sustained set, which keeps sounding with no finger on it.

It is not identified by its pitch. Two sustained voices may sound the same pitch, and
Jeff has ruled that legal: "Now we have two sustained voices at the same pitch. Still
fine. They'll phase; whatever." The old library could not represent that, and silently
discarded one of the two.

The word "drone" used to mean this and named the pitch-addressing that made it wrong.
The word is retired.

** fading voice
A voice ramping to silence over its release. Nothing can address one.

** sounding
A voice is sounding when the player can hear it. A voice sounds while a finger holds
it, or while it is sustained, and for no other reason.

Edit is not a third reason. Jeff: "edit really isn't a third reason for being for a
voice. It is a property that can be true of any voice."

** sustained set
The voices on one grid that keep sounding with no finger on them. It holds VoiceIds
rather than pitches.

Two gestures write it: the accrete pedals, and the sustain monomekey. A clear-sustain
empties it, which is why a clear reaches both, and it leaves fingered voices alone.

** sustain monomekey
The monomekey directly above a sounding voice, which toggles that voice's sustain. It
is the mirror of the edit monomekey directly below.

Where several voices sound one pitch, the gesture acts on all of them, because nothing
in it distinguishes them. Jeff settled the mixed case: if any voice there is already
sustained, the press ends the sustained ones and does not sustain the fingered ones,
"if I wanted the fingered note to replace the sustained note, I could have just
retriggered."

** edit monomekey
The monomekey directly below a sounding voice, which toggles that voice's edit
property.

Both gestures are geometric rather than pitch-defined, so they do not move when the
tuning changes. Jeff's reason: "my other fingers can do either side but my thumb can
only reach from below." A voice on the top row has no sustain monomekey, and one on the
bottom row has no edit monomekey.

* the factored pulse

** factored pulse
A unipolar-triangle amplitude modulation applied to a voice, at a rate derived from the
tapped tempo. It is called the factored pulse rather than the pulse to avoid clobbering
PWM, or some other future use of a word that general.

It is also not "slow AM". That name already belongs to the timbre tremolo, which is a
different mechanism, and the two can be on one voice at once -- which is exactly when
telling them apart matters.

** tapped tempo
The one global tempo, defined by tapping OSS pedal 8 twice. Defining it starts nothing.

** tempo factor
A per-grid rational multiplier on the tapped tempo, held as exponents =2^a * 3^b=, so
x3 followed by /3 is exactly unity rather than nearly. It is called the tempo factor,
not the factor, because "factor" is general enough to clobber something in future.

** applied tempo
The tapped tempo times a grid's tempo factor. A voice's factored pulse runs at the
applied tempo its grid had when the voice was struck, unless an edit retunes it.

* editing

** edit mode
A property of a voice: the player has selected it for editing. It is a property rather
than a reason to sound, so it cannot outlive the voice it is a property of.

That is what makes a whole bug class unbuildable. Edit used to keep a voice alive, so a
clear could silence the voice and leave its pitch marked as edited, dancing around
nothing, with no gesture able to reach it.

** deferred release
What edit mode does to a fingered voice whose finger lifts. Jeff: "A fingered voice in
edit mode should not end on key-up... When edit mode is released, the fingered voice
resumes normal release behavior. But if my finger isn't on it when I take it out of
edit mode, it ends with the end of the edit mode."

So the voice is still the finger's, and the finger has merely let go for now. Pressing
its home brings the finger back and retriggers it.

** the diamond dance
One lit cell turning around each edited voice, marking it. It is the only LED rule in
the instrument that is local: it marks the exact monomekeys sounding that voice's pitch
on that one grid, and it never marks octave-equivalents or the other grid.

** drag
Moving an edited voice's pitch by pressing where you want it. The voice glides rather
than jumping, and it takes the pressed monomekey as its new home, so the finger that
dragged it now holds it.

** clear-sustain
The pedal that empties one grid's sustained set. It leaves fingered voices alone.

** clear-edit
The pedal that drops the edit property from every voice on one grid. It silences
nothing that a finger or a sustain holds, and it ends the voices that only a deferred
release was carrying.
