#+title: Sustained-pitch refinger volume spike

* Reproduction boundary

With =monomes_two-timbres_sustain_chords= at its shipped settings, accrete a
note, release its play cell so that the pitch is sounding as a drone, then press
a play cell for the same absolute pitch.  The replacement attack is much louder
than an ordinary attack at the same tone-target volume.  Chords and loops need not be
active, the second monome is irrelevant, and the fault is not tied to an
enharmonic second cell: pressing the original cell reproduces it.

“Pitch belongs to the sustained set” is slightly broader than the failing
condition.  The hot path requires an actual fingerless =SurfaceDrone= at that
pitch.  If the pitch has sustain membership but its original finger is still
down, there is no drone to cut and another equal-pitch cell takes the ordinary
tone-target-aware note-on path.  The defect disappears when the fingered/sustained
tone target is exactly 0 dB, and grows as that tone target is attenuated; both facts follow
from the gain mismatch below.

* Cause

The play-cell handler in
[[file:edo_surface/src/surfaces_runtime/keys.rs][=surfaces_runtime/keys.rs=]]
computes the current fingered/sustained tone target's timbre slot and gain, then calls
=SurfaceSink::cut_sustained(pitch)=.  In
[[file:edo_surface/src/surfaces_runtime/synth.rs][=surfaces_runtime/synth.rs=]],
=cut_sustained= removes =SurfaceDrone { grid, pitch }= from its addressable key,
rekeys it as a unique =SurfaceRetired= voice, starts its normal release ramp,
and returns its oscillator and factored-pulse state.  This is intentional: the
old sound reaches silence without a discontinuity, the sustain key is free for
the replacement, and the replacement can continue the old oscillator cycle.

Before the repair, the mismatch occurred at the next two calls:

1. =keys.rs= called =note_on_continuing(cell, pitch, timbre, cut)=.  That generic
   helper called =note_on_with_phase=, which initialized both =grid_gain= and
   =grid_gain_target= from =pedal_gain()=.  This rig has no expression pedal, so
   the value is unity.
2. After the voice has been inserted, =keys.rs= called
   =set_tone_target_gain(..., gain)= with the correct fingered/sustained tone-target gain.
   =set_tone_target_gain= deliberately changes only =grid_gain_target=; the applied
   =grid_gain= remains at unity and approaches the destination through the renderer's
   20 ms gain slew.

Fresh notes did not do this. With no drone cut, the same handler called
=note_on_with_tone_gain(..., gain)=, which reaches =note_on_with_phase_and_gain=
and initializes both gain fields to the tone-target value. Momentary chord and
compact-loop attacks likewise started both fields at their respective tone-target
gains. The bug was therefore one missed tone-target-aware constructor on the
sustained-retrigger branch, not a general fault in tone-control arithmetic or rendering.

The voice's amplitude coefficient is multiplied in
[[file:edo_surface/src/sawwave/voices.rs][=sawwave/voices.rs=]] as

#+begin_example
envelope * timbre gain * fader gain * grid gain * modulation * master amplitude
#+end_example

so the erroneous unity value directly multiplies the new attack; no later stage
normalizes it back to the selected tone target.

* Magnitude with the shipped rig

[[file:rigs/monomes_two-timbres_sustain_chords.org][The rig]] starts every tone target
at -12 dB, hence the intended linear gain is

#+begin_example
g = 10^(-12/20) = 0.2511886.
#+end_example

The replacement starts at 1.0 and the renderer applies a 20 ms one-pole slew
toward =g=.  Its attack reaches the peak after 3 ms.  At that point the applied
gain is approximately

#+begin_example
g + (1 - g) exp(-3/20) = 0.8956,
#+end_example

which is 3.566 times, or 11.04 dB, above the correct replacement attack.  The
old drone is simultaneously only 3 ms into its 50 ms release.  If it had
settled at the configured 0.35 sustain envelope, its remaining envelope is
about 0.329; near the onset, where the two continued oscillator phases are
still close, their possible summed coefficient is about 0.978 rather than the
0.334 produced by a correctly gained replacement plus the same tail.  That is
another way to describe the heard transient: the intended overlap is present,
but one member is roughly 11 dB too hot.

The global crowded-pitch allocator added later does not cause the fault.  With
the shipped 5-cent spacing, the incumbent retired tail keeps the zero lane and
the replacement receives the +5-cent lane.  The small frequency difference
decorrelates them only over time; it cannot correct the replacement's gain and
they begin with the continued phase nearly aligned.  Setting the spacing to
zero would leave the gain error intact.  Conversely, deleting the release tail
would still leave the new voice near unity.  The older
[[file:TODO/monomes_two-timbres_sustain_chords/4_DUCKED_voice-model.org][ducked voice-model note]]
correctly identified the gain bug, but its description of a specially
six-cent-detuned old tail is obsolete: the current rig leaves
=retrigger_tail_detune_cents= at its zero default and obtains separation from
global crowded-pitch allocation instead.

* Repair

The repair adds =SurfaceSink::note_on_continuing_with_gain= without changing
the generic =note_on_continuing= contract. It invokes
=note_on_with_phase_and_gain= with the supplied tone-target gain, then restores
the oscillator-adjacent factored-pulse state carried by =CutVoice=:

#+begin_src rust
pub fn note_on_continuing_with_gain(
  &mut self,
  cell: (i32, i32),
  pitch: i32,
  timbre: Timbre,
  gain: f32,
  cut: CutVoice,
) {
  self.note_on_with_phase_and_gain(cell, pitch, timbre, None, cut.phase, gain);
  self.restore_cut_pulse(cell, cut);
}
#+end_src

The =Some(cut)= branch in =keys.rs= now calls that variant when tone-target
state supplies a gain and retains the generic variant for rigs whose gain
component is owned by an expression pedal. The replacement is therefore
inserted atomically with =grid_gain == grid_gain_target == gain=; the audio
thread has no interval in which it can observe unity.

The existing =set_tone_target_gain= call after note creation remains. It is
redundant for the newly inserted replacement, but still enforces the selected
tone-target gain on every other live finger/drone and preserves the slide/mono
paths. It still assigns only =grid_gain_target=: live volume-button changes need
the 20 ms slew, whereas note onset needs correct initialization of both fields.
The retired tail, oscillator phase, pulse state, and crowded-pitch ownership
remain unchanged because they are orthogonal and deliberate.

* Regression coverage

The direct integration regression is
[[file:edo_surface/src/surfaces_runtime/tests.rs][=surfaces_runtime/tests.rs=]]:
=crowded_pitch_retrigger_keeps_the_incumbent_lane_and_offsets_the_new_finger= runs
the real rig's key path through strike, accrete, release-to-drone, and same-cell
refinger. It now computes =db_to_gain(-12.0)= and, immediately after the
refinger, asserts both

#+begin_example
finger.grid_gain        == expected_gain
finger.grid_gain_target == expected_gain
#+end_example

within the existing floating-point tolerance. The old code failed the first
assertion with =1.0= and passed the second with about =0.2511886=. The test
therefore pins the fault without waveform samples or wall-clock audio while its
existing assertions independently pin the retired tail's zero lane and the
replacement's +5-cent lane.

The synth-level =tone_target_retrigger_starts_at_its_attenuated_gain= test uses
a non-unity gain and asserts that oscillator phase and pulse rate/phase still
continue while both gain fields begin at the supplied value. The renderer
already has separate gain-slew tests; these state-boundary assertions are more
diagnostic than an audio-peak test.

After Cargo recovered from repeated crates.io connection resets and downloaded
the missing dependencies, =cargo test -p edo_surface --no-run= compiled the
library and test executable successfully. Separate focused runs passed the
integration regression, the synth-level regression, and the renamed rig's
broader tone-target integration test. The full =cargo test -p edo_surface= run
reported 665 passed and 11 failed; the failures are in the pre-existing
46-EDO/pedal-slide fixture and follow the unrelated dirty-worktree edit that
changes =rigs/2-edogrids_ss-accrete_ss-pulse.org= from 46/9/80 to 41/8/110.
The clearest failure says “same 46-EDO tuning” while observing 41 versus 46.

* Glossary

** Audio voice

One independently owned oscillator plus its envelope, gain, timbre, and
lifetime state.  Several voices may have one nominal pitch.

** Drone

The fingerless audio voice that realizes one pitch in the sustained set.
Sustain membership alone is not a drone.

** Tone

The combined timbre and volume setting. This is deliberately broader than the
ordinary use of “tone” as timbre alone.

** Tone target

One control group whose voices receive the same timbre and volume edits. This
rig has fingered/sustained, chord, and loop tone targets. A tone target groups
voices; it is not the source identity of an individual voice.

** =ToneTarget=

The enum in
[[file:edo_surface/src/surfaces_runtime/tone_controls.rs][=tone_controls.rs=]]
encoding the three tone targets. Its =matches_source= method maps
=SurfaceFinger=, =SurfaceDrone=, and =SurfaceRetired= to
=FingeredSustained=; =SurfaceChord= to =Chord=; and =SurfaceLoop= to =Loop=,
always for one specified grid. The comment beside its definition explicitly
defines tone as including timbre and volume.

** =ToneControls=

The per-grid state holding the current =ToneTarget= plus each tone target's
selected timbre slot and volume state.

** =VoiceSource=

The enum used as the key of the shared voice map. A value says which musical
source owns and can address a voice. The tone-target classifier uses its five
=Surface*= variants.

** =SurfaceFinger=

The source-key variant for a voice owned by a physically held play cell.  Its
fields identify the grid and cell.

** =SurfaceDrone=

The source-key variant for the one addressable fingerless sustain voice at a
particular grid and pitch.  In source code and this report its value is often
written =SurfaceDrone { grid, pitch }=.  =SurfaceDrone= is a =VoiceSource=
variant, not a separate Rust type.

** =SurfaceRetired=

The source-key variant for a displaced drone completing its release.  Its
sequence field makes each retired key unique, so a replacement can immediately
take ownership of the pitch's =SurfaceDrone= key.  =SurfaceRetired= is a
=VoiceSource= variant, not a separate Rust type.

** =SurfaceChord=

The =VoiceSource= variant for one recalled chord voice, keyed by grid and a
unique recall sequence number so equal-pitch chord voices remain independent.

** =SurfaceLoop=

The =VoiceSource= variant for one compact-loop playback occurrence, keyed by
grid and a unique scheduled-attack sequence number.

** Source key

The =VoiceSource= value used to find and control a voice in the shared voice
map.

** Rekey

Move the same =VoiceState= from one source key to another without restarting
its sound.  Finger-to-drone and drone-to-retired transitions use this.

** Retired voice / release tail

A drone displaced by a refinger, no longer addressable as sustain but still
fading to silence under a unique =SurfaceRetired= key.

** =VoiceState=

The struct holding one audio voice's live synthesis state: identity, frequency,
oscillator and modulation phases, envelope, glide, gains, timbre, and related
targets.  The bug is the inconsistent initialization of two fields in a new
=VoiceState=: =grid_gain= and =grid_gain_target=.

** =SurfaceSink=

The surfaces runtime's per-grid voice driver. It creates, rekeys, retunes, and
releases entries in the audio thread's shared voice map. The generic and
tone-target-aware constructors are methods on this struct.

** =CutVoice=

The small struct returned by =SurfaceSink::cut_sustained=.  It carries the
outgoing drone's oscillator phase and factored-pulse rate and phase into the
replacement voice; it does not carry gain.

** =Timbre=

The struct describing a voice's waveform, timbre-local gain, and AM/FM
settings. It is distinct from the tone-target gain whose bad initialization
caused this bug.

** =i32= and =f32=

Rust's signed 32-bit integer and 32-bit floating-point types. The added method
signature uses =i32= for grid coordinates and pitches and =f32= for
gain.  They are language primitives, unlike the project-domain types above.

** Envelope

The time-varying multiplier that supplies a note's attack, pluck decay,
sustained level, and release.

** Attack

The envelope's rise from silence to its strike peak; configured as 3 ms in this
rig.

** Current / applied gain

=VoiceState::grid_gain=, the gain the renderer multiplies into the present
sample.

** Destination gain

=VoiceState::grid_gain_target=, the destination toward which the applied gain
moves.

** Slew / one-pole slew

A smooth exponential approach from a current value to a destination, used here to
prevent abrupt volume-control steps.  Its 20 ms value is a time constant, not a
fixed completion time.

** Unity gain

Linear gain 1.0, or 0 dB: neither amplification nor attenuation.

** Oscillator phase

Position within the repeating waveform cycle.  Continuing it avoids a
discontinuous waveform restart on refinger.

** Factored pulse

Older rigs' rhythmic amplitude modulation carried by the shared retrigger
helper.  This rig does not use it, but the fix must preserve the generic
helper's continuation semantics.

** Crowded-pitch lane

A stable small detuning assigned to one of several simultaneous voices at the
same nominal pitch; the current sequence is 0, +5, -5, +10, -10 cents, and so
on.

** Coherent addition

Two similar waveforms with nearly aligned phases add their instantaneous
amplitudes rather than merely their average powers; this makes the brief
two-voice overlap more conspicuous.

** Regression test

A test that fails for this specific old behavior and passes after the repair,
preventing the bug from returning unnoticed.
