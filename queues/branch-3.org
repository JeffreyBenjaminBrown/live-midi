** TODO ideas on why fast =1 double-presses fail
I looked and there is NO time-based debouncing of monome key signals anywhere in
edo_surface (grepped "debounce"/"dedup" across surfaces_runtime/ and monome.rs).
The only `DebounceMode` in the codebase (`drumkit_runtime/decode.rs`) is wired
exclusively to the KMSS's pressure-sensor pads (`rig.rs` ~1296-1363), never a
monome. The monome key path (`surfaces_runtime/mod.rs::grid_thread`, ~684-736) has
exactly one piece of state between an OSC `/grid/key` message and `handle_key`: a
`pressed: HashSet<(i32,i32)>` that calls `handle_key` only when a down/up actually
changes membership (mod.rs:687,734) -- a same-state dedup (a stuck/echoing device's
duplicate down without a between up gets dropped), not a time debounce, so per the
instructions above I left it alone. Concrete guesses instead, each pointing at code:

*** The pressed-set (mod.rs:687,734) can swallow a genuine second press if the
  release between the two downs is lost or arrives out of order (UDP has no
  ordering guarantee): `pressed.insert(cell)` on the second `s=1` sees the cell
  already marked pressed and drops it silently, so `handle_key`/=1 never fires.
  This path is monome-only -- the KMSS's presses go through
  `drumkit_runtime`'s own `TetherDecoder` and `hooks::rig_pedal_hook`, a
  completely different route that doesn't share this HashSet.

*** The =1 double-tap detector only arms once cycling is already ON
  (`surfaces_runtime/polyrhythm.rs::press`, ~135-166: `last_unity = None` at line
  150, `UNITY_DOUBLE_TAP` = 400ms at line 52). From a COLD (off) button, two fast
  presses land as "on" then "snap to unity", NOT "off" -- a third fast press is
  needed (see test `the_double_tap_window_applies_only_while_cycling_is_on`). A
  fast double-press from cold can look exactly like "the second press did
  nothing" since the LED just stays lit. Note this logic is shared verbatim with
  the KMSS pedal (both call `hooks::factored_pulse_press` -> `poly.press`), so
  alone it can't explain a monome-only difference, but may still be what's
  actually happening either way.

*** The grid thread reads at most one OSC packet per loop iteration, then always
  repaints (multiple mutex locks + LED sends, plus a monobright grid's DIM_PULSE
  fake-dim flashing over the same socket) before reading again
  (`surfaces_runtime/mod.rs::grid_thread`, recv at ~684-694, repaint at
  ~745-860+). Shouldn't cost anywhere near 400ms, but it is the one
  monome-specific source of jitter between "packet received" and "loop reads
  again" -- worth timestamping real /grid/key events to rule out before
  dismissing.

*** serialosc or the grid's own firmware could coalesce two very fast presses of
  one button into a single down/up pair before `edo_surface` ever sees a second
  OSC message -- entirely outside this code, and the asymmetry the online-research
  sidequest above is meant to chase.

I'd start with (1): log every /grid/key OSC event (x, y, down/up, timestamp) for
the =1 cell and see whether a fast double-press actually produces two clean
down/up/down edges, or whether the second down is missing.
** TODO why the monome doesn't need debouncing but the KMSS does
Not "better firmware" vs "worse firmware" -- the two devices hand us different kinds
of signal in the first place.

*** Monome: grid buttons are discrete conductive-rubber-dome switches, scanned AND
debounced by the grid's own firmware. The one monome firmware ever open-sourced
(monome/mk, the classic kit) shows this concretely: =button.c='s =buttonCheck()=
keeps a per-button debounce counter (24 scan cycles before accepting a release; a
press is accepted on the very next scan) driven by a hardware timer in =mk.c=.
Grepping "debounc" across all of =libmonome= and =serialosc= -- the entire host
driver chain between the USB device and our OSC socket -- finds nothing: there is
no host-side debounce step being skipped, because by the time =/grid/key= reaches
us the bounce is already gone.
*** KMSS: tether mode is not switch closures -- each pad is 4 continuous piezoresistive
pressure sensors (Kesumo/KMI patent US9546921B2) streamed as raw, unfiltered 0..127
CC values every ~10ms. KMI's own hysteresis (onThresh/offThresh) lives in the
PC-side Editor app, which we bypass to read the tether stream directly for velocity
+ concurrency. A stomp's pressure sum wobbles across our onset threshold as the foot
rocks, so =drumkit_runtime::decode::TetherDecoder='s =DebounceMode= (rig.rs
~1296-1363) is doing, in software, the job the Editor would otherwise have done.

Couldn't confirm the CURRENT grid generation's firmware source (monome/iii ships
binary releases only); the debounce evidence above is from monome's classic open
kit firmware, but the architecture -- debounce on-device, dumb relay on the host --
matches monome's own docs/forum mentions of reflashing debounce behavior for grids.
