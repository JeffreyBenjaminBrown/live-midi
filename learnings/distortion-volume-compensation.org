#+title: Distortion volume compensation: why it falls, and what to do
#+author: notes for Jeff, written by Claude
#+date: 2026-07-10

Companion to [[./distortion.org]] (the curve family). That doc says, in passing, that a
makeup gain is "optional"; =jeff-maybe-review-this.org= records the as-built decision
as *"No makeup gain is applied."* This doc is about filling that hole.

Your instinct is right on both counts: a *constant* makeup gain cannot work, and the
correction genuinely has to depend on how many notes are sounding. But you are *not*
asking for something impossible. There is an exact answer, it is cheap, and this
codebase happens to already compute the one quantity it needs.

* the short answer

The soft-clipper's loudness loss depends on one number -- the *RMS level of the summed
dirty bus*, call it =sigma= -- and on nothing else. Not on the note count directly, not
on which pitches: just =sigma=. So the compensation is not "divide by N" or anything
fragile; it is a single function of =sigma=.

And =sigma= does not have to be measured with an envelope follower (which would pump and
lag). The renderer already walks every voice every sample and knows each voice's exact
instantaneous amplitude. Summing their squares gives =sigma= *exactly*, for free, one
sample ahead of the distortion that needs it.

Feed that =sigma= through the clipper's known RMS gain =g(sigma/s)= and multiply the
distorted bus by =1/g=. The result: *the distorted bus has precisely the RMS the clean
bus would have had, at every instant.* Distortion becomes a purely timbral effect --
turning it on changes the harmonics and not the level, and playing more notes changes
the level exactly as much as it does with distortion off, which is what you are already
used to.

That is option *E* below, and it is what is now built (see *as built*): ~120 lines, on by
default, hot-reloadable, and unit-tested without a sound card.

   master volume and the drive knob are the same knob.* See *reparametrizing the knob*.

* the math

** why the loudness falls

The dirty bus is a sum of =N= voices at mutually incommensurate frequencies (58-EDO
clusters are about as incommensurate as it gets). Their relative phases drift, so they
sum *incoherently*: powers add, not amplitudes. If each voice has RMS =a/sqrt(3)=
(more on the =sqrt(3)= below), the sum has

: sigma = sqrt( sum_i a_i^2 / 3 )

which grows like =sqrt(N)=, not =N=. Meanwhile the clipper is bounded: as its input
grows, the output tends to a square wave of amplitude =s=, whose RMS is just =s=. So:

- *clean* output RMS grows without limit, as =sqrt(N)=;
- *distorted* output RMS *saturates at =s=*, whatever =N= is.

Hence the loss grows with =N=, and hence no single constant can undo it. That is the
precise form of the thing you guessed.

(A pleasant corollary, worth naming because it is counterintuitive: with distortion on,
the level is *already* far more stable against =N= than the clean path is -- it is
pinned near =s=. Your "volume fluctuates wildly with note count" fear is a property of
the *clean* signal. The distorted signal's problem is only that it is pinned *too low*.)

** the exact loss, in closed form

For =N= of any size the sum of incoherent voices is Gaussian (central limit), std
=sigma=. Define the drive ratio =r = sigma / s=. We want the clipper's RMS gain

: g(r) = RMS[ f(y) ] / RMS[ y ]        for y ~ Normal(0, sigma^2)

For the default shape *k = 2* -- =f(y) = y / sqrt(1 + (y/s)^2)= -- this integrates in
closed form. Substituting =y = sigma*z=:

: E[f^2] = sigma^2 / r^2 * ( 1 - Q(r) )
: Q(r)   = sqrt(pi/2) / r * exp( 1 / (2 r^2) ) * erfc( 1 / (r sqrt(2)) )

so

: g(r) = sqrt( (1 - Q(r)) / r^2 )

I checked this against a 400k-sample Monte Carlo at nine values of =r=; it agrees to
four decimal places. Sanity: =g -> 1= as =r -> 0= (unity slope at the origin), and
=g -> 1/r= as =r -> inf= (output RMS -> =s=, the square wave). Both hold.

| r = sigma/s | gain g(r) | loss    | makeup 1/g |
|-------------+-----------+---------+------------|
|        0.05 |    0.9963 | -0.03 dB |       1.00 |
|        0.10 |    0.9856 | -0.13 dB |       1.01 |
|        0.20 |    0.9481 | -0.46 dB |       1.05 |
|        0.30 |    0.8986 | -0.93 dB |       1.11 |
|        0.50 |    0.7931 | -2.01 dB |       1.26 |
|        0.70 |    0.6989 | -3.11 dB |       1.43 |
|        1.00 |    0.5868 | -4.63 dB |       1.70 |
|        1.50 |    0.4585 | -6.77 dB |       2.18 |
|        2.00 |    0.3748 | -8.52 dB |       2.67 |
|        3.00 |    0.2736 | -11.26 dB |      3.66 |
|        4.00 |    0.2151 | -13.35 dB |      4.65 |
|        6.00 |    0.1506 | -16.44 dB |      6.64 |
|        8.00 |    0.1158 | -18.73 dB |      8.64 |

For =k != 2= there is no closed form, but the integral is one-dimensional and smooth --
64-node Gauss-Hermite nails it. Compute a table once at load (see *implementation*).

** how badly a constant makeup fails

The makeup you need is =1/g(r)=, and =r= moves with =sqrt(N)=. Simulated on real
58-EDO note sums at =env = 1.0=, here is the makeup required at each =N=, and the
*spread* -- how far the right answer moves between one note and sixteen:

| s    | N=1   | N=2   | N=4   | N=8   | N=16  | spread |
|------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+--------|
| 1.00 | x1.01 | x1.02 | x1.04 | x1.08 | x1.14 | 1.1 dB |
| 0.50 | x1.02 | x1.06 | x1.15 | x1.24 | x1.41 | 2.8 dB |
| 0.30 | x1.06 | x1.16 | x1.31 | x1.65 | x1.84 | 4.8 dB |
| 0.20 | x1.13 | x1.32 | x1.56 | x1.92 | x2.42 | 6.6 dB |
| 0.10 | x1.43 | x1.89 | x2.38 | x3.09 | x3.97 | 8.9 dB |
| 0.05 | x2.20 | x3.09 | x4.08 | x5.36 | x7.45 | 10.6 dB |

Read this as the *cost of choosing option B*: pick one constant, split the spread, and
you are wrong by half of it at the extremes. So:

- =s >= 0.5= (gentle): a constant makeup is off by at most ~1.4 dB. *Genuinely fine.*
- =s ~ 0.3=: off by ~2.4 dB. Audible, tolerable.
- =s <= 0.1= (the heavy sound): off by 4-5 dB. *Unusable* -- exactly the wild
  fluctuation you don't want.

** the quantity the renderer already has

Here is the piece that makes the good option cheap. In =accumulate_voices=
(=sawwave/voices.rs:314=) each voice's sample is

: s_i = osc(waveform, phase, dt) * wf_norm * env * gain * amm * pulse * amplitude

=waveform_norm= exists precisely so that =osc * wf_norm= has RMS =1/sqrt(3)= for *every*
waveform (that is what the =waveform_norm_equalizes_rms_across_shapes= test asserts). So
if we name the rest of that product

: b_i = env * gain * amm * pulse * amplitude

then voice =i= has RMS exactly =b_i / sqrt(3)=, and the bus has

: sigma = sqrt( sum over dirty voices of b_i^2 / 3 )

*No detector, no smoothing, no lag.* It is one extra multiply-add inside a loop we are
already running, evaluated at the same (possibly oversampled) rate as the distortion
that consumes it. Because =env= ramps from zero on note-on and voices are dropped only
at =env == 0=, =sigma= is continuous: notes fade in and out of it with their own
envelopes. No zipper, no click, nothing to smooth.

I verified the prediction against the time-domain sum (=s = 0.2=, sine voices):

| N | sigma predicted | sigma measured | model gain | true gain | error |
|---+-----------------+----------------+------------+-----------+-------|
| 1 |          0.0866 |         0.0866 |     0.8278 |    0.8860 | -0.59 dB |
| 2 |          0.1225 |         0.1218 |     0.7383 |    0.7580 | -0.23 dB |
| 4 |          0.1732 |         0.1727 |     0.6328 |    0.6415 | -0.12 dB |
| 8 |          0.2449 |         0.2416 |     0.5218 |    0.5322 | -0.17 dB |
| 16 |         0.3464 |         0.3436 |     0.4156 |    0.4202 | -0.10 dB |

Better than 0.2 dB for =N >= 2=. The =N = 1= row is the only real error, and its cause
is understood: *a lone sinusoid is not Gaussian.* It is arcsine-distributed -- it dwells
near its peaks -- so the true clipper gain is higher than the Gaussian model predicts.
The model therefore over-corrects a single note by up to ~0.9 dB (worst case, at
=s = 0.1=). Three ways to handle that, in *implementation* below; the cheapest is to
shrug, since 0.9 dB on a lone note is under the just-noticeable difference for a
timbral change of this size.

** two things that make this safe

*Peaks do not come back.* The obvious worry with any makeup gain is that you restore the
level and re-hit the =+-0.95= clamp, trading a smooth clipper for a hard one. It does
not happen, and there is a reason: makeup restores *RMS*, and the distorted waveform is
*squarer* than the clean one -- lower crest factor. Restoring its RMS to the clean RMS
therefore leaves its peak *below* the clean peak. Measured:

| s    | N  | clean peak | distorted peak after makeup | ratio |
|------+----+------------+-----------------------------+-------|
| 0.30 |  4 |      0.457 |                       0.333 | 0.73  |
| 0.30 |  8 |      0.767 |                       0.422 | 0.55  |
| 0.30 | 16 |      1.163 |                       0.530 | 0.46  |
| 0.10 |  8 |      0.707 |                       0.310 | 0.44  |
| 0.10 | 16 |      1.252 |                       0.405 | 0.32  |

Always below 1.0. Since the clean path already lives with the clamp, the compensated
dirty path is strictly safer than what you ship today. (Look at the =N = 16= rows: clean
peaks at 1.16 and 1.25 -- *the clean bus is already hard-clipping on big chords*. With
distortion + makeup it is not. That is a bonus, not a design goal.)

*The pluck decay survives.* Because =sigma(t)= tracks the envelope exactly and the
makeup is recomputed every sample, the compensated bus's RMS equals =sigma(t)= as
=sigma= moves: the strike, the exponential decay toward =sustain_level=, and the release
all keep their shape. An audio-domain envelope follower (option D) could not manage
this -- it would lag the 3 ms attack and flatten the decay into a compressor.

Yes -- and asking cost the first cut two bugs. Both were invisible at the shipped

** the two bugs

*1. The makeup was capped at 8x, and the cap bound on a playable rig.* Once it binds, the
correction stops correcting and the volume drop comes straight back. Error against the
true makeup, in dB (negative = under-boosted = still too quiet):

| r = sigma/s | k = 0.5 | k = 1.0 | k = 2.0 |
|-------------+---------+---------+---------|
|           4 |   -2.22 |    0.00 |    0.00 |
|           8 |   -6.38 |   -1.98 |   -0.67 |
|          16 |  -10.90 |   -7.23 |   -6.36 |
|          32 |  -15.74 |  -12.76 |  -12.21 |

And =r= is easy to reach: =r = sigma / s=, and =sigma = amplitude * sqrt(N/3)=. At the
current =amplitude = 0.15=, sixteen notes give =sigma = 0.35=, so =s = 0.1= is already
=r = 3.5= (past the cap), and =s = 0.02= is =r = 17=. Raise =amplitude= and it moves
further. The cap now sits at 1024x -- 60 dB, high enough that it cannot bind anywhere in
the table. It is an infinity guard, not a limiter.

*2. Above the table the makeup used =m = r=, which is wrong for =k < 2.=* The reasoning
was: the clipper's output RMS plateaus at =s=, so restoring the input RMS =sigma = r*s=
needs a gain of =r=. The plateau is real. But it is approached *extraordinarily slowly*
for a soft elbow. Writing =h(r) = g(r)*r= for the output RMS in units of =s=:

| r    | k = 0.5 | k = 1.0 | k = 2.0 | k = 4.0 |
|------+---------+---------+---------+---------|
| 4    |  0.3873 |  0.6952 |  0.8605 |  0.9134 |
| 16   |  0.5700 |  0.8701 |  0.9620 |  0.9787 |
| 100  |  0.7674 |  0.9662 |  0.9941 |  0.9973 |
| 1000 |  0.9090 |  0.9940 |  0.9979 |  0.9979 |

At =k = 0.5= and =r = 16= the output has reached only *57%* of its asymptote, so =m = r=
under-boosts by 4.9 dB there and 11 dB by =r = 32=. The table now runs to =r = 256=, and
beyond it continues *linearly from its last entry* (=m = m_last * r/R_MAX=), which assumes
nothing about the asymptote, is continuous, and errs by at most +0.7 dB out at =r = 1000=
-- on the *loud* side, which is the safe direction here.

** the robustness surface, axis by axis

| you change            | is the makeup right?                                                    |
|-----------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =distortion_scale= (s) | *Exact.* =s= only enters as =r = sigma/s=, and the table spans =r= to 256. |
| =distortion_shape= (k) | *Exact.* The table is rebuilt per =k= on load / ='r'=. Verified 0.25..8.  |
| =amplitude=            | *Exact.* It is inside =sigma=, so =r= tracks it.                          |
| =[[timbres]] amplitude=, AM depth, polyrhythm pulse | *Exact.* All are factors of =b=, so they are inside =sigma=. |
| note count =N=         | *Exact for N >= 2* (0.35 dB). At =N = 1=, over-corrects by <= 0.73 dB.   |
| waveform               | *<= 1.3 dB*, worst case only. See below.                                |
| unisons / octaves      | Under-corrects by <= 3 dB on an exact unison. Errs quiet. Rare, brief.  |
| =oversample=           | *Exact.* The makeup is recomputed per sub-sample.                        |
| both grids distorting  | *Exact.* =sigma= sums both grids' dirty voices.                          |

The two soft spots share one cause: *the model assumes the dirty bus is Gaussian*, which
a sum of many incoherent voices is, and a single voice is not. A lone sinusoid dwells near
its peaks; a lone square wave never leaves them. So the model mis-predicts the clipper's
gain most for one loud note, and the error depends on which waveform:

| curve         | notes | sine | triangle | saw  | square |
|---------------+-------+------+----------+------+--------|
| s=1.0, k=0.5  |     1 | 0.43 |     0.31 | 0.31 |   0.74 |
| s=1.0, k=0.5  |     2 | 0.14 |     0.14 | 0.12 |  -0.01 |
| s=0.1, k=1.0  |     1 | 0.73 |     0.50 | 0.50 |   1.32 |
| s=0.1, k=1.0  |     2 | 0.14 |     0.19 | 0.15 |  -0.40 |
| s=0.1, k=1.0  |     4 | 0.18 |     0.12 | 0.16 |  -0.06 |

(dB; positive = the makeup over-boosts.) Worst case is *1.3 dB, on a single square wave
into a heavy clipper*, and it collapses below 0.25 dB by two notes. You said roughly is
fine; this is roughly. If it ever isn't, option E's sine-blend fixes it for ~6 lines.

*So: yes, robust.* Not by luck -- because the correction inverts the actual curve rather
than approximating it, and =sigma= is derived from the same per-voice amplitudes the
renderer already multiplies in. The only approximation left is the Gaussian one, and its
error is bounded and measured above.

** a warning about small k

The sweep through =shape = 0.3= is worth recording, because a soft elbow is nothing like
what the =k= table in [[./distortion.org]] suggests by the word "gentlest":

| notes | loss before makeup | makeup needed |
|-------+--------------------+---------------|
|     1 |          -14.41 dB |         5.26x |
|     2 |          -15.60 dB |         6.03x |
|     4 |          -16.86 dB |         6.97x |
|     8 |          -18.20 dB |         8.13x |
|    16 |          -19.61 dB |         9.56x |

The reason is that "unity slope at the origin" is a *limit*, not a neighbourhood.
Expanding the curve for small =y=:

: f(y)/y  ~  1 - |y/s|^k / k

For =k = 2= the correction is quadratic and dies fast -- a quiet signal really is clean.
For =k = 0.3= it dies like =y^0.3=, which is barely dying at all: at =sigma = s/10000=
the makeup is *still 1.26x*. A soft elbow does not mean a late one; it means the curve
bends everywhere. =k < 1= is a *saturation that is always on*, and that is presumably why
you like it. Just do not read the =k= column of =distortion.org= as "how loud before it
bites" -- =s= sets that, and for =k < 1= barely even =s= does.

* is a per-sample correction dangerous?

Jeff's question, and it deserves a precise answer rather than a reassurance. Three
separate claims, of which the third is the one worth internalizing.

*It cannot click, because a voice enters and leaves the sum at exactly zero power.* A
voice is born with =env = 0= (the attack ramps it up) and is dropped only once =env=
reaches 0 again, so its =b^2= contribution to =sigma= fades in and out continuously.
There is no moment at which the makeup steps. Unit-tested
(=makeup_is_continuous_through_note_birth_and_death=).

*It is not a waveshaper.* =sigma= is computed from *envelopes*, never from the waveform,
so the makeup is constant across each cycle of every note. It changes only as fast as
your envelopes change -- 3 ms at the very fastest, and 0.5 s through the pluck decay. It
adds no sidebands the envelopes did not already have. The danger you are imagining is
real, but it belongs to the *opposite* implementation: updating the gain once per cpal
callback (128 frames, a 375 Hz staircase) is what produces zipper noise. Per-sample is
what avoids it.

*The one genuinely sharp thing, and why it is harmless.* At =k < 1= -- your setting --
=1/g= has a =sqrt(sigma)= cusp at silence: strike a note into an empty bank and the
makeup swings from 1.0 to ~1.5 within a handful of samples. That looks alarming. It is
inaudible, and the cancellation is exact rather than approximate: the makeup is *only
ever multiplied by a signal whose own RMS is =g * sigma=*, so the compensated envelope is

: m(sigma) * g(sigma) * sigma  ==  sigma        (identically, by construction)

The clipper's cusp and the makeup's cusp are the same cusp, and they cancel. What
reaches your ears is =sigma=, the clean envelope. Both halves of that -- the cusp being
real at =k < 1=, and the product being flat anyway -- are asserted in the same test, so
nobody "fixes" the cusp later and breaks the reason it was safe.

*What the makeup does not do* -- and this is the honest limit. It restores mean-square
level; it does not restore the moment-to-moment envelope *within* a chord. A clipper
compresses the beating between close pitches, so on a 12 ms window the distorted bus can
sit several dB above where the clean bus would have dipped into a beat null. That is the
sound of bus distortion levelling a chord, it is what makes a fuzz a fuzz, and it is
already there today -- the makeup scales it, it does not create it. If you ever hear
something "weird but maybe cool", that is what you are hearing, and turning
=distortion_auto_makeup= off will not remove it (only option G would, at the cost of all
intermodulation).

* should the correction be slow, so attacks stay loud?

Jeff: "whatever is boosting or squashing the signal should change slowly over time ...
maybe the correction should take a while to kick in, so that attacks still come through
as louder."

This is the standard compressor intuition, and it is *exactly inverted here*. The reason
is one line of monotonicity, and it is worth pinning down because it decides the knob.

** why lag hurts attacks

=m(sigma) = 1/g(sigma/s)= is *increasing* in =sigma=: a louder bus is driven further past
the elbow, so the clipper eats more of it, so more must be given back. The makeup's target
is therefore at its *highest* during a strike.

A lagged makeup is one that has not got there yet. So at the strike it under-compensates,
precisely at the moment the clipper is biting hardest. The attack comes out *squashed*,
and then *swells* as the makeup catches up. Simulated on a plucked note (=k = 0.5=,
=s = 1=, 3 ms attack, 0.5 s decay to =sustain_level = 0.35=):

| slew   | attack/sustain contrast | vs clean | swell after the strike |
|--------+-------------------------+----------+------------------------|
| 0 ms   |                 4.92 dB |  0.00 dB |               -0.03 dB |
| 20 ms  |                 4.07 dB | -0.85 dB |               +3.51 dB |
| 50 ms  |                 2.78 dB | -2.14 dB |               +3.15 dB |
| 200 ms |                 0.66 dB | -4.26 dB |               -0.01 dB |

The clean note's own attack/sustain contrast is *4.92 dB* (that is the pluck envelope,
1 -> 0.35). *The exact, zero-slew makeup reproduces it precisely.* Every millisecond of
lag takes contrast away and, in the middle of the range, adds an audible +3.5 dB bloom
after each strike.

So: *the punchiest possible level-restoring makeup is the instantaneous one.* It is what
is built. If you want attacks louder than that, you want a transient shaper, not a slower
makeup -- nothing in the makeup's design can exceed the clean envelope, because the clean
envelope is exactly what it targets.

** the compressor's time constants exist for a reason we don't have

A compressor needs an attack and a release because it must *estimate* the signal's level
from the waveform, and any estimator over a waveform is a filter: too fast and it ripples
at the fundamental, too slow and it lags. All of a compressor's characteristic artifacts
-- pumping, breathing, transient smear -- are the sound of that filter.

We do not estimate. We *know*: the renderer multiplies the per-voice envelopes in, and
=sigma= is read straight out of them, before the waveform is even summed. There is no
detector, so there is no filter, so there is no time constant to tune and no artifact
class that tuning one would fix. Your worry -- "changing fast will introduce artifacts" --
is a worry about a detector we don't have.

What the makeup *does* inherit is the envelopes' own rate of change: 3 ms at the very
fastest (the attack), 0.5 s through the decay. That is precisely as slow as your request
asked for, without anyone choosing a constant.

** the knob, and what it actually means

Built anyway, because it is worth being able to hear: =distortion_makeup_slew_ms= on the
=cpal_synth= sink, default =0.0= (exact, and bit-identical to having no slew at all).

Its real meaning is not "how fast does the correction react". It is *how much of the
clipper's own dynamic compression do you keep?*

- =0 ms=: none. The distorted bus carries the clean bus's envelope. Option *E*.
- large: the makeup freezes at one value and can no longer track anything. That *is* a
  plain constant makeup gain -- option *B*.

The knob slides continuously between the two options in this document, and nothing else is
reachable. A fuzz pedal's compression of dynamics is part of why it sounds like a pedal;
if the exact version sounds too composed, too *un*-squashed, this is the dial, and 20-50 ms
is where the character lives. But it is a character choice, not a fix, and it costs attack.

** candidates I considered and rejected

- *Asymmetric slew (fast up, slow down).* Fixes the swell -- the makeup reaches the strike
  in time -- and keeps a slow tail. But "fast up" is just "exact" at the only moment that
  matters, so it buys nothing the zero-slew version doesn't already have, while adding a
  time constant and a state variable to reason about.
- *Drive the makeup from a slow average of =sigma= (the "program level").* Tracks note
  count over ~200 ms and ignores per-note envelopes. Same defect as the plain slew: within
  a strike the makeup is frozen, so the attack keeps the clipper's squash. It also cannot
  distinguish "a chord arrived" from "one note got louder", which is the whole quantity
  we already have exactly.
- *Slew =sigma= rather than =m=.* Identical dynamics -- =m= is a monotone function of
  =sigma=, so lagging either lags the other. No.
- *A transient shaper on top* (boost =m= for the first few ms of each strike). The only
  design here that could make attacks louder than clean. It needs per-voice state and it
  is a different effect wearing the makeup's clothes. If you want it, ask for it by name.

** and the thing you don't need to worry about

"If it took a few periods to adjust when the number of notes changes, that's fine." It
takes *zero*, and that is not a hazard: when a note joins, its envelope ramps its power
into =sigma= from zero over the 3 ms attack, so the makeup slides to its new value across
those same 3 ms. Nothing steps. There is no adjustment period to smooth because there is
no discontinuity to smooth over.

* three different things "compensation" could mean

Worth separating before choosing, because they are not the same request and only you
know which you want.

1. *Level-transparent.* Turning distortion on changes timbre and not loudness. Note
   count then affects level exactly as it does clean. -> option E.
2. *Level-flat.* Loudness is constant regardless of note count -- more notes do not get
   louder at all. This is what the *uncompensated* clipper already approximates (it
   pins near =s=). Probably not what you want; it makes the instrument unresponsive.
3. *Character-flat.* The *grit* stays the same regardless of note count -- one note is
   as dirty as a twelve-note chord. -> option F.

You asked for (1). It is also the one that behaves like a real overdrive: play harder or
play more notes, get dirtier, and the loudness tracks what you played.

* the options

| # | option                          | fixes the fall | fixes N-dependence | cost      | keeps intermodulation |
|---+---------------------------------+----------------+--------------------+-----------+-----------------------|
| A | raise sink =amplitude=          | no             | no                 | 0         | yes                   |
| B | constant =distortion_makeup=    | on average     | no (see spread)    | ~3 lines  | yes                   |
| C | drive + =1/drive= makeup        | same as B      | no                 | ~5 lines  | yes                   |
| D | envelope-follower auto-makeup   | yes            | yes, with pumping  | ~40 lines | yes                   |
| E | *envelope-predicted makeup*     | *yes, exactly* | *yes, exactly*     | ~20 lines | yes                   |
| F | input-normalize the bus         | yes            | yes (character too)| ~15 lines | yes                   |
| G | distort each voice separately   | yes            | yes                | ~5 lines  | *no*                  |

** A -- turn the master up when distortion is on

Non-starter, listed to dismiss. =amplitude= is on the sink, shared by *both* grids and by
the clean bus. Grid A's toggle would change grid B's loudness. It also cannot track =N=.

** B -- a constant =distortion_makeup= on the sink

: out = clean + makeup * distort(dirt)

One float in the rig, hot-reloadable with ='r'= like =amplitude= already is. Three lines
in =post()=.

This is *the right answer if you keep =s >= 0.5==, per the spread table: worst error
~1.4 dB. It is the wrong answer for the heavy sounds. Its honest description is "fixes
the average level, leaves the note-count dependence exactly as it is."

If you want something in your hands this afternoon, take B, set =makeup = 1.26= for
=s = 0.5=, and see whether you still care. It is also a strict subset of E (E's trim
knob *is* B), so it is not wasted work.

** C -- a drive knob with =1/drive= makeup

The idiom [[./distortion.org]] proposes at "Relation to an amount / drive knob":

: out = distort(drive * y, s, k) / drive

Worth noting that *this is algebraically option B*: pushing =drive*y= into a curve with
asymptote =s= is identical to pushing =y= into a curve with asymptote =s/drive=, and the
=1/drive= is then just a constant makeup. So C = B with the makeup constant welded to
the scale. It is a nicer *control surface* (one knob, and small signals stay put), but it
buys no new N-invariance. Don't implement it thinking it solves the problem.

Its real merit is elsewhere and is worth stealing: expressing drive *relative to the mix*
decouples the distortion character from the master volume, which is the calibration bug
diagnosed at the top. See *reparametrizing the knob* below.

** D -- audio-domain auto-makeup (envelope follower)

Measure the dirty bus's RMS before and after the clipper with one-pole followers, and
apply =makeup = rms_pre / rms_post=, smoothed over ~30 ms.

This is the textbook "auto makeup gain" and it does adapt to =N=. But:

- *It lags.* The attack is precisely where the clipper bites hardest, and a 30 ms
  follower is still climbing when the strike is over.
- *It ripples.* At =fundamental_hz = 80= one cycle is 12.5 ms. A window short enough not
  to lag is short enough to ripple at the note frequency -- that is intermodulation
  distortion you did not ask for.
- *It fights the envelope.* As the pluck decays, =rms_pre= falls, the makeup rises, and
  the decay flattens. You have accidentally built a compressor.
- *It is stateful and hard to test.* Two more filters whose time constants need tuning
  by ear, in a codebase whose distortion is currently a pure function with a unit test.

Every one of these problems exists *only because the follower is guessing at a number
the renderer already knows exactly.* Which is option E.

** E -- envelope-predicted makeup  [recommended]

Compute =sigma= from the voice envelopes as shown above, look up =m = 1/g(sigma/s)=, and
apply it to the distorted bus:

: sigma = sqrt( sum of b_i^2 over dirty voices / 3 )
: m     = min( 1 / g(sigma / s), CAP ) * trim
: out   = clean + m * distort(dirt, s, k)

*What it gives you.* The distorted bus carries the clean bus's RMS at every instant.
Turning the toggle on is a pure timbre change. Note count changes the level exactly as
much as it does with the toggle off -- which is to say, the way you are used to, the way
the clean instrument already behaves. Adding a note gets louder; it does not lurch.

*What it costs.* One multiply-add per dirty voice per sub-sample, one =sqrt=, one table
lookup + lerp. Nothing transcendental in the audio path (=erfc= runs once at load). No
new state, so =oversample= and the decimator are untouched.

*What it does not do.* The grit still grows with =N= -- twelve notes are dirtier than
one, at the same loudness. That is (1) not (3), and I believe it is what you want: it is
how an overdriven amp behaves.

*The caveats, all of them:*

- *Coherent voices.* =sigma= assumes voices are mutually incoherent. Unisons and octaves
  are not. The same pitch fingered on *both* grids is two voices (by design, per the
  rig readme) and sums coherently: true RMS is =2a=, predicted is =a*sqrt(2)=, so the
  makeup runs up to 3 dB low on an exact unison. Rare, brief, and it errs on the *quiet*
  side. Ignore it.
- *A lone note.* Gaussian is wrong at =N = 1=; over-corrects by up to 0.9 dB. Options:
  accept it; or blend toward the exact sinusoid gain when one voice dominates (compute
  =max_i b_i^2 / sum_i b_i^2= -- it is 1 for a single voice, =1/N= for equals -- and
  lerp between the sine and Gaussian tables on it). The blend is ~6 lines and removes
  the last audible artifact. I'd ship without it and add it if you hear it.
- *Equal RMS is not equal loudness.* A distorted signal at the same RMS *sounds louder*:
  it is brighter, and the ear is more sensitive where the new harmonics land. So expect
  the mathematically-exact makeup to feel slightly hot. That is what the =trim=
  multiplier is for. Start it at ~0.8 (-2 dB) and tune by ear.
- *Cap the makeup.* =m -> r= without bound as the drive grows. Clamp at ~8x so a
  pathological =sigma= cannot run away.

** F -- input-normalize the bus (constant character)

: out = clean + sigma * distort(dirt / sigma * drive, 1.0, k)

Normalize the bus to unit RMS, clip, scale back. Level-transparent *and* character-flat:
a chord is exactly as gritty as a single note.

Rejected, but for an interesting reason rather than cost. Normalizing by the *instant-
aneous* =sigma= means that as a note decays, the drive stays constant -- the note stays
maximally gritty all the way down into silence. Notes would not clean up as they fade,
which is the opposite of every physical instrument. Fixing that means floor-ing or
slewing the normalizer, at which point you have rebuilt option D's tuning problems.

Reach for F only if you decide you want (3), character-flat, and are willing to lose the
"quiet = clean" dynamic.

** G -- distort each voice, then sum

Move =distort()= inside =accumulate_voices=, per voice.

Loudness then grows exactly linearly in =N=, with no compensation at all, because each
voice is clipped at its own level and the sum is linear. Simplest possible fix.

The reason not to: *it destroys the intermodulation*, which is the whole character of
this effect and an explicit design intent -- the rig readme says the switched-on grids'
voices "are summed and distorted together ... so two switched-on grids intermodulate, as
the old global mode did", and =misc.org= specified distortion on "the sum of all the
voices". Per-voice distortion is a chorus of independently fuzzed sines; bus distortion
is a fuzz pedal. They do not sound alike.

Note that *E gives you G's loudness stability while keeping the intermodulation.* That
is the argument for E in one sentence.

* recommendation

Take *E*, with *B* as its trim knob. *This is what is now built* -- see *as built* below.

1. =distortion_makeup= on the =cpal_synth= sink -- a *trim*, default =1.0=, applied on
   top of the automatic correction. This is B, and it is your by-ear control.
2. =distortion_auto_makeup= (bool, default =true=). With it =false=, =distortion_makeup=
   acts alone as a plain constant -- so B is reachable, and E is falsifiable by A/B test
   from the rig file without a rebuild. Both are ='r'=-reloadable, like =amplitude=.
3. The =sigma= accumulation and the makeup table.

* as built (2026-07-10)

Option E, defaulted on. Four files, ~120 lines including the table:

- =sawwave/voices.rs= -- =Makeup= (the =1/g(r)= table + trim + auto), =DistortionStage=
  (curve + makeup), =gaussian_rms_gain= (the Simpson integral), and the =dirt_pow=
  accumulation. =accumulate_voices= now splits the amplitude coefficient =b= out of the
  per-voice product so its square can be summed: one extra multiply-add per dirty voice.
- =rig.rs= -- the two sink fields, their defaults, and a =distortion_makeup > 0= check.
- =surfaces_runtime/mod.rs= -- the fields into =Settings=; the table into =Live= behind
  its own =Arc= (it is an allocation, so it cannot ride in the =Copy= =LiveParams=), built
  by =live_makeup= on the load / hot-reload thread, *never* on the audio thread.
- =surfaces_runtime/audio.rs= -- the callback clones the =Arc= (one atomic increment) and
  hands the renderer a =DistortionStage=.

Plus =distortion_makeup_slew_ms= (default 0), the character knob argued above.

Three implementation notes that were not obvious up front:

- *The table is sampled in =sqrt(r)=, not =r=.* At =k < 1= the makeup has a =sqrt=
  cusp at the origin (see *is a per-sample correction dangerous?*), and a uniform
  table is off by *0.64 dB* near zero. Warped, it is off by *0.0001 dB*. Since =k = 0.5=
  is the shape you actually play, this mattered.
- *The table must run far past where you think.* 1024 entries to =r = 256=, then a linear
  continuation. See *is it robust to any parameters I choose?* -- stopping at =r = 16=
  and assuming the asymptote cost 11 dB at =k = 0.5=.
- *The Simpson integral is exact enough to be boring.* 256 nodes over =z in [0, 8]= sit
  within 1e-4 dB of a 2048-node reference, and match the closed form at =k = 2=. It runs
  1024 times per rig load, off the audio thread.

Tests (all in =voices.rs=, no cpal, no sound card):

| test                                              | asserts                                             |
|---------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| =gaussian_rms_gain_matches_the_closed_form_at_k2= | the integral vs =erfc=; the =r -> 0= and =r -> inf= limits; monotonicity at k = 0.5, 1, 2, 4 |
| =makeup_table_inverts_the_gain_and_off_is_unity=  | table + lerp within 0.02 dB over the whole domain, k = 0.25..8; the cap never binds in-domain; the continuation errs loud; NaN/inf inputs stay finite |
| =makeup_is_robust_across_the_settings_a_player_can_dial= | the regression for both bugs: every =(k, scale, N)= a player can dial is within 0.05 dB and uncapped |
| =makeup_restores_the_clean_rms_across_note_counts= | the whole point: within 0.5 dB of clean for N = 2..16, over six curves including yours |
| =makeup_never_raises_the_peak_above_the_clean_peak= | the crest-factor argument -- no new hard clipping   |
| =makeup_is_continuous_through_note_birth_and_death= | dead voices contribute zero; =m * g * sigma == sigma=; the k < 1 cusp is real and harmless |
| =slewing_the_makeup_costs_attack_punch_rather_than_protecting_it= | zero slew reproduces the clean attack contrast; every slew erodes it, monotonically |
| =zero_slew_is_the_exact_makeup_and_a_huge_slew_is_a_constant= | the knob really does interpolate E <-> B, and nothing else |
| =render_distortion_compresses_...= (rewritten)     | the *uncompensated* clipper still compresses, under =Makeup::off()= |

That last row is the one to notice: its old assertion, =rms(heavy) < rms(clean) * 0.7=,
was the bug written down as a guarantee. It now runs against =Makeup::off()=, which is
exactly what that assertion was always describing.

The two slew tests exist to stop the tempting-but-backwards intuition from being
re-introduced by a later reader (or a later me).

*Measured accuracy*, from the passing tests and the Python replica behind them: within
*0.35 dB* of the clean bus for =N >= 2= across six =(s, k)= pairs from gentle to heavy,
and *0.73 dB* at =N = 1= (the Gaussian-vs-sinusoid bias -- 1.3 dB for a lone square wave;
below the JND for a timbre change this size, and fixable with the sine-gain blend
described under option E).

*What to try first.* Play it at your =k = 0.5=. If it reads hot -- it may, since equal
RMS is not equal loudness -- set =distortion_makeup = 0.8= and press ='r'=. If you want
to hear what you have been living with, set =distortion_auto_makeup = false= and press
='r'= again; the fall comes back.

** reparametrizing the knob (optional, separable)

Independent of all the above, and worth doing whenever you next touch this: =s= in
absolute units means *the master volume is a drive knob*. Pull =amplitude= down for
headroom and the distortion quietly vanishes -- which is, I suspect, how you got here.

Fix by referencing the drive to *one voice at full envelope*, =sigma_1 = amplitude/sqrt(3)=:

: s = sigma_1 / drive          # so r = sqrt(N) * drive

Now =drive= is dimensionless and means something: =drive = 1= puts a single struck note
exactly on the elbow. Master volume no longer changes the character; note count still
does. Pre-makeup loss, by =drive=, at =k = 2= (your =k = 0.5= is far steeper -- that is
the point of question 3 at the bottom):

| drive | N=1     | N=2     | N=4     | N=8     | N=16    |
|-------+---------+---------+---------+---------+---------|
|  0.25 |  -0.7 dB |  -1.2 dB |  -2.0 dB |  -3.1 dB |  -4.6 dB |
|  0.50 |  -2.0 dB |  -3.1 dB |  -4.6 dB |  -6.4 dB |  -8.5 dB |
|  1.00 |  -4.6 dB |  -6.4 dB |  -8.5 dB | -10.8 dB | -13.3 dB |
|  2.00 |  -8.5 dB | -10.8 dB | -13.3 dB | -16.0 dB | -18.7 dB |
|  4.00 | -13.3 dB | -16.0 dB | -18.7 dB | -21.5 dB | -24.4 dB |

With E applied, every cell of that table becomes 0.0 dB. The table then describes only
how much *harmonic* content you are manufacturing -- which is what a drive knob should
mean.

Your current =s = 1.0=, =amplitude = 0.15= corresponds to =drive = 0.087=: off the top of
the table, in the "nearly clean" corner. That is the diagnosis in one number.

* implementation sketch

Three files.

** =sawwave/voices.rs= -- the accumulation

Split =b_i= out of the existing product and accumulate its square. In =accumulate_voices=:

#+begin_src rust
  let b = v.env * v.timbre.gain * amm * pulse * amplitude;
  let s = osc(v.timbre.waveform, v.phase, dt) * wf_norm * b;
  if dirty(src) {
    dirt += s;
    dirt_pow += b * b;        // <- the only new work
  } else {
    clean += s;
  }
#+end_src

Return =(clean, dirt, dirt_pow)=. Then =sigma = (dirt_pow / 3.0).sqrt()=.

** =sawwave/voices.rs= -- the makeup table

=g(r)= depends only on =k=, so build the table when the sink loads and when ='r'=
hot-reloads. 512 entries of =1/g= over =r= in =[0, 16]=, linear interpolation; above 16
use =m = r= (the asymptote, accurate to well under a percent by then). For =k = 2= fill
it from the =erfc= closed form; for other =k=, 64-node Gauss-Hermite on
=E[f(sigma z)^2]=. Both run once, at load, off the audio thread.

Then =post()= becomes:

#+begin_src rust
  let post = |clean: f32, dirt: f32, dirt_pow: f32| match distortion {
    Some(d) => {
      let sigma = (dirt_pow / 3.0).sqrt();
      let m = makeup.lookup(sigma / d.scale) * d.trim;   // table is capped
      (clean + m * distort(dirt, d)).clamp(-0.95, 0.95)
    }
    None => (clean + dirt).clamp(-0.95, 0.95),
  };
#+end_src

The =None= arm stays bit-identical, so =distortion None is bit-identical to render()=
keeps passing.

Two new optional sink fields with defaults (=distortion_makeup = 1.0=,
=distortion_auto_makeup = true=), threaded through =Live.params.distortion= exactly as
=distortion_scale= already is -- it is already read fresh in the audio callback, so

The pure-function shape of =distort= is preserved, so all of this is testable without
cpal, in the style of the existing tests at =voices.rs:974..1060=.

- =makeup_table_matches_closed_form_at_k2= -- table vs the =erfc= expression, =r= in
  =[0.01, 16]=. Tolerance 1e-3.
- =makeup_restores_the_clean_rms= -- render =N= incoherent voices for =N= in =1..=16= and
  =s= in ={1.0, 0.3, 0.1}=; assert =rms(dirty_with_makeup) / rms(clean)= is within
  *0.5 dB of unity* for =N >= 2=, and within *1.0 dB* at =N = 1= (the Gaussian bias --
  encode the known error rather than pretending it is absent).
- =makeup_never_raises_the_peak_above_clean= -- the crest-factor argument, asserted.
- =distortion_none_is_bit_identical= -- unchanged, must still pass.

*One existing test will need rewriting*, and it is worth knowing before you start:
=render_distortion_compresses_the_summed_mix_and_off_is_identical= (=voices.rs:1002=)
asserts =rms(heavy) < rms(clean) * 0.7=. That assertion *is the bug*, restated as a
guarantee. Under E it must become "RMS is preserved; the *waveform* differs" -- e.g.
compare RMS for equality and assert a nonzero third-harmonic content, or keep the
compression assertion but with =auto_makeup = false=.

* honesty about these numbers

The model (the Gaussian RMS gain, the closed form, the tables above) was derived and
cross-checked in Python: the =erfc= form against a 400k-sample Monte Carlo, the
envelope-predicted =sigma= against a time-domain sum of real 58-EDO voices. The *shipped*
numbers -- the 0.35 dB / 0.73 dB accuracy, the peak bound, the continuity -- come from
the Rust unit tests listed under *as built*, which render the actual =BlockRenderer= path.

What has *not* happened: nobody has heard it. I have no grids and no sound card in this
container, so the audio callback, the =Arc= handoff, and the hot-reload of the table are
exercised only by construction and by the type system. The DSP under them is tested. The
first real test is you pressing the toggle.

Two things I would listen for specifically:

1. *Does it read hot?* The compensation is exact in RMS, and RMS is not loudness. If so,
   =distortion_makeup = 0.8= and ='r'=.
2. *Does a lone note struck into silence sound right?* That is the one place the model is
   weakest (0.73 dB over-correction, plus the =k < 1= cusp in the makeup). Both are
   argued harmless above; a note struck alone, loud, at =k = 0.5= is where the argument
   would fail if it is going to.

*Revisit if:* you find you want (3) character-flat rather than (1) level-transparent --
then F, and the "notes stay gritty as they decay" problem needs a decision. Or if you
add a per-grid distortion *amount* control on the grid (there is a free cell next to the
toggle), in which case the drive reparametrization above stops being optional. Or if the
compensated sound is too *composed* -- then =distortion_makeup_slew_ms=, and you have
traded attack for a fuzz's own compression, knowingly.
