#+title: Two monomes + KMSS drums -- reference
#+date: 2026-07-01

The =2-monomes_kmss-drums= rig drives *three surfaces at once*: two
16x16 monome grids as independent 46-EDO play surfaces, and the Keith McMillen
SoftStep (KMSS) pedalboard as a velocity-sensitive drumkit. It is the first rig
that *composes* what used to be three mutually-exclusive runtimes (the sawwave EDO
grid, the looper's scroll pad, the drumkit) into one -- see the "surfaces runtime"
in =buried/midi_pulse/surfaces_runtime/=.

* glossary

- *surface* -- one of the three playable devices this rig brings up at once: grid
  A, grid B, or the KMSS.
- *play grid* -- a 16x16 monome, the whole grid a 46-EDO note surface (=x_step 9=,
  =y_step 1=, =fundamental_hz 80=). Each grid has its *own* voices and its *own*
  scroll register.
- *scroll pad* -- a 3x2 pad in a grid's bottom-right (=[13,14,15,15]=) that moves
  *that grid's* pitch window: the four arrows shift by one cell, the two top corners
  by an octave. Reuses the looper's =edo_shift_pad=.
- *waveform strip / selector* -- a 4-cell radio on a grid's top row
  (=[0,0]..[3,0]=): sine / triangle / square / saw, left to right. Each grid's strip
  sets its *own* timbre (self-control, since 2026-07; the =controls= field can still
  point strips across grids, but this rig no longer does). No AM/FM.
- *(volume strip -- removed 2026-07.)* The old 12-cell fader right of the waveform
  strip is gone (misc.org "drop the amplitude row"); per-waveform loudness now lives
  in the rig's =[[timbres]] amplitude= (hot-reloadable with 'r'), and the master
  is the synth sink's =amplitude=. Cells 4..12 of the top row are play cells again;
  13..15 belong to the polyrhythm pad.
- *trail* -- up to ~trail.max~ recently played *pitch classes* (octave-independent; 9
  here), shared across both grids, shown dim on both as a backdrop; a currently-sounding
  class shows bright instead. Repeating a note refreshes its single entry (so hammering one
  note never floods or erases the trail), and playing a note clears any trailed neighbour
  within ~1/trail.clobber_radius~ of an octave (1/27 here, ~44 cents), so close pitches
  never crowd the backdrop. Both knobs live in the ~[trail]~ table (parameter names and
  ranges: [[file:README.org::*Surfaces][rigs/README.org "Surfaces"]]). Lets you see
  where to finger on one grid to harmonize with the other.
- *independent voices* -- the same pitch fingered on both grids is *two* separate
  voices; releasing one never cuts the other. Even two cells on one grid that collide
  to the same pitch are independent (one voice per finger). Voices are keyed by
  =(grid, cell)=, not by pitch (contrast the looper, which ref-counts by pitch).
- *distortion toggle* -- a single cell at =(0,1)= (second row, far left) on each
  grid: key-down flips a soft-clip distortion for THIS grid's voices (per-monome;
  drums are untouched). The switched-on grids' voices are summed and distorted
  together; a grid with its toggle off bypasses the clipper entirely. The curve's
  =distortion_scale= / =distortion_shape= live on the synth sink; see
  [[../../../learnings/distortion.org]].
- *slide toggle* -- =(1,1)= (right of the distortion toggle): while on, a new note
  on THIS grid re-triggers the nearest pitch released within the last
  ~slide.candidate_window_ms~ and *glides* it into the new pitch over
  ~slide.duration_ms~ (both in the ~[slide]~ table). A used slide source leaves
  the candidate set. Per-monome switch; each grid keeps its own candidate history.
- *mono toggle* -- =(1,2)= (below slide): while on, each new note cuts that grid's
  other fingered notes (through the normal release path, so accrete still captures
  them). With slide on too, the new note *steals* the cut note's voice and glides
  it over -- true legato, no attack re-trigger. Per-monome switch, like the rest.
- *feet-accrete toggle* -- =(0,14)= (just above the accrete trio): PER-GRID -- while
  grid g's toggle is on, the KMSS pedal triple mapped to grid g (1/2/3 for the older
  monobright grid, 8/9/0 for the other) mirrors g's accrete trio (clear /
  needs-holding / accrete) instead of playing samples; pedals 4-7 keep drumming.
  The softstep can mirror one monome, both, or neither. Holding pedal 3 or 0 IS
  holding that bank's accrete button (the tether decoder reports foot lifts).
- *polyrhythm pad* -- the 3x2 top-right corner (=[13,0]..[15,1]=): =| x3 x2 tap |=
  over =| /3 /2 =1 |=. Tap twice within ~tap_tempo.window_ms~ to define the ONE
  global tapped tempo (displayed by the blinking tap cell, unfactored; defining it
  starts nothing). Each grid then has its own *applied* tempo -- tapped x its own
  2^a x 3^b factor -- and its own amplitude-cycling switch on =1 (press to start,
  keeping the factor; press again to snap to unity; fast double-tap to stop;
  starts off). While a grid's cycling is on, its notes pulse with a *unipolar
  triangle* at its applied tempo at their onset. See *polyrhythm* below.
- *accrete buttons / sustained set* -- four single-cell buttons at the bottom-left
  of each grid (=(0,15)= clear, =(1,15)= needs-holding, =(2,15)= accrete, =(1,14)=
  erase). Notes added to the *sustained set* keep ringing after their fingers lift,
  until *clear* flushes them or *erase* removes them one by one. One accrete BANK
  per monome: a grid's buttons drive only its own bank, and only notes fingered on
  that grid can join it. See *sustain (accrete)* below.
- *drumkit* -- the KMSS as nine TR-808 one-shots, velocity-sensitive (tether mode).
  Reused verbatim from =kmss-drumkit= (see [[./kmss-drumkit_readme.org]]).

* usage

Launch under =pw-jack= so the synth *and* the drum sampler share the sound card via
PipeWire (two JACK nodes in one process):

#+begin_src sh
pw-jack cargo run --bin midi_pulse -- 2-monomes_kmss-drums
#+end_src

Needs *both* 16x16 grids connected (the runtime hands each rig monome a distinct
live grid in rig order; it does not matter which physical grid is which) and the
KMSS. On the host, connect audio with =./tools/connect-midi.sh= if needed. The
drum WAVs resolve under the top-level =drum-samples/= folder (same as
=kmss-drumkit=); that folder must be present in this worktree.

=Ctrl-C= blanks *both* grids, stops the synth, and restores the KMSS to standalone
mode.

A headless device-layer smoke (two virtual grids, no sound, no KMSS) runs as a unit
test (=surfaces_runtime::tests::two_grids_run_against_mock_grids=) against the
=2-monomes_kmss-drums-mock= rig; see [[../../../MOCK-MONOME.org]].

* per-grid layout

The two grids are identical. The whole 16x16 is the play surface, with two
overlays drawn on top:

#+begin_example
  col: 0   1   2   3   4   5  ...        13  14  15
  row 0  [SIN TRI SQR SAW]  .   .  (play cells)  x3  x2 TAP  <- waveform strip + polyrhythm
                                                               (see the polyrhythm section)
  row 1  DIST SLIDE  .  .  .                   .   .   .   <- per-grid toggles
  row 2   .  MONO .   .   .                    .   .   .   <- per-grid toggle
   ...                     (46-EDO play cells)
  row 13  .   .   .   .   .                    .   .   .
  row 14 FEET ERASE .  .  .  . . . . . . .  8vaDN UP  8vaUP  <- feet-accrete + erase + scroll pad
  row 15 CLR HOLD ACC  .  .  . . . . . . .  LEFT DOWN RIGHT  <- accrete + scroll pad
#+end_example

- The whole top row (0,0)..(15,0), the pad cells (13..15, 14..15), the accrete
  buttons (0..2, 15), erase (1,14), and the toggles -- distortion (0,1), slide
  (1,1), mono (1,2), feet-accrete (0,14) -- *occlude* the play grid: pressing them re-timbres / sets volume / scrolls /
  sustains / toggles rather than sounding a note.
- The scroll-pad corners follow the looper's convention: top-left = down an octave
  (notes sound lower), top-right = up an octave; arrows raise pitch down/right,
  lower it up/left. See =midi_pulse::edo_play=.

* LEDs

The play-grid LEDs are *register-aware* and *shared across both grids* (the visuals pass
-- see =TODO/3-surfaces-visuals/=):

- *Held notes (bright).* A held note lights all its octave-equivalents solid, computed
  through the *current* scroll register -- so shifting the window *moves* the lit cells to
  where that pitch now lives (the sounding pitch itself stays put; only the lights move).
- *Cross-grid reflection.* A note held on *either* grid lights on *both* grids; each grid
  places it under its own register, so the same note can appear at different cells on the
  two grids -- it shows where to finger to match the other surface.
- *Trails (dim).* Up to ~trail.max~ recent *pitch classes* (shared across both grids,
  octave-independent; 9 here) stay lit *dim* after release, until newer presses push them
  out. A trailed class that is also currently sounding shows *bright* (bright clobbers dim).
  Repeating a note keeps a single entry, and playing a note clears any trailed neighbour
  within ~1/trail.clobber_radius~ of an octave (1/27 here) -- so the dim backdrop stays
  sparse. Both are set in the ~[trail]~ table.
- *Waveform strip*: the selected waveform is bright, the other three dim; a grid's strip
  shows the waveform *it controls* (its own timbre, in this rig).
- *Scroll pad*: the four arrows are *dim*, the two octave corners dark. The pad fully
  occludes the edo grid beneath it -- no note or trail ever shows on a pad cell.
- *Toggles* (distortion / slide / mono / feet-accrete): *dim* at rest, *bright*
  while on. Every toggle is PER-GRID -- each grid's cell shows only its own
  switch; nothing mirrors.
- *Accrete buttons*: *dim* at rest (findable, like the arrows), *bright* when active --
  clear while pressed, needs-holding while on, accrete / erase while their condition is
  live (with both live, both light; erase wins behaviorally). Each grid's buttons show
  their OWN bank (per-monome, not mirrored). A *sustained*
  note still paints exactly like a fingered one -- bright octave-equivalents on BOTH
  grids (it is sounding) -- until its own grid's clear.

*Monobright vs varibright.* One of the two 16x16 grids (an old Series 256, id =m256-282=)
cannot dim a single LED -- it thresholds any level <= 7 to off. So its "dim" (unselected
waveform cells, trails, arrows) is *faked by flashing* the cell on/off fast (~1/32 duty,
~67 Hz) via whole-quad writes; the varibright grid (id =m0000102=) uses a native dim
level. The runtime decides per grid from the *serial id* (both report type "monome 256").
With many dim cells lit at once the flash slows into mild flicker on the monobright grid
-- an accepted trade.

* the waveform control (how to read it)

Each grid's strip re-timbres its *own* grid. In the rig this is the =controls=
field of the =waveform_selector= window:

#+begin_src toml
[[monome_windows]]        # on grid A
id = "wave-a"
monome = "a"
kind = "waveform_selector"
rect = [0, 0, 3, 0]
controls = "a"            # -> sets grid A's own timbre
#+end_src

So: press a cell on *grid A's* strip -> *grid A's* future notes use that waveform,
and the strip lights the chosen cell (a strip always *displays what it controls*).
The change is *radio* semantics: it affects future notes on the target grid; notes
already held keep their waveform.

Historical note: this rig originally *cross-controlled* (each strip set the OTHER
grid, 6_plan-style "edit X on the not-X surface"); per =TODO/misc.org= (2026-07)
the two-edo-grid rigs now self-control. The =controls= field still accepts any
play grid, so cross-wiring remains a rig choice, not a code change.

* sustain ("accrete")

Three buttons at each grid's bottom-left, driving one accrete *bank* per monome
(misc.org "two monome-specific accrete banks"): grid A's trio controls only grid
A's bank, only notes fingered on grid A can join it, and clear on grid B leaves
grid A's drones ringing. Requested in =TODO/misc.org= "sustain (accrete)".

| cell   | button        | on key-down                                  | LED                    |
|--------+---------------+----------------------------------------------+------------------------|
| (0,15) | clear         | silence + flush THIS grid's sustained set    | bright while pressed   |
| (1,15) | needs-holding | toggle how *accrete* and *erase* behave      | bright while on        |
| (2,15) | accrete       | hold / toggle accretion (see below)          | bright while accreting |
| (1,14) | erase         | hold / toggle erasure (see below)            | bright while erasing   |

- *needs-holding OFF* (the default): tapping *accrete* toggles "accrete mode". While
  it is on, every note played on this grid -- plus any that were held here when it
  turned on -- joins this bank's *sustained set*. Un-toggling stops additions;
  nothing leaves the set.
- *needs-holding ON*: notes fingered on this grid while *accrete* is physically held
  join the set (including notes pressed before the hold began -- they are captured
  at the moment the hold starts, or when they are released under a live hold).
- A note in the set keeps ringing after its finger lifts -- the very same voice, so
  the transition is seamless -- until this grid's *clear* flushes the bank or its
  *erase* removes that pitch (the per-note removal).
- *Retriggering a sustaining note replaces it*: fingering a pitch that is ringing in
  THIS grid's sustained set cuts the old drone at the strike -- no doubling -- and
  the new voice takes its place. The pitch keeps its set membership, so releasing
  the new finger re-drones it (accrete need not be live); you can re-articulate a
  drone without stacking or losing it.
- Toggling *needs-holding* cancels an active accrete OR erase mode (the set is
  untouched).
- *erase* (=(1,14)=): the accrete button's mirror image, under the same
  *needs-holding* rule -- hold it (needs-holding on) or tap it to toggle "erase
  mode" (needs-holding off). While the erasing condition is live, every pitch
  pressed on this grid LEAVES the sustained set; the note keeps sounding until its
  own finger lifts, then releases normally instead of re-droning. Notes already
  held when erase went live are removed the same way (still sounding under their
  fingers). When accrete and erase are both live, erase wins -- nothing is added.
  Erase has no pedal mirror (the softstep triple stays clear / needs-holding /
  accrete).
- The banks are fully independent: the other grid's trio, notes, and clear never
  touch this one. The trio LEDs show their own grid's bank only.
- Sustained voices follow the *volume fader* of the grid they came from, and paint
  bright on both grids like any sounding note (you hear them everywhere, so you see
  them everywhere).

* distortion

Each grid's =(0,1)= cell toggles distortion for THAT grid (key-down; key-up does
nothing; per-monome, not mirrored). A grid's voices -- fingered and sustained --
route to a *dirty bus* while its toggle is on; the dirty bus is summed and
distorted (so two switched-on grids intermodulate, as the old global mode did),
the switched-off grids' voices bypass the clipper entirely, and the two buses are
summed before the output clamp (inside the oversampling loop, so the distortion
harmonics don't alias). The drums bypass it always. The curve is the algebraic
soft-clipper =f(y) = y / (1 + |y/s|^k)^(1/k)=
([[../../../learnings/distortion.org]]); =s= and =k= are the sink's
=distortion_scale= / =distortion_shape= (defaults 1.0 / 2.0 -- a clearly-audible,
smooth saturation).

*The toggle changes timbre, not volume* (since 2026-07-10). A soft-clipper loses
level, and loses more of it the more notes you sum into it -- so switching it on used
to drop the volume, by an amount that moved with the chord. The sink's
=distortion_auto_makeup= (default on) restores the dirty bus to the RMS the clean bus
would have had, so the toggle is now purely a timbre change and note count affects
loudness exactly as much as it does undistorted. The grit still grows with the chord,
like an overdriven amp; only the level is held. =distortion_makeup= (default 1.0)
trims it by ear -- equal RMS is not equal *loudness*, since the distorted signal is
brighter, so ~0.8 may sit better. Set =distortion_auto_makeup = false= for the old
uncompensated clipper.

The correction is computed from the *voice envelopes*, not from an audio detector:
the renderer already knows each voice's instantaneous amplitude, so the dirty bus's
RMS is exact and free, recomputed every sample. It therefore never lags the 3 ms
attack, never ripples at the 80 Hz fundamental, and never flattens the pluck decay
into a compressor. Derivation, options and measured error bounds:
[[../../../learnings/distortion-volume-compensation.org]].

* slide and mono

*Slide* (=(1,1)=; key-down toggles; PER-GRID -- each grid's toggle governs its
own notes): each grid remembers its recently *released* notes (audible releases
only -- a note that became an accrete drone is still ringing and never a slide
source). When a new note is struck with that grid's slide on, the nearest
remembered pitch released within ~slide.candidate_window_ms~ (default 1000) is
consumed as the *source*: the new note's voice starts at the source pitch and
glides -- pitch-linearly -- into the struck pitch over ~slide.duration_ms~
(default 100). Ties break toward the more recent release; a source is never
reused.

*Mono* (=(1,2)=; key-down toggles; PER-GRID, like every toggle): a new note
cuts the same grid's other fingered notes. Cuts go through the ordinary release
path: under a live accrete condition a cut note still joins the sustained set.

*Mono + slide together* play like a true legato monosynth per grid: the new note
does not strike a fresh voice at all -- it STEALS the voice of the note it cuts,
gliding it into the new pitch while the envelope simply continues (no attack, no
pluck re-trigger; the stolen voice keeps its timbre and gain). If the cut note
becomes an accrete drone instead, it cannot be stolen -- the new note falls back
to the ordinary slide above (a fresh strike gliding from the nearest recent
release).

All four toggles -- distortion, slide, mono, feet-accrete -- are per-monome;
one grid can play as a legato monosynth while the other stays polyphonic.

* feet accrete

Each grid's =(0,14)= toggle (key-down flips; per-grid, NOT mirrored) hands that
grid's accrete trio to your feet: pedals *1 / 2 / 3* act as the *older*
(monobright) monome's *clear / needs-holding / accrete*, and pedals *8 / 9 / 0*
as the other monome's -- each triple in the trio's on-grid order, and each only
while its own grid's toggle is on. A mirroring pedal plays no sample; pedals 4-7
(the hats and toms) keep drumming, and a triple whose toggle is off drums as
usual -- so the softstep can accrete for one monome, both, or neither. Foot
lifts are real releases (the tether decoder reports them), so *holding* pedal 3
or 0 in needs-holding mode behaves exactly like holding that grid's accrete
button. Turn a toggle off and its triple is drums again.

* polyrhythm

The top-right 3x2 pad (both grids). The TAP button sets one global tempo; the
factor buttons and the =1 pulse switch belong to the grid you press them on:

| =(13,0)= x3 | =(14,0)= x2 | =(15,0)= tap |
| =(13,1)= /3 | =(14,1)= /2 | =(15,1)= =1  |

Full behavior -- the tap-pairing window, the per-grid 2^a x 3^b factor, the
=1 double-tap dance (start / snap-to-unity / stop), the unipolar-triangle
pulse, and the tap cell's unfactored blink display -- is the
~factored_pulse_pad~ window kind's spec (called ~tap_tempo_pad~ until 2026-07),
shared verbatim by every rig that has
one: [[file:README.org::*Monome windows][rigs/README.org "Monome windows"]].
On this rig the tap is shared by both grids (one global tempo) while each
grid keeps its own factor and cycling switch, so one grid can pulse at, say,
3/2 the rate of the other, or alone (see jeff-maybe-review-this.org).

* the KMSS drumkit

Identical to [[./kmss-drumkit_readme.org]] -- the same pedal->sample map, the same
tether-mode velocity path, the same =cpal_sampler= sink. The surfaces runtime
*consumes* the drumkit's bring-up (=drumkit_runtime::start=) rather than forking
it, so any future drum change (kit, decode, velocity feel) is inherited for
free. Pedal map (see =kmss-drumkit_readme.org= "the mapping" for the *ditto*
pad's semantics and the wood-block-at-8 / cowbell-at-9 rationale):

| foot  | pedal | voice                    |
|-------+-------+--------------------------|
| left  |     1 | snare                    |
| left  |     2 | kick                     |
| left  |     6 | high tom                 |
| left  |     7 | low tom                  |
| left  |     8 | wood block               |
| right |     4 | closed hat               |
| right |     5 | open hat                 |
| right |     9 | cowbell                  |
| right |     0 | crash (electronic)       |
| --    |     3 | ditto (repeats last hit) |

* robustness to missing gear

This rig brings up whatever is connected and loads *around* what is not, instead
of refusing to start (TODO.org "robust to missing gear"). On startup it lists, in
*red*, every component it skipped for a missing dependency, then plays on.

- *One grid unplugged.* The connected grid loads and plays normally (its own
  waveform strip, accrete bank, toggles, scroll pad, polyrhythm pad all work); the
  absent grid's surface + overlays are skipped and reported. Cross-grid reflection
  simply has nothing to reflect from the missing side. (These rigs self-control
  their strips, so the present grid keeps its own timbre control.)
- *No grids, SoftStep only.* The drumkit still comes up as a standalone velocity-
  sensitive sample trigger -- it never depended on the monomes. The feet-accrete
  toggles live on the (absent) grids, so they never turn on and all ten pedals
  drum. =Ctrl-C= restores the KMSS to standalone mode as always.
- *No SoftStep.* The two play grids load; the drumkit is skipped and reported. The
  feet-accrete toggles still light and flip, but with no pedals attached they do
  nothing (harmless).
- *Nothing connected.* The run errors out ("no gear present") rather than idling.

A *cross-controlling* strip (a ~waveform_selector~/~volume_strip~ whose ~controls~
names the OTHER grid) is a genuine functional dependency: if its target grid is
absent, the strip is dropped (its cells become play cells) and named in the red
report. The shipped rig self-controls, so this only bites a hand-wired cross rig.

* what is deliberately NOT here

- No looper (no slots / transport / loop display), no remap, no piano.
- No AM/FM on the play grids -- the strips control waveform + volume only. (The
  waveform-capable synth *can* do AM/FM; surfaces leaves those at their inert defaults.)
- No on-grid metronome. (The only flashing is the monobright grid faking its dim LEDs --
  see *LEDs* above -- not a clock.)
