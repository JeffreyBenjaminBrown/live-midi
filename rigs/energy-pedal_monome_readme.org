#+title: Monomes: coupled strings and an energy pedal

* Run
** in brief
on host:
  tools/pedals/bridge.sh
in docker:
  pw-jack cargo run --bin midi_pulse -- energy-pedal_monome
** for others
Connect one or two 16x16 monomes. For the energy pedal, run
=tools/pedals/bridge.sh= on the HOST first, in a terminal you
leave open, every session. Then, from the repository root inside
the container:

#+begin_src sh
pw-jack cargo run --bin midi_pulse -- energy-pedal_monome
#+end_src

Either physical grid can be used alone. The rig looks for the
pedal only at startup: if the bridge wasn't running then, it
prints a red "expression pedals skipped" line and plays on with
no pedal energy (every note is struck, nothing blooms) until
restarted. A working pedal prints "expression pedals: connected"
instead. Ctrl-C blanks the grids and stops audio.

* Layout
#+begin_example
       x=0                                 13    14    15
y=0  | . . . . . . . . . . . . .         SHIFT SHIFT SHIFT
y=1  | . . . . . . . . . . . . .         SHIFT SHIFT SHIFT
     |
     |                EDO PLAY SURFACE
     |
y=15 | . . . . . .  OFF  ON  TOGGLE  . . . . . . . . .
                    x=6  x=7  x=8
#+end_example
Everything but the shift pad and the three sustain buttons is
play surface: 41-EDO from 110 Hz, eight steps per cell rightward
and one step per cell downward. A control at rest flashes dim on
the shared 150 ms clock; a lit control is solid bright. A held
note is solid bright, and a sustained note no finger holds
flashes bright on that same clock.

Every note is a string. A key strikes it (as a piano hammer
does), and it rings on its own decay, longer in the bass than in
the treble. Every sounding string, held or sustained, shares one
bridge with every other, so a struck note sets the others
ringing in sympathy where their partials meet, in any tuning.
The sound passes through a resonating body.

* The pedal
The EX-P on MPC-20 channel 1 is the energy pedal. It adds energy
to a string around the moment its key goes down; the strike
itself is always there, at the same level for every key.
** the window
Each key-down has a window around it, shaped like a lopsided
triangle: it rises from nothing 300 ms before the key-down to
full weight at the key-down, then falls back to nothing 600 ms
after. Only the window decides which notes the pedal reaches: a
note is fed whether or not a finger still holds it, sustained or
not, and never after its window closes. Striking the key again
opens a new window. Notes outside their windows just ring.
** before the key: pre-energizing
The pedal's recent history counts. If the pedal was down in the
moments before you strike, the string starts with that energy
already in it, as if it had been bowed silently: you hear
nothing until the key goes down, and then the note starts
fuller. Energy from early in the window counts for less, since
the string would have partly rung it away.
** after the key: the bloom
After the key-down the pedal drives the string live, feeding the
string's own motion back into it, so the note swells from the
strike (and from whatever sympathetic ringing it has) rather
than starting anew. As the window closes the drive fades and
the note settles back to its own decay.
** heel and toe
At the heel the pedal adds nothing: notes are struck and decay
exactly as with no pedal. A little pedal lengthens a note's
sustain; more makes it swell; full toe at the key-down swells it
fastest, at the same rate in every register, up to a ceiling.
The ceiling rises with the pedal, so a light pedal blooms to a
low level and a full one to the full level.
** the exponent
The pedal's travel is raised to a power, 2 here, before it acts:
half pedal is a quarter of the energy. So the first part of the
travel gives fine control of light energy and the last part
does the most. =pedal_energy_exponent= in the rig changes it.
** knobs
The window's two legs, the exponent, the bloom's speed and
ceiling, and how much of the history the pre-charge takes are
in the rig's energy table, each with its range and its math.
Until the pedal first moves it puts in nothing, so an unplugged
pedal leaves plain struck strings. If the pedal arrives on
channel 2, change the =channel= in the rig.

* Sustain
Each grid has its own sustained set. OFF, ON and TOGGLE act on
their own grid only. A sustained string keeps ringing on its own
decay, and keeps taking part in the sympathy: a sustained chord
rings along with what you play over it.
** the handle: the cell below a note
Press the cell directly below a sounding note to toggle that
note's sustain. The press never sounds. Sustaining a held note
keeps it ringing after you lift your finger. Pressing the handle
of a sustained note un-sustains it, which ends it unless a
finger still holds it. The cell above a note is an ordinary
note.

The handle is geometric, so it stays put when the tuning
changes. With this tuning it is also the pitch one EDO step
above the note, which therefore can't be played from that cell
while the note sounds. A note on the bottom row, or directly
above OFF, ON or TOGGLE, has no handle. The rig can move the
handle to another side with =sustain_handle=.
** OFF
Ends every sustained note on this grid that no finger holds.
Held notes keep sounding, now unsustained.
** ON
Notes already held, and notes played while ON is live, join the
sustained set.
** TOGGLE WHAT ON MEANS
Bright means ON is momentary: hold it. Dim means ON latches: tap
it on, tap it off. It starts bright.

* Shift
The four arrow cells move the visible register one cell. The two
outer top corners move it one octave.

* Two options: the howl and the top register
Both live in the rig under "parameters you might also enjoy".
** the howl
=howl.gain= above 0 turns on an active loop: every sounding
string, summed, saturated, delayed a few milliseconds and fed
back into every string, as a guitar howls into its amplifier.
Above a threshold (about 1.5 to 3 at the defaults, depending on
the pitch) notes stop decaying and sustain for as long as they
are held or sustained; releasing still ends them. At 20 every
note howls. =howl.level= sets how loud they settle, and
=howl.delay_ms= which pitches howl readily. Caution: the howl
locks pitches. Among several notes the partials they share win
the saturation, and it pulls near-unisons, near-fifths and
near-octaves toward just ratios, which fights the EDO tuning,
the more the higher the gain. It is off as shipped.
** the top register
A note above =modal.crossover_hz= (2000 Hz as shipped; 0 turns
it off) is not a string of loops but a few resonators at the
string's partials, with the same decay, stiffness and level, and
it rings in sympathy, blooms under the pedal and howls like the
rest. Up there a string's period is only a couple of dozen
samples, too short for its filters, and the resonators stay
stable and in tune. The trade: harmonics. A modal note has at
most =modal.mode_count= partials and none near or above the top
of the audio band (six at 3000 Hz), no unison beating, and a
slightly brighter tail than a string at the same pitch. The
kind is chosen at the key-down, so a note keeps it through a
slide.

* Two more kinds of pedal energy: the bow and the wind
Set =kind= in the rig's energy table to ="bow"= or ="wind"=
instead of the shipped ="ebow"=, and the pedal bows the strings
or blows across them. Their own knobs are in a second energy
table, under "parameters you might also enjoy". The window, the
exponent and the pre-charge work the same for all three; the
bloom's speed and ceiling (=growth_rate_per_second_max= and
=drive_limit=) are the ebow's alone.
** the bow
The pedal sets the bow's speed: strongest at the key-down, fading
as the window closes, and a lighter pedal bows the same way,
softer. A bowed note has rosin bite and a bright, locked
spectrum, at about the same level in every register. Caution: the
bow has non-starts and squeaks at extreme settings. Whether it
speaks depends on =bow_velocity= times =bow_slipperiness=: below
about 0.7 the bow grips the string and damps it (the note dies
sooner than with no pedal), from about 1.5 the low strings squeak
(they lock onto their 4th partial), and from about 2.5 the bow
skates and adds little. As shipped the product is 0.9, which
speaks everywhere; =bow_velocity= alone is the loudness if you
keep the product.
** the wind
Noise blown across the string, as across an aeolian harp. The
partial nearest the wind's pitch blooms, and as the pedal rises
the wind's pitch climbs (to =wind_speed_hz_max= at full pedal,
2000 Hz as shipped), so the bloom moves up the harmonic series.
Caution: the wind blooms one partial, not the note. Under it a
note becomes a breathy whistle at one of its harmonics over the
strike (a 220 Hz note at full pedal whistles on its 9th), and a
low note spreads it over several close partials. =wind_gain=
sets how hard it blows; =wind_q= how narrow the band is.

* Hot reload
Edit the rig, then press =r= then Enter in the terminal running
it. The new values apply without a restart:
** at once, to strings already sounding
The string table (decay, the upper partials' decay, detune,
stiffness, dispersion), the bridge, the body (its resonators
restart from rest, a brief dip), the howl table,
=modal.mode_count=, =amplitude= and the tuning.
** to notes struck afterward
The exciter table (kind, pick energy, brightness, position,
hammer, knock), the energy table (the kind, the window, the
exponent, the drive, the pre-charge, the bow and the wind),
=unison_count= and
=modal.crossover_hz=.
** only after a restart
=sample_rate=, =buffer_frames=, =release_secs=, the grid layout,
the ports, and a change of sink kind.
A rig that fails to load or validate leaves the running values
in place and says why.

* Listening without grids
The harness renders scripted scenes with this rig's string
parameters to WAV files, to judge a parameter change by ear:

#+begin_src sh
cargo run -p tools --bin strings_harness -- --rig energy-pedal_monome
#+end_src

It writes =target/strings-harness/<scene>.wav= for each scene
(single notes across the range, a chord, a re-strike over a
sustained chord, a pedal swell, the same swell under the bow
and under the wind, sympathy, the chord with the howl on, and
three treble notes across the crossover) and prints each path
and peak. =--scene NAME= renders one, =--pedal TRACE= scripts
the pedal (='0:0,1:1'= rises from heel to toe over the first
second), =--howl GAIN= sets the howl's gain for every scene,
=--energy KIND= the energy kind (=ebow=, =bow= or =wind=),
=--out DIR= writes elsewhere; =--help= lists them all.

* Deliberately not here
No timbre keys and no volume keys (a string has no waveform, and
the pedal is not a volume pedal), no chord storage, no loops, no
tone-target cell, no edit mode, no SoftSteps, no square or
diamond dances.

A global volume pedal on the second EX-P can be added: put this
under the rig's "the energy pedal" headline, after the energy
pedal's own =ELEM=, without the two-space indent, and restart.
It is a live level over every note, held or sustained, on every
grid, and fades to silence at the heel (the volpedal rig's
=curve_*= knobs work on it too).

  **** PARAM channel = 2
  **** PARAM role = "global_volume"
#+end_example
