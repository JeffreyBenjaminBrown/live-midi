#+title: The edo editor: record, overdub and edit tracks

* Run
On the host, once per login, let the container open windows:
#+begin_src sh
xhost +local:
#+end_src
Then, from the repository root inside the container:
#+begin_src sh
pw-jack cargo run --release --bin editor -- songs/my-song
#+end_src
The song directory is created on the first change. Leave it out
to use 'songs/untitled'. Add '--rig NAME' to play another rig
live; the default is [[file:timbres-volume-scroll_41edo.org]].
One or two 16x16 grids work; a missing one is skipped. Closing
the window, ctrl-q or Ctrl-C ends the editor.

* What a song is
A song is a directory. Each track is one text file,
'<name>.track', and 'tracks.txt' lists the tracks top to bottom.
Every change is saved the moment you make it, so there is no
save command; ctrl-z takes a change back.
** a track file
#+begin_example
  # an edo-editor track; rigs/editor_readme.org describes the format
  muted no

  0.000 rig timbres-volume-scroll_41edo
  0.000 tuning 41 edo 110 hz
  0.000 timbre 1
  0.000 volume -12 dB
  1.250 note 37 for 0.650
  2.000 timbre 3
#+end_example
Each line after the header starts with a time in seconds.
*** notes
'note 37 for 0.650' sounds pitch 37 for 0.65 seconds. A pitch is
an integer, read in the tuning in force where the note starts:
pitch p sounds at the tuning's hz times 2^(p/edo).
*** settings
A setting holds from its line until the next line that changes
it.
**** rig
The rig whose synth plays the track: its timbres and envelope.
The rig file is read when the track plays, so editing the rig
changes how old tracks sound; their pitches stay put.
**** tuning
How the integer pitches become frequencies. A recording writes
the tuning of the grid it was played on, so retuning a rig later
does not move recorded notes.
**** timbre
A slot of the rig's timbre keys, 0 to 3 from the left.
**** volume
In dB.
*** editing by hand
You may edit a track file in Emacs while the editor is closed.
Lines may come in any order; the editor sorts them when it
saves.

** the boundaries file
A song's scale ghost section boundaries (see "Scale ghost
sections") are 'sections.boundaries': one time in seconds per
line, '#' starting a comment.

* The window
** layout
A status bar at the top shows the transport, the time, the
current track, the live rig and the last thing that happened.
Under it a ruler counts seconds. Each track has a lane: its
header on the left (name, note count, and the tuning and rig in
force at the lane's left edge), its notes on the right, higher
pitches higher. The strip along a lane's bottom shows the timbre
by its color and the volume by the height of its white line
(bottom -60 dB, top 0 dB). A gold line marks a later change of
rig or tuning. The red line is the playhead.
** keys
'?' covers the window with this list in a big font; '?' or
escape puts it away.
*** transport
**** space
Play from the playhead, or stop.
**** r
Record from the playhead into the current track.
**** o
Overdub parameters into the current track.
**** esc
Stop.
**** home
Move the playhead to the start.
**** b
Put a section boundary where the music is (or at the playhead),
also while recording.
**** [ and ]
Move the playhead to the previous or next section boundary;
while playing, the music jumps there.
**** { and }
Slower, faster: 0.25x to 2x, in steps. The status bar shows a
speed other than 1. Pitch does not change. A recording made
slowly is stored in song time, so it plays back faster at 1x.
**** backslash
Back to normal speed.
*** tracks
**** n
Add an empty track below the current one.
**** p
Open the track panel: see "The track panel".
**** f2
Rename the current track: type, then enter (escape cancels).
A name is letters, digits, '-', '_' and '.'.
**** ctrl-k
Delete the current track.
**** ctrl-e
Render the song to an mp3, '<song dir>/<song name>.mp3', in the
background; the status bar says when it is written. Muted tracks
stay silent. From a shell, without the window:
'cargo run --release --bin editor -- songs/my-song --render out.mp3'.
**** m
Mute the current track, or unmute it.
**** up, down
Choose the current track.
*** editing
**** drag
Select a region of time across one or more adjacent lanes. A
click without a drag moves the playhead and chooses the track.
**** ctrl-a
Select the whole of the selected tracks (or the current one).
**** 1, 2, 3, 4
Give the selection one timbre throughout: the timbre slot 0, 1,
2 or 3, left to right as on the grid. After the selection the
timbre goes back to what it was.
**** h
Step the selection's ghosts through while-sounding, no, and yes
again: see "Scale ghost sections".
**** ctrl-click a header
Add that track to the selection, or take it out, so a selection
can skip tracks.
**** ctrl-c, ctrl-x
Copy or cut the selection.
**** ctrl-v
Paste at the playhead, replacing what lies under the pasted
material. The first copied track goes into the current track;
each other one goes as far below it as it was copied from below
the first.
**** ctrl-shift-v
Paste at the playhead, moving everything after it right.
**** delete
Clear the selection: it becomes silence, nothing moves.
**** shift-delete
Remove the selected time: everything after it moves left.
**** ctrl-z, ctrl-y
Undo, redo.
*** view
**** the wheel
Over the lanes it scrolls time; over the headers it scrolls the
lanes; with ctrl it zooms.
**** + and -
Zoom in and out.
**** left, right
Scroll time.
*** rigs
**** g
Play the next rig the editor can play, live.
**** l
Read the live rig's file again, after you edit it.

* Recording
** what gets recorded
Record (r) starts the transport at the playhead and keeps the
notes you play, adding them to the current track's notes. Every
other track plays while you record. Stopping returns the
playhead to where the recording began, ready for another take.
** parameters
A timbre or volume key you press during a recording replaces
that parameter's old values from your first press until you
stop; afterwards the old values resume. A parameter the track
has never set takes the grid's value at the recording's start,
so a new track sounds the way you played it.
** overdubbing parameters
Overdub (o) plays the current track and records only the
parameters. Notes you play sound but are not kept. Press r or o
while playing to start recording there. A timbre or volume key
you press is heard at once on the track you are recording into,
not only on your own notes.
** two grids
If both grids play during a recording, the grid that acts first
records into the current track and the other into a new track
below it.
** the rig and tuning
Where a recording adds notes, the track takes the live rig and
tuning from the recording's start to its stop. Notes already
there in another tuning move to the nearest pitch of the new
one.

* Scale ghost sections
** what they are
A song may have one boundaries track, at the top, holding
nothing but moments: section boundaries. Between each two
adjacent boundaries lies a section. While the music (or the
stopped playhead) is in a section, the grids show a dim ghost of
every pitch that a note starting in that section plays, on any
unmuted track, in every octave. Ghosts don't flash; a ghost
being played flashes between dim and bright. Before the first
boundary and after the last there is no section and no ghosts.
** making and editing them
'b' puts a boundary where the music is, making the boundaries
track the first time. Select boundaries like notes, by dragging
across the boundaries lane: delete clears them, and cut, copy
and paste move them. Boundaries paste only into the boundaries
track, notes only into notes tracks. You can't record notes into
the boundaries track.

** turning ghosts down
A chromatic run in some track can fill the grid with ghosts and
say nothing. Every track has a 'ghosts' setting over time, like
its timbre, with three values.
*** yes
The default: the notes starting here cast ghosts, and flash
while they sound.
*** while-sounding
No ghosts; the notes still flash while they sound.
*** no
The grid shows nothing of these notes, sounding or not.
*** setting it
Select a region (or ctrl-a for whole tracks) and press 'h' to
step through the three. A lane darkens and hatches a region
whose ghosts are not 'yes': '/' lines for while-sounding, '\'
lines for no. In a track file it is a line like
'12.000 ghosts while-sounding'.
*** song-wide
The boundaries track has the same setting, which holds for every
track while the music is there: select a region of the
boundaries lane and press 'h'. The more hidden of the two wins.

* The track panel
'p' covers the window with the current track's parameters: name,
muted, ghosts, timbre, volume, rig and tuning (a boundaries
track has only name and ghosts). Up and down choose one; left
and right change it (shift for volume in 6 dB steps); enter
changes it too, or on the name, renames the track. Escape or 'p'
closes the panel.
** what a change applies to
If the selection covers the current track, a change applies to
the selection's time; otherwise to the whole track. The panel's
title says which. A value is the one at the start of that time,
marked "varies" if it changes inside it.
** the tuning
The tuning shows but cannot change here, because changing it
would move every recorded pitch.

* The editing rules
** a note belongs where it starts
A region holds the notes that start in it, at their full length,
so a copied phrase keeps its ringing last note. Clearing a
region also cuts short a note that started before it and would
sound into it.
** settings after an edit stay put
An edit never changes the settings after its region: if the
region held a timbre change, a change at the region's end
restores what was in force there.

* The monome
The keys play the live rig. A note you hold lights solid on
every cell that sounds its pitch in any octave (and see "Scale
ghost sections" for the dim ghosts). A note any
unmuted track is playing blinks the same way, on the cells
nearest its pitch in the grid's tuning. The selected
timbre key is bright; the other control keys are dim, except
the scroll pad's two octave keys, which work but stay dark. A
monobright grid fakes the dim keys by flashing them faintly, as
the surfaces runtime does.

* Not here yet
No sustain, no pedals, no loop playback, no metronome.

* For developers
The code is 'edo_surface/src/editor/'; its 'mod.rs' lists the
layers. Every layer below 'window' and 'grid_io' is pure and
tested without hardware; 'grid_io' is tested against the monome
mock. To look at a frame without a display:
#+begin_src sh
EDO_EDITOR_SNAPSHOT=/tmp/frame.png cargo test -p edo_surface --lib editor::view::tests::snapshot
#+end_src
To play it headless against the mock (see [[file:../MOCK-MONOME.org]]):
#+begin_src sh
cargo run --bin mock_monome -- --detector-port 17002
MIDI_PULSE_NO_AUDIO=1 MIDI_PULSE_DETECTOR_PORT=17002 cargo run --bin editor -- --headless
#+end_src
