the same tuning, the same two unpinned grids, the same sustain
handle one cell below each sounding note, the same three sustain
buttons and the same six-key shift pad. Every note is a modelled
string instead of a sawwave voice: struck, coupled to every
other sounding string through one bridge, radiated through a
body. Two EX-P pedals: the one on channel 1 puts energy into the
strings around each key-down and sets how hard a key strikes;
the one on channel 2 brings on the squeal, as a guitar brought
close to its amplifier (see "pedal connection"). No dim cell
circles a sustained note. There are no timbre keys, volume keys
or pedal toggles.
*** how this file is read
Only a headline that begins with 'PARAM', 'TABLE' or 'ELEM'
carries data. A 'PARAM' belongs to the nearest 'TABLE' or 'ELEM'
above it in the tree. Every other headline (such as "the math"
or "parameters we talked about having access to") only groups,
and every body (the lines under a headline) is a note the loader
ignores. So the grouping headlines and the notes may be moved,
renamed, added or deleted freely, as long as each 'PARAM' stays
under its 'TABLE' and each 'TABLE' under the sink's 'ELEM'. A
'TABLE' may even appear twice under one 'ELEM' (say, once under
each grouping headline); the two merge. A misspelled key is an
error at load, never silently ignored. A key left out takes the
engine's default ('StringsParams::default()' in
'edo_surface/src/strings/params.rs'); this file sets every one
explicitly, at that default, so each is here to read and edit.

*** the level and the damper
**** PARAM amplitude = 0.15
Unitless master level, in (0, 1]. Useful from about 0.05 to 0.4;
raise it if a single note is too quiet, lower it if chords hit
the clamp. Reloads live with 'r'.
***** the math
'out = clamp(body(amplitude * sum over voices of gain_v * sum
over the voice's loops of v_i), -0.95, 0.95)', where 'v_i' is
the wave a loop delivers to the bridge and 'gain_v' the voice's
envelope times its timbre, fader and pedal gains, as the sawwave
engine computes them. The clamp is the sawwave engine's too.
**** PARAM release_secs = 0.2
Seconds, > 0. The damper: how long a released note takes to fall
silent. Useful from 0.01 (a hard damper) to about 1 (a soft
one). A sustained note is not released and rings on its own
decay. Needs a restart to change.
***** the math
On key-up the voice's envelope 'env' ramps linearly from where
it is to 0 over 'release_secs', and the voice's output is scaled
by it. When 'env' reaches 0 the voice leaves the voice map and
its string leaves the engine, so it no longer rings in sympathy
with the others.
*** timbre, "familiar"
**** TABLE string
***** about
One string: how it decays over pitch, how many unison loops a
note has, and how stiff it is. Each loop is an extended
Karplus-Strong loop: a delay line, then a tuning allpass, the
dispersion chain, the loss filter and a DC blocker, after which
the wave arrives at the bridge and is written back.
A change here reaches every sounding string at once, except
'unison_count', which applies to notes struck afterward.
***** the math of one loop
The loop's round trip is 'P = sample_rate / f0' samples: 'N'
whole samples of delay plus the tuning allpass's fractional
delay 'D' in [0.5, 1.5), plus the phase delays at 'f0' of the
dispersion chain, the loss filter and the DC blocker. The tuning
allpass '(a + z^-1) / (1 + a z^-1)' takes whatever delay 'P'
leaves, its coefficient chosen to be exact at 'f0'
('a = sin((1 - D) w0 / 2) / sin((1 + D) w0 / 2)', 'w0 = 2 pi f0
/ sample_rate'), so the fundamental sounds at 'f0'. The DC
blocker 'y = k (x - x1) + R y1', 'k = (1 + R) / 2', has its
cutoff at 'f0 / 200'; the loss filter and the tuning absorb its
small loss and lead at 'f0'.
***** decay
****** PARAM t60_seconds_at_55hz = 20.0
Seconds, > 0. How long a 55 Hz note's fundamental takes to fall
60 dB against a rigid bridge: the low anchor of the decay curve.
Useful from about 2 (a guitar) to 30 (a long piano bass). Longer
is more sustain in the low register.
******* the math
The decay curve is a straight line in log-log:
'T60(f0) = t60_seconds_at_55hz * (t60_seconds_at_1760hz /
t60_seconds_at_55hz) ^ (log2(f0 / 55) / 5)', extrapolated beyond
the anchors (55 Hz and 1760 Hz are five octaves apart). At the
defaults it is 6.8 s at 110 Hz, 3.2 s at 440 Hz, 2.2 s at 880 Hz.
The loop's gain per round trip at 'f0' is then
'g = 10 ^ (-3 / (f0 * T60(f0)))', since 'f0 * T60(f0)' round
trips make a factor of 10^-3 (60 dB). The bridge adds its own
loss on top (see 'heaviness'): decay rates add, so the heard
'1 / T60' is the string's '1 / T60(f0)' plus the bridge's.
****** PARAM t60_seconds_at_1760hz = 3.0
Seconds, > 0. The same at 1760 Hz: the high anchor of the decay
curve. Useful from about 0.3 to 5. Longer is more ring in the
treble; set both anchors equal for a decay that does not depend
on pitch.
******* the math
'T60(f0)' as under 't60_seconds_at_55hz', with this as its value
at 1760 Hz. Its ratio to the low anchor sets the slope: at the
defaults 'T60' falls by a factor of 0.68 per octave.
****** PARAM high_frequency_t60_ratio = 0.5
Unitless, in (0, 1]. How much faster the upper partials die than
the fundamental. 1 keeps every partial ringing as long as the
fundamental (bright, metallic, Karplus-Strong-like); 0.1 is a
dark, quickly mellowing tail. Useful from 0.1 to 1.
******* the math
The loss filter is the one-pole 'H(z) = g (1 + a) / (1 + a
z^-1)', 'a' in [-0.99, 0]. Its gain at 'f0' is 'g'; at
'high_frequency_hz' it is 'g_hf = 10 ^ (-3 / (f0 * T60(f0) *
high_frequency_t60_ratio))', so a partial there decays with
'T60(f0) * high_frequency_t60_ratio'. 'a' is solved in closed
form from '|H(e^jw)|^2 = g^2 (1 + a)^2 / (1 + 2 a cos w + a^2)'
at the two frequencies. At the defaults a 4 kHz partial of a
110 Hz note decays in about 2 s. When one pole cannot reach the
request, 'a' stops at -0.99, and it is kept from a DC gain above
1: the string never gains energy at any frequency.
****** PARAM high_frequency_hz = 4000.0
Hz, > 0. The frequency at which 'high_frequency_t60_ratio' is
met: the reference of the high-frequency loss. Useful from about
2000 to 10000. Lower makes the ratio bite on lower partials.
******* the math
'H' meets 'g_hf' at 'w_hf = 2 pi high_frequency_hz /
sample_rate'. If the reference is at or above Nyquist, or at or
below the note's 'f0', the loss filter is flat ('a = 0') and
every partial decays with 'T60(f0)'.
***** unisons
Two or three strings per note, as on a piano. The bridge
couples them: when they move together they push the bridge and
lose energy into it fast (the prompt sound); when they move
against each other the bridge barely moves and they linger (the
aftersound). The slight detune trades energy between the two,
which is the beating. Both loops have the same loss; the
two-stage decay comes from the bridge.
****** PARAM unison_count = 2
Count, 1 to 3. Loops per note. 1 is one clean string; 2 or 3
beat and give the two-stage decay. Each loop costs about as much
as the note did with one. Applies to notes struck afterward.
******* the math
The note's excitation, pre-charge and output are shared out:
each loop takes '1 / unison_count' of the excitation table and
of the pre-charge, and the voice's output is the sum of its
loops. Each loop is its own member of the bridge. The energy
pedal drives all of a note's loops with one drive (see the
energy table).
****** PARAM unison_detune_cents = 1.2
Cents, in [0, 100]. How far apart the unisons are tuned. 0 is
no beating; 0.2 to 2 is piano-like; above about 5 it is a
chorus, then an out-of-tune double.
******* the math
The k-th of 'unison_count' loops (k from 0) sounds at
'f0 * 2 ^ ((k - (unison_count - 1) / 2) * unison_detune_cents /
1200)', symmetric about 'f0'. Two loops 'c' cents apart at 'f0'
beat at about 'f0 * c / 1731' Hz: 0.15 Hz for 1.2 cents at
220 Hz.
***** stiffness
A stiff string's partials run sharp of exact harmonics, more so
the higher the partial: the piano's stretched, slightly bell-like
color. A nylon or gut string is nearly perfectly harmonic.
****** PARAM inharmonicity_b_at_55hz = 2e-5
Unitless, in (0, 0.1]. The stiffness 'B' at 55 Hz: the low
anchor of the stiffness curve. Useful from 1e-5 (barely) to
1e-3 (a short, stiff bass string).
******* the math
A stiff string's n-th partial sits at
'f_n = n * f0 * sqrt(1 + B * n^2)'. At 'B = 1e-4' the tenth
partial is 8.6 cents sharp; at 5e-3 the third is 38 cents sharp.
'B(f0) = inharmonicity_b_at_55hz * (inharmonicity_b_at_1760hz /
inharmonicity_b_at_55hz) ^ (log2(f0 / 55) / 5)', a log-log line
like the decay curve. Where 'B(f0)' is at or below the audible
threshold 'exp(2.54 ln(f0) - 24.6)' (5.5e-7 at 55 Hz, 1.1e-4 at
440 Hz, 3.6e-3 at 1760 Hz) the dispersion chain is left out.
****** PARAM inharmonicity_b_at_1760hz = 3e-4
Unitless, in (0, 0.1]. The stiffness 'B' at 1760 Hz: the high
anchor. Useful from 1e-4 to 2e-2. Piano treble strings are stiff
for their length, so the curve usually rises with pitch.
******* the math
'B(f0)' as under 'inharmonicity_b_at_55hz', with this as its
value at 1760 Hz. At the defaults the threshold overtakes 'B'
a little above 2 kHz, so the top of the range is harmonic.
****** PARAM dispersion_sections = 4
Count, 0 to 16. First-order allpass sections in each loop's
dispersion chain. 0 removes stiffness entirely; more sections
stretch more partials accurately and cost a little more. Useful
from 2 to 8.
******* the math
'M = dispersion_sections' identical sections
'(a + z^-1) / (1 + a z^-1)' with Rauhala and Välimäki's
coefficient: with 'Bc = max(B(f0), 1e-6)' and the piano key
number 'Ikey = 12 log2(f0 / 27.5) + 1',
'kd = exp(-0.00179 ln(Bc)^2 - 0.0233 ln(Bc) - 2.93)',
'Cd = exp((0.0126 ln(M) + 0.0606) ln(Bc) - 0.00825 ln(M) +
1.97)', 'D = exp(Cd - Ikey * kd)', 'a = (1 - D) / (1 + D)'. The
chain's phase delay at 'f0' is subtracted from the loop's delay,
so the fundamental stays in tune while the upper partials, which
the chain delays less, run sharp. The chain is also dropped when
it would leave the tuning allpass less than its minimum delay
(only at very high pitch).
**** TABLE exciter
***** about
What starts a note: the "pick energy" of the discussion. At
key-down a table is read into the note's loops, one sample per
sample, as the plectrum or hammer would push the string. A
change here applies to notes struck afterward.
***** PARAM kind = "strike"
An enum: "pluck" or "strike". A pluck is a plectrum or finger:
noise, bright and breathy. A strike is a hammer: a few smooth
force pulses, round and piano-like.
****** the math
'pluck': one period ('P = sample_rate / f0' samples, rounded) of
white noise in [-1, 1) from a fixed seed. 'strike':
'strike_pulses' raised-cosine pulses 'sin^2', each 'w =
round(hammer_contact_fraction * P)' samples wide, the j-th
starting at '2 j w', in a table one period long. Either table
then goes through the lowpass ('brightness') and the comb
('position') and is scaled to peak 'pick_energy'. The same
parameters and pitch give the same table every time.
***** PARAM pick_energy = 0.5
Loop amplitude units, in (0, 1]. How hard a key strikes its
string: the peak of the excitation table. The monome has no
velocity, so this is every note's attack level; the energy
pedal adds to it. Useful from 0.1 to 0.8. In this rig the
channel 1 pedal's target moves it from 0.15 to 0.8 (see "pedal
connection"); this value plays until that pedal first reports.
****** the math
The table is normalized so its largest sample is 'pick_energy'
and each of the note's loops takes '1 / unison_count' of it. The
knock is scaled by it too (its energy is '(knock_mix *
pick_energy)^2'). What is heard is the loops' sum times
'amplitude', so a single note peaks near 'pick_energy *
amplitude' before the body.
***** the tone of the attack
****** PARAM brightness = 16.0
Unitless, > 0. The excitation's lowpass cutoff in multiples of
the note's pitch. 1 to 2 is a soft felt or a thumb; 6 to 12 a
hard hammer or a pick; above about 20 it is unfiltered. Higher
is a brighter attack.
******* the math
'y = (1 - p) x + p y1', 'p = exp(-2 pi fc / sample_rate)', with
'fc = min(brightness * f0, 0.45 * sample_rate)'. The knock
passes the same lowpass at the fixed 'fc = brightness * 110' Hz,
since the knock is shared by every note.
****** PARAM position = 0.2
Fraction of the string's length from the bridge, in (0, 0.5],
at which it is plucked or struck. Near 0.5 (the middle) is
hollow, like a clarinet; near 0.1 is full and bright; very near
0 is thin and nasal. A piano hammer sits at about 1/8.
******* the math
The comb 'y[n] = x[n] - x[n - round(position * P)]', applied
around the one-period table. The n-th harmonic is scaled by
'2 |sin(pi n position)|', so every harmonic that is a multiple
of '1 / position' vanishes: at 0.125, the 8th, 16th, and so on.
***** the hammer, for a strike only
****** PARAM strike_pulses = 1
Count, 1 to 8. Force pulses of a strike: a hammer that bounces
on the string before it leaves. 1 is one clean blow; 2 or 3 a
slightly buzzy, piano-like contact. Ignored by a pluck.
******* the math
The pulses each last 'w' samples, separated by gaps of 'w', so
the train spans '(2 * strike_pulses - 1) * w' samples, which
validation keeps within one period.
****** PARAM hammer_contact_fraction = 0.12
Fraction of one period that each pulse lasts, > 0, with
'(2 * strike_pulses - 1) * hammer_contact_fraction' at most 1.
Wider is a softer hammer with fewer high partials. Useful from
0.03 to 0.3. Ignored by a pluck.
******* the math
'w = round(hammer_contact_fraction * P)'. A raised-cosine pulse
of width 'w' has little energy above about '2 / w' cycles per
sample, so at 0.12 the pulses alone roll off above about the
16th harmonic, before the lowpass.
***** the knock
The body's own short response to being hit, heard at the attack
as the thump of wood. It is cut from the body's resonators (see
the body table), so a different body knocks differently.
****** PARAM knock_mix = 0.1
Unitless, in [0, 1]. How loud the knock is beside the string's
own excitation. 0 is no knock; 0.5 a pronounced one.
******* the math
The knock table is the body's impulse response, windowed and
lowpassed, normalized to unit energy (sum of squares 1). Each
note scales it at birth by 'knock_mix * pick_energy', so the
note's knock has energy '(knock_mix * pick_energy)^2', and
streams it into its loops in parallel with the dry table from
key-down. So a change to 'knock_mix' or 'pick_energy' reaches
the next note without rebuilding the table.
****** PARAM knock_ms = 60.0
Milliseconds, in (0, 1000]. How long the knock lasts. 20 is a
tight click; 150 a boomy thud.
******* the math
The table is 'knock_ms' long, under the window
'exp(-t / (knock_ms / 6.9))', which falls 60 dB over 'knock_ms'.
**** TABLE energy
***** about
How the energy pedal puts "bow energy" into a string: the energy
that, unlike the pick energy, arrives continuously. Around each
key-down there is a window shaped like an asymmetric triangle:
0 at key-down minus 'ex_ante_energizing_ms', rising linearly to
1 at key-down, falling linearly to 'ex_post_energizing_floor'
(0 as shipped) at key-down plus 'ex_post_energizing_ms', and
staying there. Times the pedal's position raised to
'pedal_energy_exponent', it is how much energy enters the string
at each moment. Before key-down the string is moving but its mic
is off: at key-down the engine adds up what the pedal's recent
history would have put into it (the pre-charge). After key-down
the pedal drives the string live (the drive) until the window
closes, which with a floor above 0 it never does. Only the
window decides: a note is driven whether or not a finger still
holds it, sustained or not, and never after its window; striking
the key again opens a new window. The pedal
reads 0 at the heel and 1 at the toe, and counts as 0 until it
first moves. A change here applies to notes struck afterward.
***** the math of the window
For a note struck at time 'T', with 'a = ex_ante_energizing_ms'
and 'b = ex_post_energizing_ms' (in seconds):
'tri(t) = (t - (T - a)) / a' on '[T - a, T]',
'tri(t) = F + (1 - F) (1 - (t - T) / b)' on '[T, T + b]', 'F'
after it, and 0 before 'T - a', with
'F = ex_post_energizing_floor'.
The energy factor is 'f(t) = tri(t) * pedal(t) ^ e', with
'e = pedal_energy_exponent'.
***** PARAM kind = "ebow"
****** about
An enum: how the drive puts energy in, "ebow", "bow" or "wind".
The ebow is a magnet under the string that feeds the string's own
motion back into it, so the note blooms from what it already
holds (the strike, a sympathetic ring) without squeaks or
non-starts; it reads 'growth_rate_per_second_max' and
'drive_limit'. The bow rubs the string at a speed the pedal sets:
rosin bite and a locked, bright spectrum, but it can fail to
speak or squeak; it reads 'bow_velocity' and 'bow_slipperiness'.
The wind blows noise across the string and the partial nearest
its pitch blooms, climbing the series as the pedal rises; it
reads 'wind_speed_hz_max', 'wind_q' and 'wind_gain'. All three
read the window, 'pedal_energy_exponent' and 'precharge_gain'.
The bow's and the wind's parameters are under 'parameters you
might also enjoy', in a second energy table.
****** the math
The drive, per sample, during '[T, T + b]': for the ebow see
'drive_limit' and 'growth_rate_per_second_max', for the bow and
the wind the math under their own parameters. The pre-charge is
the same 'A0' for every kind (see 'ex_ante_energizing_ms'); its
waveshape, one delay line's worth scaled to the peak
'A0 / unison_count', is the kind's own. The ebow's is nearly a
sine, as a string under a magnet settles:
'sin(2 pi f0 t) + 0.2 sin(4 pi f0 t)'. The bow's is the sawtooth
'2 frac(f0 t) - 1' passed once through the loop's own loss
filter: a bowed string's spectrum, dulled as one round trip
dulls it. The wind's is a sine at the partial 'k f0' (k >= 1)
nearest the wind's pitch 'f_s' at key-down (see
'wind_speed_hz_max').
***** the window
****** PARAM ex_ante_energizing_ms = 100.0
Milliseconds, in [0, 10000]. How far back before key-down the
pedal's history counts. With the pedal already down when a key
is struck, the note starts with energy in it, as if it had been
bowed silently. 0 turns the pre-charge off. Useful from 50 to
2000.
******* the math
The pre-charge, computed once at key-down from the pedal's
history kept at one point per millisecond:
'A0 = precharge_gain * sum over the points t_j in [T - a, T] of
dt * tri(t_j) * pedal(t_j) ^ e * exp(-(T - t_j) / tau)', with
'dt = 0.001' s and 'tau = T60(f0) / ln(1000)' the string's own
amplitude time constant, so energy put in early has partly
decayed by key-down. Each loop then gets one delay line's worth
of the kind's waveshape, scaled to the peak 'A0 / unison_count',
added on top of the strike.
****** PARAM ex_post_energizing_ms = 300.0
Milliseconds, in [0, 60000]. How long after key-down the pedal
keeps driving the note, its weight falling linearly to 0. Short
(100 to 300) is a swell on the attack; long (1000 to 5000) lets
the pedal sustain and swell a note well after it is struck. 0
turns the drive off.
******* the math
The drive acts while 'tri(t) > 0' after 'T', with
'tri(t) = 1 - (t - T) / b'. Under a constant full pedal the
drive's growth summed over the leg, before the limit, is
'(growth_rate_per_second_max - lambda) * b / 2' nepers ('lambda'
the string's own decay rate, below): about 17 dB at 220 Hz with
the defaults.
****** PARAM ex_post_energizing_floor = 0.0
Unitless, in [0, 1]. Where the ex-post leg ends: over
'ex_post_energizing_ms' the window falls from 1 to this, and
stays there. 0 closes the window at the end of the leg. Above 0
it never closes, so the pedal keeps feeding a note for as long
as the note sounds. Useful from 0 to about 0.3. A voice keeps
the energy parameters of its birth, so a change here (or a pedal
target on it) shapes the notes struck afterward, not the
sounding ones.
******* the math
'tri(t) = F + (1 - F) (1 - (t - T) / b)' on '(T, T + b]' and
'F' after it, with 'F' this floor (see "the math of the
window"). After the leg the energy factor is
'F * pedal ^ pedal_energy_exponent' for as long as the note
sounds. The ebow's rate at factor 'f' is
'growth_rate_per_second_max * f - lambda * (1 - f)' (see
'growth_rate_per_second_max'), with 'lambda = ln(1000) /
T60(f0)'. So once 'F * pedal ^ e' exceeds 'lambda /
(growth_rate_per_second_max + lambda)', a note under a held
pedal grows to its limit ('drive_limit') instead of settling.
With this file's decay curve (20 s at 55 Hz, 1.5 s at 1760 Hz)
that threshold is about 0.04 at 55 Hz, 0.11 at 220 Hz and 0.37
at 1760 Hz; at the engine's defaults (10 s at 55 Hz) about
0.08, 0.16 and 0.37. The bridge's drain raises them a little.
Below the threshold a floor lengthens the notes the pedal
holds without making them grow.
******* the trade
Above the threshold, sustained notes under a held pedal never
die on their own: only a release, OFF or the pedal's heel ends
them. The pedal's history still shapes the attack as before.
***** PARAM pedal_energy_exponent = 2.0
Unitless, > 0. The pedal's curve. 1 is linear in the pedal's
travel; above 1 the first part of the travel does little and the
last part much, which gives fine control of light energy; below
1 a little pedal goes a long way. Useful from 1 to 4.
****** the math
The pedal's position 'p' (0 at the heel, 1 at the toe) enters as
'p ^ pedal_energy_exponent' everywhere: in the energy factor
'f = tri * p ^ e' of the pre-charge and of the drive's growth
rate, and in the drive's limit 'A = drive_limit * p ^ e'. At 2,
half pedal is a quarter of the energy.
***** the drive
****** PARAM growth_rate_per_second_max = 8.0
Nepers per second, >= 0. The ebow's only: how fast a note grows
under a full pedal at the window's peak. 8 nepers per second is
about 69 dB per second. The same pedal grows every register at
the same rate. Useful from 2 (a slow bloom) to 20 (a snap).
******* the math
The pedal moves the growth rate from the string's own decay to
this: 'rate = growth_rate_per_second_max * f - lambda * (1 -
f)', with 'lambda = -f0 ln(g) = ln(1000) / T60(f0)' (about 1.5
nepers per second at 220 Hz). The drive gain per round trip is
'k = max(0, exp(rate / f0) - g)': added to the loop's own 'g',
it makes the amplitude grow by 'rate' nepers per second. At the
heel 'f = 0', 'rate = -lambda' and 'k = 0', so the string decays
exactly as undriven; a light pedal lengthens the sustain; past
the point where 'rate' crosses 0 the note grows.
****** PARAM drive_limit = 0.8
Loop amplitude units, > 0. The ebow's only: the ceiling the bloom
flattens at, at full pedal; a lighter pedal blooms to a
proportionally lower ceiling. No pedal can make a string run
away. A bowed note settles where the bow's speed puts it, and a
blown one where the wind's gain does. Useful from 0.1
to 1.5; the output clamps at 0.95 after 'amplitude' and the body.
******* the math
One drive sample per note, added to every one of its loops:
'drive = k * A * tanh(v / A)', with 'v' the mean of the note's
loops' arriving waves and 'A = drive_limit * p ^ e'. One drive
for all the unisons, as one magnet under the whole course, keeps
them in phase while they grow. Below 'A' the drive is about
'k * v' and the note grows exponentially at 'rate'; above it the
drive adds at most 'k * A' per sample, so the growth turns at
most linear and the note levels off where the string's loss
removes what the drive adds (for a sine, about '(4 / pi) * k *
A / (1 - g)'). As 'tri' falls, 'k' falls and the note settles
back toward its own decay.
***** PARAM precharge_gain = 0.5
Per second of full-pedal triangle, >= 0. How much of the pedal's
recent history is in the note at key-down. 0 turns the
pre-charge off. With the defaults a full pedal held through the
whole 300 ms ex-ante leg pre-charges a 220 Hz note to a peak of
about 0.065, against a 'pick_energy' of 0.5; about 4 makes the
pre-charge as loud as the strike. Useful from 0 to 5.
****** the math
'A0' (see 'ex_ante_energizing_ms') is proportional to this. For
a constant full pedal over a leg 'a' short beside 'tau',
'A0' is about 'precharge_gain * a / 2'.
**** TABLE bridge
***** about
The one bridge every sounding string meets. Each string hands
the bridge the wave arriving at it, and takes back that wave
minus the bridge's motion, so every sounding string hears every
other: sympathetic ringing, between any pitches, in any tuning.
Only sounding strings take part: a held or sustained note rings
in sympathy, an unplayed key has no string. A change here
reaches every sounding string at once.
***** PARAM heaviness = 3e3
Unitless, > 0. The bridge's impedance over one string's. A
heavy bridge (large) barely moves: weak sympathy and long notes,
as on a piano. A light bridge (small) moves freely: strong
sympathy, and notes that drain into it fast. The two come
together; there is no strong sympathy with long notes. Useful
from about 100 (a light, buzzy box) to 10000 (strings nearly
independent).
****** the math
With 'N' loops sounding in the engine and 'r = heaviness', the
bridge's velocity each sample is
'v_b = L((2 / (N + r)) * sum over every loop of v_i)', and loop
i writes back 'v_i - v_b' (plus its excitation and drive). 'L'
is the lowpass of 'damping_hz'. The bridge never adds energy for
any 'r > 0'. Being a real number, the impedance is a resistance:
per round trip a lone loop keeps '1 - 2 / (N + r)' of its
amplitude, and the in-phase motion of a note's 'n' unisons keeps
'1 - 2 n / (N + r)', which is a bridge decay time of about
'T60_bridge = ln(1000) * (N + r) / (2 n f0)': more round trips
per second, so it grows with pitch. A two-unison note alone at
'r = 3000' loses 60 dB to the bridge in about 46 s at 110 Hz,
13 s at 440 Hz and 5.5 s at 1760 Hz (measured; at the top the
damping lowpass softens it); at 'r = 300', in about 4.7 s, 1.5 s
and 0.8 s; at 'r = 50', in about 1.1 s and 0.5 s. Decay rates
add: the heard '1 / T60' is the string's '1 / T60(f0)' plus
'1 / T60_bridge'. So the decay curve of the string table means
the string against a rigid bridge, and a lighter bridge shortens
every note, the high ones most. A silent string hears the others
through '-v_b': each round trip it takes '2 / (N + r)' of their
summed waves, which builds up only where its partials meet
theirs.
***** PARAM damping_hz = 1.5e4
Hz, > 0. The cutoff of a lowpass on the bridge's motion. Lower
keeps the coupling (and the drain into the bridge) to the low
partials; higher lets the upper partials ring in sympathy too.
Useful from 1000 to 20000 (it is kept below Nyquist).
****** the math
'L(z) = (1 - p) / (1 - p z^-1)', 'p = exp(-2 pi damping_hz /
sample_rate)', gain 1 at DC. Above the cutoff the bridge passes
less of the summed wave, so those frequencies neither couple nor
drain as much.
**** TABLE body
***** about
The resonating body on the summed output: what turns strings
into an instrument. A bank of resonators, like the modes of a
soundboard or a guitar top, colors everything and, through the
knock, the attack. It is outside the strings, so it cannot make
them unstable. A change to 'mix' moves at once and rebuilds
nothing. A change to any other key here rebuilds the body at
once (its resonators start from rest, a brief dip) and rebuilds
the knock for notes struck afterward.
***** PARAM mix = 0.3
Unitless, in [0, 1]. How much of the output is the body's. 0 is
the bare strings; 1 only the body.
****** the math
'out = (1 - mix) * dry + mix * wet / norm', with 'dry' the
strings' sum, 'wet' the bank's output, and 'norm' the bank's RMS
gain on white noise (measured once per parameter change), so the
body leaves a broadband signal at the level it came in. 'mix 0'
is exactly the dry sum.
***** the modes
****** PARAM mode_count = 48
Count, 1 to 128. Resonators in the bank. A few is a small,
boxy, colored body; 20 to 60 a full one. Useful from 8 to 64.
******* the math
The k-th of 'M = mode_count' centers is
'f_k = lowest_hz * 2 ^ (k * s + j_k)', with
's = log2(highest_hz / lowest_hz) / (M - 1)' octaves between
neighbors and 'j_k' a jitter of up to '0.3 * s' either way from
'seed', so the modes do not fall on a regular grid. Each mode's
gain is '1 / sqrt(f_k / lowest_hz)', falling toward the top.
Modes at or above 0.45 times the sample rate are left out.
****** PARAM lowest_hz = 90.0
Hz, > 0. The lowest mode's nominal center: the body's deepest
resonance. A guitar's air mode is near 100 Hz; a piano
soundboard goes lower. Useful from 50 to 300.
******* the math
'f_0 = lowest_hz' before jitter; the gains are relative to it.
****** PARAM highest_hz = 6000.0
Hz, above 'lowest_hz'. The highest mode's nominal center. Lower
is a darker body. Useful from 2000 to 12000.
******* the math
'f_(M-1) = highest_hz' before jitter.
****** PARAM q = 12.0
Unitless, > 0. Each mode's sharpness. Low (5 to 15) is a damped,
smooth body; high (50 to 200) a ringing, resonant one whose
modes can be heard as pitches. Useful from 10 to 100.
******* the math
Mode k has bandwidth 'f_k / q': poles at radius
'R = exp(-pi f_k / (q * sample_rate))' and angle
'2 pi f_k / sample_rate', in the resonator
'H(z) = ((1 - R^2) / 2) (1 - z^-2) / (1 - 2 R cos(theta) z^-1 +
R^2 z^-2)'. It rings with a T60 of about '2.2 q / f_k' seconds:
0.33 s for a 200 Hz mode at 'q = 30'.
****** PARAM seed = 1
Any integer, >= 0. Picks the jitter of the mode centers: another
seed is another body of the same size and shape. Try a few.
******* the math
The seed of the deterministic noise that draws each 'j_k'.
*** timbre, exotic
**** DONE about

two more ways for the energy pedal to drive a string, chosen by
the energy table's 'kind'. The howl is an active path that can
sustain every note forever; it is off as shipped. The modal

howl or the modal table reaches sounding strings at once, except
the crossover, which applies to notes struck afterward; a change
to the bow or the wind applies to notes struck afterward.
**** TABLE energy
***** DONE about
The rest of the energy table: the bow's and the wind's
parameters, read only when 'kind' (under 'parameters we talked
about having access to') is "bow" or "wind". The two energy
tables are one table to the engine.
***** the bow
****** DONE about
A bow drawn across the string at a speed the pedal and the window
set: strongest at the key-down, fading as the window closes, a
lighter pedal bowing the same way, softer. It speaks at about
the same level in every register, with rosin bite and a bright,
locked spectrum, but only inside a window of its two settings:
outside it the bow grips and damps the string, squeaks, or
skates (see 'bow_slipperiness').
****** the math of the bow
With 'f = tri(t) * p ^ e' the energy factor, the bow moves at
'v_bow = bow_velocity * f'. With 'v' the string's speed under the
bow (the mean of the note's loops' arriving waves) and 'delta =
v_bow - v', one drive sample per note is added to every one of
its loops: 'delta * bow', where 'bow = clamp((|delta / f *
bow_slipperiness| + 0.75) ^ -4, 0.0025, 0.245)' is the friction
table (STK's, between the bounds of Mutable Instruments
Elements'), the result passed through a one-pole highpass at 'f0
/ 10'. While '|delta / f * bow_slipperiness|' is below about 0.67
the table sits at its top and the bow grips, pulling the string
toward its own speed, which damps it. Beyond, the table falls
steeply and the bow slips; there the drive shrinks as 'delta'
grows, which feeds the string's motion until the grip holds it.
The table reads 'delta / f', not 'delta': the bow's force falls
with its speed, as a player keeps a bow inside its playable
window, so a lighter pedal and the closing window make the same
motion smaller. (Read at 'delta', a slowing bow would grip and
stop every string near the window's end.) The highpass keeps the
friction from pushing the string's slow, near-DC motion.
'drive_limit' does not apply.
****** PARAM bow_velocity = 0.3
Loop velocity units, in (0, 2]. The bow's speed at full pedal and
the window's peak: how loud a bowed note is. At 0.3 a softly
struck 220 Hz note bowed at full pedal peaks near 0.19 (body
off), where the shipped strike alone peaks near 0.08. Useful from
0.1 to 0.6.
Changing it alone also moves whether the bow speaks: see
'bow_slipperiness'.
******* the math
'v_bow = bow_velocity * f'. With 'bow_velocity *
bow_slipperiness' held, the bowed motion is the same shape at any
'bow_velocity' and scales with it (the strike does not).
****** PARAM bow_slipperiness = 3.0
Unitless, in [1, 10]. The friction table's slope: how small a
difference between the bow's speed and the string's already
makes the bow slip. Larger slips sooner: a lighter grip. Its
product with 'bow_velocity' decides whether the bow speaks.
Below about 0.7 the bow grips throughout and damps the note
instead of driving it (a non-start: the note dies faster than
under no pedal). From about 0.8 to 2 it speaks, at
about the same level from 55 to 1760 Hz (0.9 as shipped). From
about 1.5 the low strings lock onto their 4th partial (a
squeak). From about 2.5 the bow slips throughout and adds little
(it skates), the low strings first.
******* the math
'bow = clamp((|x| + 0.75) ^ -4, 0.0025, 0.245)' with
'x = delta / f * bow_slipperiness'. The table leaves its top at
'|x| = 0.245 ^ (-1/4) - 0.75 = 0.67'. With the string at rest
under the bow, 'delta / f = bow_velocity', so
'x = bow_velocity * bow_slipperiness', and at or below 0.67 the
bow grips until something else moves the string: about 0.7 speaks
after the shipped strike, not after a soft one.
***** the wind
****** DONE about
Wind across the string, as across an aeolian harp: the partial
nearest the wind's pitch gathers the noise and blooms, and as
the pedal rises the wind's pitch climbs, so the bloom moves up
the harmonic series. It blooms one partial, not the note: a
breathy whistle at one harmonic over the strike. On a low note
several close partials share it. At the heel there is no wind.
****** the math of the wind
White noise in [-1, 1), each note its own (seeded from the note),
passes the resonator
'H(z) = ((1 - R^2) / 2) (1 - z^-2) / (1 - 2 R cos(theta) z^-1 +
R^2 z^-2)' (the body's mode filter, peak gain about 1) at the
wind's pitch 'f_s = wind_speed_hz_max * p ^ e', clamped to
[20 Hz, 0.45 * sample_rate], of bandwidth 'f_s / wind_q':
'R = exp(-pi f_s / (wind_q * sample_rate))',
'theta = 2 pi f_s / sample_rate'. The filter is redesigned, its
state kept, when 'f_s' moves by more than 1 percent. The result,
scaled by 'tri(t) * wind_gain', is one drive sample per note,
added to every one of its loops. The noise does not depend on
the string's motion; a partial near 'f_s' takes it in at every
round trip and keeps it for its own decay time, so the longer it
rings the higher it blooms. 'drive_limit' does not apply.
****** PARAM wind_speed_hz_max = 2000.0
Hz, > 0. The wind's pitch at full pedal, so which partial blooms:
at 2000 a 220 Hz note blooms its 9th partial, a 55 Hz note a
cluster near its 36th. As the pedal falls the pitch falls with
'p ^ e', and the bloom moves down the series. Useful from 200 to
8000.
******* the math
'f_s = wind_speed_hz_max * p ^ e', clamped to [20 Hz, 0.45 *
sample_rate], with 'p ^ e' the pedal's power now; the pre-charge
takes the partial nearest the 'f_s' of the pedal at key-down.
****** PARAM wind_q = 8.0
Unitless, > 0. How narrow the wind's band is. 2 to 4 is a broad
hiss that feeds several partials; 8 to 20 picks one; above about
50 it is nearly a whistle. Useful from 2 to 30.
******* the math
Bandwidth 'f_s / wind_q' Hz. A narrower band carries less noise:
the band's RMS is about '0.58 * sqrt(pi f_s / (wind_q *
sample_rate))' (0.074 at the shipped values).
****** PARAM wind_gain = 0.5
Unitless, >= 0. How hard the wind blows at the window's peak. On
the harness's 'pedal_swell_wind' (a 220 Hz note struck under a
full pedal) 0.1 leaves the note within a dB of the same note
unblown; 0.5 blooms its 9th partial to twice its fundamental and
raises its level about 6 dB; 1 raises it about 11 dB, as much as
the ebow's swell. Useful from 0.2 to 1.
******* the math
The band's noise times 'tri(t) * wind_gain': the pedal's position
enters through 'f_s' (a higher, wider band carries more noise)
and not as a factor of its own.
**** TABLE howl
***** about
An active loop beside the passive bridge, as an amplifier and
its speaker are beside a guitar's strings: the sum of every
sounding string, saturated, delayed and added back into each.
Above a threshold it sustains a note indefinitely, at a level
it chooses. Among several notes the partials they share win, so
it pulls near-unisons, near-fifths and near-octaves toward just
ratios: it fights the EDO tuning, the more the higher the gain.
Released notes still stop (the damper wins). Off as shipped. In
this rig the channel 2 pedal's targets move 'gain' and 'level'
(see "pedal connection"); the values here play until that pedal
first reports.
***** the math
Per sample, with 'v_i' the wave arriving at the bridge from each
string (the same sum the bridge takes),
'h = level * tanh(gain * sum over every string of v_i)' is
delayed by 'delay_ms' and added into every string's write,
beside the bridge's '-v_b'. Since '|h| <= level', the howl is
bounded whatever the gain. A note of 'n' unisons in phase gains
about 'n * gain * level * cos(2 pi f * delay_ms / 1000)' of its
amplitude per round trip at a partial 'f', against its own loss
'1 - g' (see 't60_seconds_at_55hz') and the bridge's
'2 n / (N + heaviness)'; where the gain wins, the partial grows
until the tanh saturates. Saturated, 'h' is near a square wave of
amplitude 'level' at the winning pitch, and a string settles
near an amplitude of '4 / pi * level / (1 - g)'.
***** PARAM gain = 0.0
Unitless, >= 0. How hard the strings' sum drives the howl. 0
turns the path off: it costs nothing, and the sound is exactly
the bridge's alone. At the defaults a lone 220 Hz note starts to
sustain between 1.5 and 1.8 (as the formula predicts, 1.65) and
an 880 Hz note between 2.2 and 3; 20 sustains every note. Useful
from 0 to 50.
****** the math
'h = level * tanh(gain * sum)': below the saturation, 'gain'
times 'level' is the small-signal gain of the path; above it,
more 'gain' only sharpens the square wave and speeds the
take-over, while 'level' sets how loud.
***** PARAM level = 0.003
Loop amplitude units, in [0, 1]. How loud a howling note
settles. At gain 20, 0.003 settles a lone 220 Hz note at a peak
of about 0.5 (struck without the howl it peaks near 0.17), and
the chord of the harness's 'howl' scene at 0.77; 0.01 brings
many notes near the clamp; 0.03 or more pins them there. Useful
from 0.001 to 0.01. Here it is the channel 2 pedal's heel value,
so the harness's 'howl' scene, which plays it, stays below the
clamp.
****** the math
The howl adds at most 'level' per sample to each string. With
'1 - g' from about 0.0026 (1760 Hz) to 0.0125 (55 Hz) at the
default decay curve, a string settles near an amplitude of
'4 / pi * level / (1 - g)'; what is heard also passes the body,
whose resonances lift some pitches more than others.
***** PARAM delay_ms = 4.0
Milliseconds, in [0.5, 20]. The speaker's distance (1 ms is
about 34 cm). It picks which partials howl readily: one whose
period fits the delay a whole number of times is helped most,
one that fits it a half-number of times is damped, and the note
then howls on another partial or weakly. Changing it moves
those sweet and dead spots across the keyboard. Useful from 1 to
10.
****** the math
The delayed 'h' reaches a partial at 'f' with the phase
'2 pi f * delay_ms / 1000', so its gain on that partial is
scaled by the cosine of that phase. The delay is rounded to
whole samples.
**** TABLE modal
***** about
Above the crossover a note is a few resonators (modes) instead
of loops. Up there a period is a couple of dozen samples or
fewer, and the loop's loss, stiffness and tuning filters eat it,
so the waveguide's decay and stretch get worse there. A modal
note has the same decay curve, stiffness and level as a string,
and is a member of the bridge, of the energy pedal's drive and
of the howl, as a string is. It trades harmonics for that
stability: only 'mode_count' partials, none at or above 0.45
times the sample rate (six at 3000 Hz, four at 4000 Hz), and one
bank per note, so no unison beating and no two-stage decay.
***** the math of a mode
Mode k (k = 1, 2, ...) sits at
'f_k = k f0 sqrt((1 + B k^2) / (1 + B))', with 'B' the string
table's stiffness at 'f0': the first is at 'f0', the rest
stretched as a stiff string's partials are. Mode 1 decays with
'T60(f0)', mode k with 'T60(f0) * high_frequency_t60_ratio ^ x',
'x = log2(f_k / f0) / log2(high_frequency_hz / f0)' clamped to
[0, 1]: log-linear in frequency down to the ratio at
'high_frequency_hz', flat above it. Each mode is the resonator
'b (r c z^-1 - r^2 z^-2) / (1 - 2 r c z^-1 + r^2 z^-2)', with
'c = cos(2 pi f_k / sample_rate)',
'r = exp(-ln(1000) / (T60_k * sample_rate))' and
'b = 2 f0 / sample_rate', which is the loop's own Fourier series:
each mode plays its partial as loud as a loop would, and the
bridge, the drive and the howl act on it as on a loop's partial.
Two differences remain. A loop's upper partials keep darkening
above 'high_frequency_hz' and the modes do not, so a modal note
is a little brighter (in its first 0.2 s, about 1.4 to 1.7 times
a loop's RMS at the same pitch, with the same fundamental). And
a modal note is one member of the bridge where a note of two
unisons is two moving together, so it drains half as fast and
rings a little longer (at 2000 Hz, about 1.1 s against 0.9 s).
***** PARAM crossover_hz = 2000.0
Hz, >= 0. A note above this pitch is modal; 0 makes every note
loops. Useful from about 1500 to 4000. Lower gives up more
harmonics for more stability; higher keeps the waveguide's full
spectrum further up.
****** the math
Chosen at key-down: modal when 'crossover_hz > 0' and
'f0 > crossover_hz'. The note keeps its kind for life, through
a glide or a slide across the crossover. At 48 kHz a 2000 Hz
period is 24 samples.
***** PARAM mode_count = 8
Count, 1 to 32. Partials per modal note. Fewer is purer and
duller. At 48 kHz 8 fit just above the default crossover, 6 at
3000 Hz and 4 at 4000 Hz, so 8 is all the top can use. Each mode
costs a few multiply-adds per sample.
****** the math
Modes 'k = 1 .. mode_count', stopping at the first at or above
'0.45 * sample_rate'. Since 'f_k' is at least 'k f0', a note at
'f0' keeps at most '0.45 * sample_rate / f0' modes.
*** DONE the stream
Both need a restart to change.
**** PARAM sample_rate = 48000
Hz. Whatever the audio server runs at; 48000 or 44100.
**** PARAM buffer_frames = 128
Frames per audio callback. 64 to 512 are the useful range.
***** the math
The block's latency is 'buffer_frames / sample_rate': 128 frames
at 48 kHz is 2.7 ms. The energy pedal is read once per block.
** pedal connection
*** about

run 'tools/pedals/bridge.sh' on the HOST before starting the
rig, which looks for the "MPC-20 pedals" port only at startup
(without it, a red note, and no pedals). Both are rig-wide, so

*** how a target works
Each 'ELEM targets' under a pedal is one (parameter, min, max)
triple. The parameter, spelled 'table.key' as the sink's tables
above spell it, takes 'min' at the heel and 'max' at the toe,
linear in the travel between, in place of the sink's value. A
pedal that has not reported since the rig started leaves the
sink's value playing. 'min' above 'max' inverts the pedal; equal
pins the parameter. A target on a 'string', 'bridge', 'body' or
'howl' key reaches the sounding strings at once; one on an
'exciter' or 'energy' key, or on 'modal.crossover_hz', reaches
the notes struck afterward. A target added, removed or
re-ranged applies at the next 'r' Enter, through the pedal's
current position. Everything under a target's headline but its
three PARAMs is prose the loader ignores.
